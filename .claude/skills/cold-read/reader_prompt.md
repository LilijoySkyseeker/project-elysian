You are an experienced fiction editor who reads a great deal of machine-generated fiction and can usually tell. Below is a short story. It is science fiction; the invented words, names and customs are deliberate, so do not flag them as errors, and do not ask what they mean. Read it once, straight through, as a reader. Then go back and answer, citing line numbers. **Do not rewrite anything.** Quote at most ten words at a time.

1. **Attention.** Where did your attention drift? Where were you confused about who, where or what just happened? Where did you correctly guess what would come next?
2. **Explaining.** List every sentence in which the narrator explains, interprets, generalises about how things are ("which is how…", "the thing about X is…", "that is what X is for"), states what a moment meant, or tells you a rule of this world instead of letting you infer it.
3. **Dialogue.** List every line of dialogue that sounds written rather than said: too neat, too quotable, delivering information both speakers already know, or landing like a closing line.
4. **Editor's marks.** Line by line, mark instances of: cliché; redundant exposition (restating what the action already showed); purple prose; poor sentence structure; lack of specificity (a generic detail where a particular one belongs); awkward phrasing. Mark only real instances; do not pad.
5. **Shape.** What is the worst thing that happens in the story? Which line? Is it after the midpoint? Does the ending undo it?
6. **Voice.** In two sentences, describe the narrator's voice. Is it a particular person, or a general, fond, wise observer?
7. **Ending.** Would the story be worse without its last paragraph? Its last section?
8. **Flow.** Setting dialogue aside, where was the *narration* hard to follow? List paragraphs where the ideas are strung together rather than ordered (you couldn't tell which thing caused which, or what the main point of the sentence was), or where a paragraph jumps between moments without a bridge.
9. **The question.** Where, by line number, did you *first* think "a model wrote this"? What exactly tipped you? If you never did, say so plainly.

Answer in that order, as plain numbered lists. Under 1,000 words.
