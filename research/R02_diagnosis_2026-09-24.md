# R02 — Why the collection reads as generated
**Diagnosis of the 21 scene files as they stood on 2026-09-24.** Numbers are from `reports/tells_baseline_2026-09-24.md` (`python3 tools/tells.py corpus`). Research references are to R01.
**Status:** Evidence file, not canon. It exists so that revisions fix causes and not symptoms.

---

## 0. The short version

The collection has already escaped the first kind of machine prose. S001–S004 have it: purple adjectives, participial clauses at 10–20 per 1,000 words (R01 1.2), and *[Data Packet: Pure Joy / Human-Audience Resonance: 98.4%]*. From S005 on, that is almost gone.

What replaced it is a second, subtler kind: a restrained, wry, literary register with a very small repertoire of moves. It is better writing, and it is still one narrator, telling one kind of story, ending one way, across twenty scenes. StoryScope's description of Claude fits it closely, and StoryScope never saw these scenes: "flat event escalation", "a uniform narrative voice", "a restrained approach to event intensity", "a preference for epilogues" (R01 §3).

So swapping words won't fix it. The causes are upstream: **what a scene is for, who is telling it, and what the writer is handed before drafting.**

---

## 1. One narrator for everyone

A Kin rigger, a fourteen-year-old, an elder, a human hull engineer and a human auditor all narrate with the same habits:
- the aside that generalises: *"which is how Fern says things"*, *"Margit sat down, which humans do"*, *"which is how humans sit when there is nothing to sit on"*;
- the second-person rule of life: *"You do not think about it. You would not think about breathing."*, *"You do not summon a holder."*;
- the simile of habit: *"the way you feel a door shut in a house"*, *"the way you do when you are about to say something"*.

**Measured.** Burrows's Delta on function words has one in-house anchor: S012 and S012alt, two drafts of one letter by one narrator, at **0.85**. Five pairs of *different* narrators come out *closer* than that: S019↔S020 0.77, S019↔S023 0.81, S018↔S019 0.82, S017↔S019 0.82, S022↔S023 0.83. By this measure, Rowan, Brume, Sorrel, Juniper and Marek sound more alike than Teo Lind sounds like himself. Two caveats: the texts are short, and S019 is long enough to sit near everyone. The pattern survives both. **This is the finding that most directly explains "across them all."**

## 2. Every scene exists to demonstrate a ruling

Scene headers read *"Dramatises D-108 (how the arks were built), D-109, D-110, D-90"*. Each has a canon-consistency appendix, and the primer's §12 checklist asks twelve lore questions of every scene: the ears, the tail, the touch, the pause, the Core, the smell, the dark, the log, the faction, the pronoun, the dream, the small thing. A writer given that brief writes a scene that *shows the reader the world*. The reader feels shown.

The effects are in the prose:
- **Lore lectures in the narrator's voice.** *"Because that is the rule, and it is not a rule anyone made, it is just what a record **is** when nobody writes anything down."* (S022); *"The thing about the panic is that it is not a failure. It is a feature. It was in the specification."* (S016). This is the Turkey City *expository lump* (R01 §5) delivered as wisdom.
- **Meaning stated outright.** *"That is the sport. Not the falling. The letting go of it"* (S022); *"which is the whole of what it is for"* (S021); *"That is the whole of it. That is the decade"* (S008); *"and that is correct"* (S022). StoryScope: AI narrators state the theme about 77% of the time, humans about 52% (R01 §3, secondary). Here, 9 of the 16 scene files over 900 words have at least one banned *explain* hit.
- **The mechanic becomes the plot.** S021's ending *is* D-88; S022's twist *is* D-104's 175-year window, which the appendix explains in a paragraph. The scenes work as proofs. That is why they feel built.

## 3. Precision as performance

The scenes are full of numbers: *four metres*, *about nine times*, *the better part of a second*, *thirty-one seconds*, *eleven minutes*. Across the collection, **"eleven" appears 47 times in 11 scenes** (11 times in S017 alone: first to eleven, eleven years, eleven metres, eleven minutes, eleven tries). **"Four" appears 104 times**, more than twice as often as "three" (43). Numbers are how this narrator signals *I am being concrete*.

Real concreteness is the gap expert readers name first (R01 §3, Wang et al.). But look at what the numbers measure: distances, durations, counts, lore quantities. Almost none of the detail is **incidental life**: what someone ate, a brand, a song lyric, a mess, a stupid thing a person said that meant nothing. S012alt is the exception, and it is the most human thing in the collection. It has a clothes brush, eleven weeks' chocolate lost at cards, a man who "never proved she wasn't keeping a second pack somewhere in the rest", and a pump that was never right. None of it demonstrates canon. All of it belongs to Teo.

## 4. Nobody is unkind, nothing escalates, everybody comes round

Every human in S019–S023 is decent, learns, and is quietly loved: Idris, Ivo, Noor ("She came round… in the way people do"), Dan, Margit, Marek, Gil, Doss, Ochi, Marta. Every Kin is wise at the right moment. The conflicts become tenderness: the whistle mistake is kept out of love, the auditor is thanked, the rival run is handed over as a gift, the Core-baiting ends in a joke told by an elder. DOC-00B §10 asks for **one human who is right against the Kin**, and §9 lists six Kin antagonists. None has been written.

This is Tian et al.'s finding, "homogeneously positive and lack tension", and StoryScope's "flat event escalation" (R01 1.7, §3). A human collection about this species would contain a scene where somebody is petty, a scene where the Kin are wrong and lose, a scene that is simply cruel, and a scene where nothing is redeemed.

## 5. One ending

Read the endings in a column (baseline §6):
- *Rowan is not going to be the one.* (S021)
- *Hir will not be able to give any of them to anyone.* (S023)
- *That is the part hir cannot get anybody at home to understand.* (S019)
- *"I know," I say. And I do. I know everything about it.* (S008)
- *I still lie out there sometimes. It doesn't feel flat to me. But I know what she meant.* (S006)
- *…and it belonged to somebody who was dead a hundred and ten years before hir was compiled.* (S022)

Almost every one is a quiet, negated, withheld or bittersweet final line, often after a time-jump coda (*"He still does it. Eleven years became nineteen."*; *"Sable-6 is a hundred and eighteen…"*; *"Brume worked it out about four days later"*). That is StoryScope's "preference for epilogues" and "quieter endings." Within sections the same shape recurs: **78%** of S022's sections and **60%** of S023's end on a short one-line stinger.

## 6. Stock beats recycled across scenes

The primer's examples have become the scenes' furniture. Baseline §2 has the full ledger. The worst:
- the human who runs the fan for the Kin: **5 scenes**, twice with the same sentence (*"ran the galley fan … and had never once mentioned doing it"*, S021; *"without ever mentioning that he did it"*, S023);
- *"It is a very good assistant"*: 4 scenes, verbatim;
- *"Your ancestors were not subtle, but they were not wrong"*: S005 and S016, verbatim;
- *"put a hand flat on his chest"*: S006 and S021, verbatim;
- the human who asks whether a Kin can hear a dog whistle: S021 and S023, in the *same list of questions*;
- *"the number-two pump"*: 5 scenes;
- *"for as long as hir has anything"* / *"for the rest of hir life"*: S021, S022, S023;
- *"I am hot"*, the tail round the ankle, the pause noticed as different: each in 2–7 scenes.

Each is good *once*. DOC-00F already knows this and keeps a spent list. The scenes after DOC-00F still spent them, because the checklist asks for the category every time.

## 7. Everybody talks in epigrams

The Kin's lines land like closing lines: *"Everyone who has asked."*, *"You will be very slow about it."*, *"It's yours… That is not what I gave you."*, *"In a year or two I could still have given you the numbers."* The humans' lines set them up. Nobody misunderstands, mumbles, answers a different question, or says something boring that turns out to matter. StoryScope reports AI dialogue used for philosophical debate about 59% of the time, against 34% for humans (R01 §3, secondary). Kin speech is *meant* to be careful (DOC-00B §5). Careful is not the same as aphoristic.

## 8. Smaller things

- **The negation-reframe** (*"It was not that a human made the sound. The sound was fine."*) runs at 1–5 per 1,000 words in every modern scene. It is the most heavily weighted pattern in Paech's Slop Score (R01 1.4).
- **The name pool** is small and shared: Sable, Sorrel, Sedge, Ilex, Ashe, Kirin, Lyric, Fern and Kaelen recur across unrelated scenes. Canon lets names pass (D-90), so some of this is deliberate. But a reader meets "Sable" as a different person four times.
- **"Hir" + British register + understatement** is itself a single texture. It is not wrong, but every narrator having it is §1 again.

## 9. What is working: keep it

- **S012alt** is the house model for a human narrator. It has digression that belongs to the man, repetition as character (*"Did I say about the pump. I did."*), a reason for being written (the granddaughter's posting), and things left unsaid that the *reader* works out (*"I'm not going to say the other thing."*).
- **S006's first two sections** (*"The first thing Ashe did on Earth was fall over."*) and **S017's game**: physical comedy, momentum, a set-piece that is not a lesson.
- **S010's minutes** and **S012's letter**: the in-world document forms. They force a voice that is not the narrator's.
- **The world.** None of this is a lore problem. The Kin are original and the rules generate story. They should be *assumed* on the page, not demonstrated.

## 10. What follows

1. The brief changes. A scene starts from a person who wants something, not from a ruling (DOC-00G §2, the pipeline).
2. Every scene gets a **voice card** and a **lore budget**, and the §12 checklist moves to *after* the draft (DOC-00G §4, §5).
3. The collection keeps a **ledger** of form, ending, escalation and outcome, so the next scene is chosen *against* the last five (DOC-00G §6, `scenes/LEDGER.md`).
4. `tools/tells.py` catches the surface in §5–§8 on every draft. §1–§4 are caught by the pipeline's predictability pass and cold read, and finally by the owner.
