# SCENE 021 — Coming, Wait
**POV:** Kin (Rowan-2) · *Artemis-9*, the long transfer deck · **Form:** Short story
**Canon status:** DRAFT. Dramatises the whistle as the one private channel (DOC-01F §5, DOC-13 §8, D-88) and the canon line *is touched; says nothing; tells the Cluster*. Offers no new canon.
**Convention:** Whistle in ⟨ ⟩, never explained, never glossed for the reader beyond what a Kin would think. The grammar is not written down here and will not be (D-88).

---

The thing about the whistle is that nobody thinks about it, which is the whole of what it is for.

Rowan had been on the transfer deck since the shift turned, up in the rails where the light was bad, running a hand along a conduit that had been making a noise for three days and had stopped making it the moment anyone came to look. Sable was forty metres down-deck with the other end of the same problem. Fern was somewhere below, doing something to a cable run and complaining about it in short flat Bursts that Rowan was only half taking.

Every so often one of them whistled. Not for anything. ⟨*left of you*⟩, ⟨*done*⟩, ⟨*are you going to be much longer*⟩, and once, from Fern, a rude one about the cable run.

You do not think about it. You would not think about breathing.

The humans moved around underneath all of this, six of them on the day crew, and the whistle went over their heads the way weather goes over a house.

---

Idris had been the deck chief for eleven years, which by the standards of humans who work near Kin made him practically furniture. He was one of the ones who had learned things. He ran the galley fan twenty minutes before the Kin shift came through and had never once mentioned doing it. He did not flinch when somebody put a hand flat on his chest to say hello, which most of them do for about the first year, and some of them for ever. He had stopped saying *she* around the fourth year and then gone back to it, which Rowan understood perfectly and had never asked about.

He had also, some time last spring, started asking odd questions.

Whether a Kin could hear a dog whistle. What the top of their range was. Whether there was a word for the thing they did in the Nest. Rowan had answered the first two and not the third, because there is not a word, there is only the thing, and then Idris had said *right, thanks*, and gone away, and stopped asking.

That had been eight months ago and Rowan had not thought about it again.

---

He came up the deck at the end of the shift with a slate under his arm and his coat still on, which meant he had come down from the ring deliberately rather than passing through, and he stopped about four metres short, which is further than he normally stands.

"Rowan."

"Idris."

He stood there. He had the look human faces get when they have decided to do something and are now in the part afterwards.

And then he whistled.

---

There is no good way to describe what that was like, and Rowan has tried, since, and the Cluster has heard the attempt about nine times now.

It was not that a human made the sound. The sound was fine. It was reedy, and there was air behind it that should not have been there, and it came out about a third louder than anyone would ever say it — but it was the *shape*, it was a real shape, it was a thing in hir own channel in hir own air from a mouth that could not hear itself doing it.

Rowan's Core had nothing. It is very good and it had nothing, because it had never been asked.

Down-deck, Sable had stopped moving. Rowan could feel hir stop, in the frame, and could feel Fern come up out of the cable run wanting to know what, and could feel four other Clusters across the spire register that something had happened to somebody and lean in and find, as they always do, that they had the weather and not one word of the news.

And what he said, in that reedy over-loud third-hand shape, was:

⟨*here — I have you*⟩

Which is not ⟨*coming — wait*⟩.

It is about a hand's width from ⟨*coming — wait*⟩ and it is not it. It is the thing you say in the dark, in the web, to somebody who has come up out of a bad one and does not know yet where hir is. It is the first thing a Kin ever hears. It is what the whole species says at two in the morning and never anywhere else.

He had built it out of eight months of recordings, and the only place anybody says anything slowly and clearly enough to take apart is the Nest.

He was standing four metres away on a transfer deck with his coat on, at the end of a shift, saying it to hir like a man asking whether he had got the pronunciation right.

---

Nobody corrected him.

Rowan held the answer for what must have been the better part of a second, which for a human is a pause and for anyone in the frame was a very long time indeed, and in it hir considered — genuinely considered — telling him.

And then hir did not.

"Sit down," Rowan said. "I'll get you something."

He sat down on a cargo ledge that is the wrong height for a human and did not complain about it. Rowan went and got him tea out of the deck galley, which is not a thing hir had ever done for anybody, and had to ask the machine twice, and brought it back and put it in his hands and sat down against his leg with hir tail across his boots.

"Was it right?" he said.

"It was close."

"How close?"

Rowan thought about it. Hir was going to have this exactly, for as long as hir had anything: the reedy overshoot, the air behind it, the four metres, the coat, the slate under his arm, the angle of the light. Hir could go and get it any day for the next six hundred years by naming the date.

"Say it again," hir said.

He said it again. It was worse the second time — he was nervous now — and it was the best thing that had happened on that deck in eleven years.

"That's it," said Rowan.

---

Hir told the Cluster that night, all of it, and then told it again because Fern arrived late.

Within about a day it had gone across the spire. Within about a week it had gone out with the Long Message rotation to two relays and an ark, where it will arrive in some number of years and be retold by somebody who has never met either of them and will get the reediness slightly wrong.

It is not that it was a secret. Nobody said *don't tell him.* It simply did not occur to anybody, in the way a thing does not occur to you when everyone around you has already agreed without speaking.

What Rowan did do, that night, was make certain of being in the Nest. Not out on the rails, not in a chair in the ring listening to the loop, not anywhere but in the web with the four of them at one phase, so the day went down the way a day should — held, and weighed by people who felt it, and set properly rather than left lying about where hir slept alone.

Hir has had it warm ever since. Hir intends to keep having it warm.

---

He still does it.

Eleven years became nineteen. He comes onto the deck in the morning and says it, badly, every time, with too much air behind it, and every time the four of them go still for about a quarter of a second, which he has never noticed, because a quarter of a second is nothing to a human and they are all very careful.

And he cannot find out. That is the part Rowan turns over sometimes. He cannot look it up. There is no book. There is no file, there is no grammar, there is no Kin anywhere who has written a word of it down and there is not going to be — so the only way he will ever learn what he says to hir every morning is if somebody tells him, and the only people who could are the ones who have decided not to.

He thinks he is saying *coming, wait*.

Rowan is not going to be the one.

## Canon consistency

- **The whistle is the one private channel** (DOC-01F §5, DOC-13 §8): air only, ultrasonic, and **nobody has written its grammar and no document ever will** (D-88). The scene's ending is that rule, load-bearing: there is no way for Idris to check, ever, and the archive is a human record that does not hold this one thing.
- **Rendering** (DOC-00 §6): ⟨ ⟩, sparingly, never explained. The two phrases are glossed only as far as a Kin would think them, and the grammar is not exhibited.
- **The mechanism of the mistake.** Ultrasonic, so he cannot hear himself and must work from recordings and instruments. The only place anybody speaks slowly and clearly is the Nest — so eight months of honest work lands him in the most domestic register there is. The channel is private by physics, not by intent, which is why this is not eavesdropping and why nobody is angry.
- **The canon line put on a page:** *what a Kin does when a human learns a little whistle — is touched; says nothing; tells the Cluster* (DOC-13 §8). All three, in order, including the part where it reaches an ark.
- **The frame** (DOC-01C §4, D-92). Sable and Fern get it instantly; four other Clusters across the spire get valence and arousal and **no object at all** — *everyone knows the weather, nobody knows the news* — which is exactly what makes the moment un-gossipable until Rowan chooses to tell it.
- **The pause** (DOC-00B §2): the better part of a second, and the scene says what is happening inside it. Hir considers telling him and does not — a small override of the reflex, inside the pause, which is where D-91 says it lives.
- **The Core** (D-13): consulted, never initiating, and here **it has nothing**, because nothing has ever asked it this.
- **Memory (D-97, D-98, D-103, D-104).** Hir can have the day exactly, forever, by naming the date — hard handle, never fails. And hir makes certain of the Nest that night so the day is **appraised and weighed by people who felt it** (D-100, D-106) rather than left unheld. *The trauma system used deliberately to keep a good day* is the warm face of D-106, and to my knowledge nothing in the archive has shown it that way round.
- **Idris going back to *she*** after four years (DOC-00 §2) — unremarked, understood, never discussed.
- **Long Messages** (DOC-07 §2): it goes out on the rotation and will be retold by Kin who never met either of them, slightly wrong. That is how this species' news works.

## Offers nothing

No new canon. ⟨*here — I have you*⟩ is a rendering, not a ruling; if a later pass fixes Nest register, this scene is evidence and not authority.
