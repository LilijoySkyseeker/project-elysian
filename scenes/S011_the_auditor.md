# SCENE 011 — The Auditor
**Origin:** Fresh-context agent, 2026-09-19 · **Form:** Short story · **POV:** Human (Ruth Okonkwo, operator safety reviewer)
**Style:** DOC-00 governs names and pronouns.  
**Canon status:** DRAFT — awaiting review. Introduces no canon. Introduces (not canon): *Cold Harbour* (an operator tug depot, Belt side); Rowan-3, Cirrus, Halo, Sedge-5 (Kin, the pump-deck Cluster); Ruth Okonkwo (human, safety reviewer); Dev (human, galley); an unnamed human deck chief.

---

I have done twenty-two of these. You arrive, you are polite, you take the logs, you find the nine hours nobody wrote down, and you write a report that says what happened in them. Somebody signs it. It goes in a folder that nobody opens unless somebody dies, and then it is the most important document in the world for about a week.

*Cold Harbour* was my first with Kin. It is a tug depot on the far side of the Belt, a Kin posting the way most depots that far out are, which is to say nobody human wanted it and four humans have it anyway.

The incident was not much. The number-two coolant loop on the pump deck had lost a pump — a bearing, not sudden — and for nine hours the human quarter had been cold and the docking spine had been shut, and then it hadn't. Nobody hurt. Nothing lost but a tug schedule. The reason I had been sent eleven days out to a depot the size of a car park was that the station's engineering log for those nine hours read, in full: *Loop 2 pump fault. Rerouted. Spine closed 0340–1250.* Signed by the deck chief, who is human, and who had been asleep for six of the nine hours, and who told me this within a minute of meeting me as if he'd been waiting to.

"The Kin did it," he said. "Ask them. They'll tell you everything." He said *everything* in a particular way I didn't understand yet.

---

The pump deck was dark. Not dim — dark, the way the inside of a cupboard is, and I stood in the hatch with my slate held out in front of me like a lantern until something touched the wall and one lamp came on, low and yellow, in a corner, and I understood that it had been switched on for me.

There were five of them. I'd been briefed — the shape, the size, the fur, the six limbs; the briefing had a diagram. The briefing did not have the way they were *arranged*: folded on ledges and rails in the dark at every height, tails hanging, all of them turned toward me, none of them looking at me, in a silence that was obviously not silence. The nearest was on the console ledge, sitting the way the briefing said they sit, upright from the waist with the rest of her folded under as if the furniture were a rumour.

"Ruth," she said. First name. They all do that; the deck chief had warned me. "I am Rowan. This is the Cluster that had the loop." A pause before it, half a second, which I took for reluctance and which was not.

I said what I say. I said I wasn't there to find fault, that the report was for the operator, that what I needed was the Cluster's records of the incident — decisions, timings, who did what — and that if they could send them to my slate I'd get out of their way.

The pause was longer.

"We have it," Rowan said. "All of it. We cannot send it."

"A copy is fine."

"There is no copy. There is us." She said it gently, as though the fact might hurt me. "You may have it. Sit with us. Halo has the most of it; Halo was on the loop when it went. Half an hour, chest to chest, and you would have the whole of that day." Her ears — enormous, and I'd been told not to read them — came forward, slow, both together, and stayed. "I know you cannot receive it. I am offering it anyway, because I would be ashamed to offer you less than what there is."

I want to be honest about what I felt, which was that I was being managed. I had a form. Item four on the form said *Cluster incident records obtained: Y/N*, and I had never in twenty-two reviews written N.

"Or," she said, "we can Tell it to you in words. It is slower. It would take —" the ears again "— four days. Five, with the pump before it failed, which you will want."

"I don't want five days. I want a file."

"A file of what?"

"Of what happened."

"Yes," said Rowan. "That is what I am offering."

---

We went round it for an hour. I have gone round things with union stewards and consortium lawyers and a man on Luna who had set a policy on fire, and I have never had a conversation so civil and so completely without traction. She was not obstructing. She was not proud. She simply could not see what a nine-line summary would be *for*, and I could not see what a five-day retelling would be for, and each of us kept explaining our side as though the other had merely not heard it.

"Someone who was not there," I said, "needs to be able to know what happened."

"Someone who was not there," she said, "should ask someone who was."

"And when everyone who was there is dead?"

The pause, that time, was a different kind. I only knew it was different.

"Then someone will have carried it," she said.

"That isn't a record. That's a rumour with good intentions."

She considered this. She did not take it badly. "And yours is being told a friend's height," she said. "It is true. It is not him."

Then, because I think she saw that I was tired: "Ruth. The first of us were written by hand. We know what writing is for. It is not that we think it is beneath us."

---

I retreated to the galley to think, which is where I go to think, and they came and found me there, which I gather is what they do.

Two of them: Rowan, and a younger one called Cirrus who was introduced as *having a piece of the second hour*. Cirrus stopped in the galley doorway with her nose working and did not come in, and Dev, who runs the galley, reached over without a word and switched on the extractor fan and went back to his soup, and after a minute Cirrus came through as though a door had opened.

I pulled a chair out for her. I don't know what I was thinking. She looked at it, and looked at me, and with great courtesy folded herself onto the table beside my slate instead, all six limbs under her, warm against my forearm through the sleeve. Rowan took the bench and sat against my hip as if the bench had been built for two and I was in her half of it. Something wound itself round the leg of my chair. When I got up to refill my coffee the chair came a little way with me. Nobody remarked on it, and I have decided not to either.

"One day," I said. "Not five. The day of the incident, in words, and I take notes. That goes in the report as the Cluster's account."

The pause. "One day," Rowan agreed. "You will be tired."

I was. It took four hours. They took turns — Halo came, and one called Sedge who had been on *Cold Harbour* longer than the deck chief — and what they gave me was the strangest testimony I have ever taken down. Every number was exact: pressures, times to the second, the bearing temperature on the hour. And around every number, stubbornly, the rest of it. *The pump had been sounding wrong for nine days; we had talked about it and decided it would last until the tug came.* *At 0340 Halo said it would not last. We discussed whether to wake the deck chief. He had been tired that week; we let him sleep.* *We closed the spine because it was the loop we understood least, and we were not proud of that.* I kept trying to cut the fact out of the story and they kept, patiently, putting the story back, the way you'd hand someone the wrapping they'd dropped.

Somewhere in the third hour I understood something I have not yet found a place for on any form: that I had reviewed twenty-two incidents and knew, in twenty-two folders, what had been written, and that this was the first time I had been told what had happened.

I don't say that to be converted. I still needed the file. Somebody who was not there will need to open it, and they will not have four hours, and they will not have Halo. I wrote it that night: three pages, correct in every particular, and nothing like the day.

---

The form needs an attestation. One line, from the crew whose account it is: *I confirm the above is accurate.* I have never had trouble getting it.

I handed Rowan the slate. She held it in both hands and looked at it for a long time — I could not read the look; *envy* is the word I'd have reached for if I hadn't spent the afternoon being taught that I didn't know what things were — and then she wrote, slowly, one finger at a time, the way she talked.

*The pump failed when she says. We rerouted. Nobody was hurt. Ruth listened for four hours and was tired and did not say so.*

Head office will strike the last sentence. It is not a record of anything.

I left it in.
