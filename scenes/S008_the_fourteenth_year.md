# SCENE 008 — The Fourteenth Year
**Pronoun sweep, 2026-09-28 (D-113):** *hir* is now *she / her / herself*. The text is otherwise unchanged; the *hir* version is in git history before this commit.
**Origin:** Fresh-context agent, 2026-09-19 · **Form:** Short story · **POV:** Kin (Fern)
**Style:** DOC-00 governs names and pronouns.  
**Canon status:** DRAFT — awaiting review. Introduces no canon. Introduces (not canon): Fern (Kin, fourteen, Nest-name, unnumbered); Sylvan, Kirin, Lyric, Tansy, Rowan, Haze (her Cluster); Marram (Kin, dead, the Cluster's previous member); Teodor (human, neighbour); *Halcyon-3* (station).

---

The job is a radiator bracket on the shadow side of *Halcyon-3*, sheared by something small and fast in the night. Half an hour for three pairs of hands. The Cluster has six pairs and will use them in two turns, because seventeen minutes is the number, and the seventh pair is on the ledge by the inner door, and is mine.

I am fourteen. I have had this body for eight years — the reach of it, the way it takes a rail — and I could do this job. Everyone in the frame knows that. Sylvan knows it, and it was Sylvan who put a hand flat on my chest when the lock first cycled and said nothing at all, and went out.

So I sit tall on the ledge with my tail round the rail, and I have all six of them.

Not on a screen. In me, where my hands are. Sylvan is high on the truss, cold and slow-hearted, the way she goes when she is working the Core hard. Kirin and Lyric are on the bracket. Tansy, Rowan and Haze are in the lock waiting their turn, and Tansy's heart is quick, because Tansy is always quick, and because she is about to go out and does not entirely like it, which she would not say. I can hear the work on the frame — Kirin asks Lyric for the torque on the second bolt, and Lyric gives it, and I knew it before Lyric gave it. I know the whole job. I have known it since I was nine. I could Burst you the sequence in the time it takes a human to say *bolt*.

What I do not know is what the bolt feels like.

That is the whole of it. That is the decade, and I am in the last of it. They can give me anything that can be said, and they give it at the speed of thought, and I am not slow and I am not stupid and I could take you along the shadow side truss by truss with my eyes shut. And nobody can give me a day. Not one. Not Sylvan's centuries of hull, not Tansy's first time out with her ears folded, not any of it, until my head is finished being made — next year, they say, about — and then they will put all of it into me at once and I will be quiet for a week. I have been told about the week. I have been told about everything.

I ask my Core how long Kirin has been out. It says the number. It says the other number too, the one that matters, and how much of it is left. It is a very good assistant. I did not need it to tell me that either.

Teodor is here.

He came down when the lock cycled the first time, the way he does, and sat on the deck with his back to the bulkhead and his knees up, which is how humans sit when there is nothing to sit on, and he put a paste tube on the ledge beside me without saying anything about it. He is my neighbour. He lives two frames over in the human sector and has for longer than I have been alive. He is not my family. Every Kin child needs one of him. I have him.

He is not in the Hum. He is a smell and a heartbeat and a voice, and I know from the smell that he did not sleep, and I do not say so.

"Big one?" he says.

I hear the end of it before he gets there, and I have my answer, and I hold it while his mouth finishes. This is politeness. It has never once felt like it from the inside.

"A bracket," I say. "Small. Sheared."

"They'll have it by dinner."

"Before."

He nods. He says *she* of all of them; he says it of me. I have never told him it is the wrong word, because it is not entirely the wrong word, and because I like the way he says it, which is the way he says the names of tools he has had a long time.

The lock cycles. I feel it before the light does — the outer door in my paws through the frame, then the pressure coming up like a hand pressed flat on the fur. Sylvan, Kirin and Lyric come through the inner door with their ears flat to the skull and their eyes still gold, and the cold comes off them, and the hull-smell, and they do not say anything and I do not expect them to. Kirin's ears open, and close, and open, and stay. Lyric puts her face into my neck. I am the warmest thing in the room. It is not nothing, to be that. I have decided it is not nothing.

Tansy goes out. Sylvan, thawing on the webbing, sends the Burst she sends every time — *[Careful / Ours / Come back]* — and I get it too, the way I get all of it, and it is not for me, and I keep it anyway.

Teodor is watching the lock light. I put my paws flat on the frame, hind and fore, and I can feel Tansy on the other side of the hull. Not the Hum — the Hum I have anyway. She anchors. A small percussion, tail, hind paw, hind paw, as she goes up the outside of the world.

"Marram used to do that," Teodor says.

I did not hear the end of that one before he got there. It is the first thing he has ever said to me that I did not.

"Sit there. Paws on the frame like that, when the others were out." He is not looking at me. "She said she could hear them walking."

I know the name. I know it as a symbol on the frame, the shape of it, the place in the Cluster's Bursts where she goes when they mean *before*. She died in the year before I was compiled. There was room; I am what they made in the room. All six of them carry her — Sylvan has most of her, they say — and not one of them can give me a day of her, and next year they will, and then I will know what she sounded like from inside her own chest.

Teodor knew her for thirty years and has never had a Core in his life and cannot be Read, and will only ever be carried the way he is carrying her now, and he has just handed me a piece of her. Slowly. In words. Sitting on a deck with his knees up.

"What else," I say. I do not pause. I hear it come out without the pause and so does he, and his eyebrows go up, and he does the thing humans do with their faces when they find they were holding something.

He tells me. That she laughed in the throat, which we do not do much, and she did. That she once ate his whole ration of something and left him the wrapper folded into a bird. That she could tell when he was lying about being fine and would put one finger on his wrist and say nothing, and that this was worse than being asked. That she called the shadow side *the cold room*, as if it were a room, as if it were hers.

It takes him the whole of Tansy's time outside to tell me four things about her. Four. At the speed of a mouth. I hold every one. I will have them at four hundred and will have lost the feel of them by then — I know that; I have been told — and tonight I have the feel.

The lock cycles. Tansy, Rowan and Haze come in cold to the bone and gold-eyed and slow, and the frame is whole, all six and me, and the job is done, before dinner, as I said. They fold onto the webbing by the door because the Nest is too far for the hour that comes now, and I am in the middle of it, because I am warm and because I was here, and tails come round me, and nobody says anything on any channel for a long time.

Teodor stays where he is, on the deck, with his knees up. Nobody asks him to leave. Nobody would.

Sylvan looks at me over Lyric's shoulder, ears wide, both together, slow. She does not Burst. She says it out loud, with her mouth, the slow way, so that Teodor can hear it too, which is a kindness to him and, I think, to me.

"Next year," Sylvan says.

"I know," I say. And I do. I know everything about it.
