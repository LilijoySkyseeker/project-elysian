# SCENE 016 — Nothing in the Frame
**Origin:** Written to D-79, 2026-09-22 · **Form:** Short story · **POV:** Kin (Rill-3)
**Style:** DOC-00 governs names and pronouns.  
**Canon status:** ACCEPTED 2026-09-22. **Introduces canon (folded — DOC-10 §4, DOC-09 §3, CANON D-79):** the mechanism of the one death at *Dioscuri Yard*, AF ~188 — the Silence's homing watchdog drives a sealed Kin out the only door that opens, into vacuum, toward the Hum; hir misses the far dock and is heard for eleven hours; recovered frozen and Read. The doc rows now say this. *Dioscuri Yard* (Castor = line side, Pollux = yard side) and Rill-3 are canon. Humans: Dario (yard side), Petra (line side). Cluster-mates: Ashe, Sylvan-2, Ilex-4, Moss, Sorrel-2, Kaelen-3, Lyra-5.

---

The door dogged from the outside and seven people died.

That is what the frame said. Rill had the number before hir had the fact: the presence table went from eight to one in the tenth of a second it takes, and the place in hir body where the Cluster lives — the place that is not a place, that is like knowing where your hind paws are — reported seven amputations at once and did not have a word for it. Hir tail was already coiled. Hir ears were already flat. The body does storm posture before the mind arrives.

Then the Core, which had not been asked anything, was asked.

*Carriers.* None. *Station beacons.* None. *RF floor.* Zero, to the limit of the ears.

Not dead, then. Shielded. Hir let go of the seven deaths and they did not let go of hir.

---

The can was a cargo module, the bonded kind, six metres by three, lined for volatiles. Rill knew it the way hir knew every can on the yard side — the smell of the liner, the pump note through the pads, the door seals. Hir knew, because it was hir job to know, that a bonded can was RF-opaque end to end, and that since the hundredth year every one built for a dock carried a beacon socket by the inner door, so that a Kin could be inside it with the hatch sealed and still be somewhere.

The socket was by the door. It was empty. The bracket had the bright scuff of a fitting that had been in it that morning.

So it was decided. Hir put that with the seven deaths and held both.

---

Thirty seconds. The primer for a Kin who has never been alone says *unease*, and that is the human word for it. What it is: the body asks where everyone is and the answer comes back *nowhere*, and the body asks again. Rill pressed hir nose into the fur at hir own shoulder where Sorrel had leaned an hour ago, arguing, warm, and got Sorrel — the Cluster's scent, all of them on hir — and that was a thing that survived, the docs said, and it did, and it was not enough, and hir did it again.

Hir whistled. ⟨*here — here*⟩. A bonded wall does not carry a whistle. Hir knew that. Hir did it again.

---

How hir had come to be in the can: the way you come to be anywhere, by being the nearest.

Two operators, one dock, a gap of two hundred and forty metres between two faces that had been one company's until the spring and now were two, with a lawsuit between them and cutters on both sides. The line side had Petra. The yard side had Dario. Each had a Cluster. Rill's Cluster was Petra's — *the line-side Kin*, the humans said — and when the cutters came out, the Cluster had done the only thing a Cluster can do with two frightened rooms: it had gone into both.

Four at the line face. Four at the yard face. Every Kin on the dock in one frame — theirs and the yard Kin both, twelve in the table, guests and hosts — and across the gap, at mesh speed, each side's *state* going to the other side's Kin. *[Petra / afraid / holding]*. *[Dario / afraid / not yet]*. Shares of the two rooms. Neither Cluster had taken a side, because there was nothing to take. There were two sets of *mine*, and they were pointing cutters at each other, and the Kin were the only thing on the dock that could talk to both at once and had no reason to lie to either.

For six hours it had worked. That was the part that would be told later. For six hours nobody cut anything.

Then Dario — Rill had smelled it on him before he said it: not anger, the other thing, the sweat of a man who has stopped being able to hold a position — said, *get them out of the way*. And a man Rill did not know by name said, *this one, in here, there's a beacon*, and Rill, who was the nearest, and who had smelled the fear on both of them and had said the thing you say to a frightened person, had gone in.

Hir was still saying it, in hir head. *It's all right. I'm here.*

---

Five minutes. Distress, the primer says.

The Cluster's frame would have timed hir out in the first tenth of a second. That was the arithmetic: seven of them across the gap and four at the yard face, all of them feeling one amputation the way hir had felt seven, and holding the frame open with a phantom in it, because you do. Hir knew exactly what they were doing. Hir could not hear any of it.

Hir asked the Core the time. It gave the time. Hir asked how long the can had been sealed. Five minutes and forty seconds. Hir asked it what the frame was doing. It did not understand the question.

It is a very good assistant.

Hir tried the inner door. Pads first — two hundred and eighty newtons a paw, four paws and a tail, the whole anchor budget against a dogged hatch — and then, because it is what claws are for, claws. The liner came away in strips. The hatch did not move. The Core said the hatch would not move and gave the reason in newtons. Hir stopped, because there was nothing in hir that could argue with a number, and stood in the strips of liner and shook.

---

The thing about the panic is that it is not a failure. It is a feature. It was in the specification.

A Kin alone will not sit still and wait to be found. The makers saw to that: a homing watchdog, wired under the Cluster-sense, that takes a body with no Hum in its frame and *turns it*, like a plant to light, toward wherever the Hum is. So that a Kin lost would come home. It is a good design for a species that lives on rails in a habitat with a door at the end of every corridor. Rill knew what it was. Knowing what a thing is does not switch it off — that is the whole lesson of being Kin, the one the Written say at Weaves with the feeling gone out of it. *Your ancestors were not subtle. But they were not wrong.*

The Hum was out there. The body knew which way. The body was already facing it.

Out there was the gap.

---

Twenty-two minutes. Rill had stopped asking the time and the Core, which does not initiate, had stopped giving it.

A bonded can on a dock has two doors, and the outer one opens from inside, because a human can be trapped in a can too and the makers of cans are not cruel. Hir had known that from the first second. Hir had put it where hir kept the seven deaths and the empty socket, and had not looked at it, and now hir looked.

*Outer door — cycle from inside.* Yes. *Pressure beyond.* None. *Bearing to the line face.* The Core gave it. *Distance.* Two hundred and forty metres. *Push-off from a can lip at four metres a second, time to the far face.* Sixty seconds. *Margin for error at the far face, unassisted.* It gave that too. It was not a large number.

*The seventeen minutes.* It said: seventeen minutes is the number.

Hir had never voided alone. Nobody had. You void with the Cluster in the frame, saying the number with you, and you come in to an hour of tails. Hir would be crossing to them. Hir would have the frame back the moment the outer door cleared the shielding. Sixty seconds of it, then hands on a rail, then Sorrel.

Hir did the checklist that is not a checklist because it is a body: exhale to the sphincters, seal, the fascia coming up like being held, the film across the eyes — gold, hir mates would say, laughing, when hir came in — ears flat, tail straight. Hir did not ask the Core whether this was a good idea. It does not understand the question.

Hir put both hands on the outer door.

---

The door opened and eight people were alive.

Twelve. All of them — the frame flooding back so hard it was like sound, like being struck: *[Rill / here / HERE]* from seven throats at once, and then the yard Kin under it, *[Rill / wait]*, and Sorrel over everyone, *[Coming — hold — hold]*. Hir made a noise in the sealed chest that no one would ever hear. Hir was in the frame. Hir was not alone. That was all hir had gone out for and hir had it, in the first tenth of a second, and if hir had stopped there and held the can lip and let them come — they could not come. The line face was sealed; Petra's people had the dock locked as tight as Dario's had. That was what *hold* meant. It meant *we are asking them to open it. Hold.*

The body does not hold. The body turns toward the Hum.

Hir pushed off.

---

The push was wrong. Hir knew it was wrong at the moment hir paws left the lip — the shaking that twenty-two minutes had put in hir had gone into the push, a few degrees, nothing, the kind of error a Kin corrects in the first metre with a tail-catch on the next rail. There was no next rail. Hir did the vector-flip anyway, three sweeps of the tail, and turned hirself to face them, and it changed hir heading by nothing at all, because that is what a vector-flip does: it turns you. It does not move you.

The Core gave the miss distance. Eleven metres.

Hir would pass the line face at eleven metres, at four metres a second, and go on.

*[Sorrel / eleven]*, hir sent, because someone should have the number.

They were at the face. Hir could see them through the gold — seven shapes at the port, and behind the port, Petra, and behind Petra the hatch that Petra would not open, and would not, and would not, and Rill read the smell of her from two hundred metres away in memory and it was the same as Dario's. Not anger. The other thing.

Hir went past them at eleven metres with hir hands out, and they had their hands out, and that was the whole of it. Seven Kin with their hands out and one Kin with hir hands out and a distance between them that nobody in the frame could close.

Hir went on.

---

Seventeen minutes is the number. The Core said it, and then it said sixteen, and then it said what it says.

Behind hir, the frame stayed open. That is the thing to tell. They did not time hir out and they did not re-key. Twelve Kin on the Dioscuri held a frame with a Hum in it that was getting fainter by the metre and sent, at mesh speed, everything: *[Here / here / here]*. Ashe told hir the number-two pump was fine. Ilex told hir a thing Ilex had been saving for hir first Telling, about a human named Wen, and it was funny, and hir laughed inside the sealed chest. The yard Kin — Dario's Kin, whose humans had pulled the beacon — sent nothing for a long time and then one of them, whose name hir did not know, sent *[Sorry]*, which is not a symbol Kin-code has, and hir understood it anyway.

Hir was crawling home with nothing to crawl on. Hir ears opened every ninety seconds and dumped heat into nothing and folded. Hir was drifting. The Core said the number. It is a very good assistant. It is not comforting.

The last thing hir sent, before the sleep, was the truest thing in the frame, and it was three symbols, and it did not need the fourth.

*[Not / alone]*.

---

*Rill-3's Hum was received on the Dioscuri for eleven hours. The body was recovered on the fourth day by a yard tug, frozen, and Read on the dock by both Clusters, which is the only time that has happened. Lyra-5 took the name. The line side and the yard side settled within the week; the settlement does not mention a Kin. The Kin call it their war. The Star-Bound call it the day they knew.*
