# S026 — Guest Slot
**POV:** Kin (Fescue-2) · **Form:** Short story · **Draft v2** (revision round 1, plus hand fixes; v1 is in git history)

---

The guest's tail went wrong just as the heap stopped moving, so they all had to come apart and do it again.

Fescue felt it before hir saw it. Rime laid the weave from the bottom, as Rime always did, with hir own tail first across the belly of the Nest and each of theirs under the last and over the next. The guest's tail came in last, from the left, and went in under Fescue's, where it was meant to lie. It stayed there until the heap let its breath out and the tendons began to lock. Then it slid over the top, turned, and hooked itself round Sorb's in a tidy figure that must have been how they did it on *Oxley Ring*. The whole weave crept a hand's width toward the bulkhead.

*[Again,]* Rime sent.

*[My apologies. It goes to its own pattern when I'm not attending to it,]* Aster sent.

*[Then attend to it ~ loud, and having the best night of the week]* Mallow sent. *[Fescue, admit it. It's a very good knot]*

Fescue did not admit anything. Hir unlocked hir legs, which hurt, since they had only just locked, and pushed off Sorb's hip so the Nest could open. The filters had cycled at the change of watch. In the dark the new ones smelled of burnt sugar, as they had since the operator fitted them, and nobody else minded; Mallow sent that it smelled like a party. Fescue asked the Core the time.

*Twenty-three forty.*

On the first night Rime had moved everyone round to make room, and Fescue had come out of it on the outside, on the cold side, next to the stranger. Hir had lost the warm hollow at the small of Sorb's back, where hir had always slept, left side down, with nothing between hir and the bulkhead but other people. Hir had not complained. Hir had taken it as understood that the guest now owed hir something for it: to lie still, to breathe on the count, and to keep hir weather down where a guest's weather belonged. Aster had been on *Keld Deep* since before the last ice run, and so far hir had paid back a very good knot.

The second settle went wrong because of Sorb. Sorb got the giggles when hir own tail went under, and couldn't stop, and shook the whole Nest on its lines until Rime sent hir name twice with nothing after it.

*[Put the guest in the middle,]* Mallow sent. *[Weave hir in like a patch. Nobody'll see]*

*[I'll sit on it! Give it here, I'll sit on it all night ~ still going, and helpless]* Sorb sent.

*[That will not be necessary,]* Aster sent.

In the middle of that, Fescue smelled salt fish.

*[Bryony. Is that my fish?]*

*[What fish? ~ oh no]* Bryony sent, with hir mouth full.

Fescue reached up to the pocket in the net by hir head, where the fish had been since the ice run, wrapped, under a coil of spare cable. Hir found the cable.

*[I'll get you another one,]* Bryony sent.

*[There isn't another one. That was the one]*

*[Settle,]* Rime sent.

On the third try Aster put hir tail in under Fescue's and held it there, stiff as a spar, so that Fescue could feel the effort in it along the whole length of hir own. It held. The heap breathed out together, and Fescue went under with the guest's tail pressing up on hirs like somebody leaning on a door.

---

The terminal woke hir at twenty past one. The relay had dropped its handshake with the dish again.

Hir came out of the lock slowly, with the Nest's last breath still in hir chest. Aster's eyes were open. They were open in the dark right beside hir, and when Fescue moved, they moved.

Hir drew hir tail out from over the guest's, left the guest's lying loose, kicked off to the console and caught the rail. It was the same fault. The dish had lost lock at the top of its sweep, and the relay had timed out waiting for it. Fescue reset it. While the handshake came back hir put the long-range channel up, sore in every joint, and went looking for *Oxley Ring*, three hundred and forty kilometres off, to find out what the guest's lot had to be so wound up about. There was nothing to find. *Oxley* was asleep, the whole Cluster down flat at the bottom of its frame, like decent people. Hir shut the channel.

Hir put the fault on the log with the time and the word BEARING, as hir had every night, so that when the operator read them all together hir would have to see it. A cartoon fish was stuck on the corner of the console, with its thumb up. Some human had put it there before any of them were posted to *Keld*, and it had been peeling off ever since, one corner at a time. Hir thumb found the curled edge and worried at it until the relay showed copy.

*[Bearing?]* Mallow sent from the Nest, barely awake. *[~ smug even asleep]*

*[Bearing]*

*[It's ice on the feed horn. You owe me a fish]*

*[Bryony owes me a fish]*

Hir went back. Aster was still awake.

Fescue lay on the cold side and composed, not for the first time, what hir would put to the operator. Hir had the whole list. The dish lost lock at the top of its sweep and nowhere else, which a feed horn with ice on it would not do, because ice doesn't care which way the dish is pointing. It happened at night, when the station cooled and the bearing tightened. It had happened on every night of the week, and every night the terminal had woken hir. After the second night the operator had written back, *Bearing within tolerance, please check config*. Fescue had checked the config. The config was hir own, and there was nothing wrong with it. Hir went down the list again, adding the filters to the bottom of it, and the fish, and the guest.

*[You're doing the list,]* Mallow sent, out of the dark. *[I can feel you doing the list]*

*[Go to sleep]*

*[I was asleep. Then you started the list]*

---

It went like that. Fescue did not need the Core to tell hir how the next two hours went, but hir asked it anyway, every time hir surfaced, as if the answer might have improved.

Aster did not move much, and that was not the trouble. The trouble was that hir was keyed into the frame, as a guest is, and hir weather sat at the top of it all night, wound so tight and so high that it came in on every one of them at once. With the heap under, the frame should have been down near nothing, so quiet that Fescue could have picked out Bryony dreaming. Instead there was a carrier at full gain in a room full of people trying to sleep, and it was on the cold side of the Nest, next to Fescue.

Twice the guest's tail began its figure again on its own, and Aster caught it. Once hir breathed out of time, a quick shallow breath halfway between theirs. Rime's next breath came early to meet it, which lifted Sorb, which woke Fescue.

*[Aster. Down,]* Rime sent. That was all of it.

Aster's weather stayed exactly where it was.

*[What do you call yours? Your weave. Has it got a name, on Oxley?]* Bryony sent at some point. Bryony was awake now too.

*[Bryony, lie down,]* Aster sent, short and level.

Bryony lay down. Fescue had been about to send the same thing, ruder, and found hir did not like it coming from the guest.

At five past three the relay dropped again. Hir reset it, put BEARING on the log, came back and locked in, and asked the Core the time before hir eyes were shut. *Zero three oh six.* Aster's weather came up to meet hir, as if hir had been waiting for Fescue to come back so there would be somebody to keep awake.

Fescue sent it at eight minutes past, and did not strip it.

*[Nobody on Oxley's lying awake over you. Give us the same ~ and meaning every word]*

All of them had it. Mallow's weather went flat. Sorb's jumped. Rime's did nothing at all. Bryony sent *[Fescue]* and then nothing, as if hir had lost the rest of it.

Aster sent nothing. For the first time since midnight hir weather came down. It came down all the way and stayed there.

---

At ten past three a new source came up on the long-range channel.

It came up on the public tier, very low, down by the noise floor where Fescue would have squelched it out on any console. It carried a hash nobody on *Keld Deep* had ever heard, and a vitals frame with it, and the answer went out of Fescue before the frame had finished. It went out of all six of them, on the same tier, in the same second, and out of every other Kin in range: the ice crew on *Tolland*, the two on the survey tug, whoever was out on Keld's ring. For that second the public tier was full.

Then the frame on *Oxley Ring* went to full gain, all of them at once. It reached *Keld* as weather and nothing else, bright and enormous, and whatever they were saying to the child inside it stayed there.

*[Whose is it?]* Bryony sent.

*[Mine,]* Aster sent. It went clean.

Sorb laughed out loud in the dark, and then *[Sorry! Sorry ~ not sorry at all]*. Mallow was already sending over the top of hir. *[Early? It's early, isn't it, you'd have said. Who's holding it? Who's there with it? Aster, who's—]*

*[Sallow,]* Aster sent. *[And Orpine, and our eldest. The rest of them]*

Rime sent nothing. But Rime's tail tightened once in the weave, the whole length of it, and all of them felt the Nest draw in.

Fescue lay on the outside, with hir line still sitting in every one of them, and asked the Core the time. *Zero three twelve.*

---

At twenty-six past, *Oxley Ring* went quiet.

The whole Cluster's weather dropped out in the same minute, from bright to nothing. Under where it had been, on the public tier, the child's carrier went thin.

Aster's weather went through the bottom of the frame and took the frame with it. Beside Fescue, the guest's ears folded flat to hir skull.

The beat had been coming in on its vitals frame steady and quick. Now it came in late, and then two came close together, and then there was a gap where one should have been, and when the next one came it came up out of the noise so low that hir had to go looking for it.

Nobody sent anything. The Nest was locked, and nobody in it was asleep.

*[Aster. They've all gone quiet. I have felt that before. I'm sorry]* Rime sent. It was the first thing Rime had sent to the guest since *Down*.

It went clean, and Fescue felt Mallow and Bryony believe it.

The carrier was still up.

*[It's still transmitting,]* Fescue sent.

*[Fescue ~ begging]* Mallow sent.

*[It's still transmitting. It's dropping frames and it's coming in late, but it's up. A carrier that's gone doesn't drop out like that. It goes]* The next beat came in under the noise, ragged, and hir sent that too. *[There. Listen to that]*

Then it came from Bryony and Mallow and Sorb, all at once.

*[Is it? Is it still—Fescue, is it?]* from Bryony.

*[Don't do that to hir if you're not sure. Don't ~ frightened, and angry about it]* from Mallow.

*[I can hear it! There! It's there, I can hear it too—]* from Sorb, who was halfway out of the lock.

*[I know what I felt,]* Rime sent, across all of them.

Rime did not send anything after that, and did not take it back. After a while Fescue felt Rime's weather go down into the lock, and then, alone of all of them, Rime slept. Rime's breath came on its own count, slow, while the other five lay in the weave and didn't breathe with it.

---

They listened for a long time.

Sorb could not lie still. Twice hir unlocked and pushed out as far as hir tail would let hir, and hung there on the end of it, and came back. The second time hir lay down with hir big head against the small of Bryony's back and left it there.

Mallow talked. Mallow sent about the operator's rota, and about the ice crew on *Tolland*, who kept a plant in their galley and argued over whose turn it was to water it, and about the survey tug. Hir did not stop, and nobody asked hir to. Hardly any of it had a rider on it.

Bryony asked. *[If it goes now, does it still count? We answered it. All of us did. So it's counted, isn't it? Even if]*

*[Bryony,]* Mallow sent.

*[I only want to know]*

Nobody answered hir. Aster didn't.

*[What's Sallow like?]* Mallow sent a little later, to Aster, when there was nothing left on the ring to talk about. *[Is hir the one who does the knot?]*

*[Hir is our second. Hir manages our stores,]* Aster sent, formally, as if Mallow had asked for a reference. Then, after a long time, *[Hir is very bad at it]*, and Mallow's weather went up a little in the dark, and Aster's did not.

Fescue listened. Both hir ears had come round sunward, toward *Oxley*. Hir took the noise floor, the station's own chatter and the tug and the far faint weather of the other Clusters on the ring, and pushed it down under everything, and kept the one carrier on top. The beat came, and missed, and came. Hir asked the Core the time and didn't listen to the answer. Hir counted gaps.

The gaps got shorter.

Hir did not send that, in case it was hir own wanting it. Hir waited for another run of beats and then another, while each one came in on its frame a little late, then less late, until they were arriving where hir had set hirself to expect them.

*[Steadier,]* Fescue sent, and nothing with it.

Then *Oxley Ring*'s weather came back up. It came slowly, from nothing, one of them and then several, low at first and then higher. It never came up as high as before. Nobody on *Oxley* said anything to *Keld Deep*.

---

At seven minutes past five the terminal chimed. Fescue unhooked and went to the console, where the dish had lost lock at the top of its sweep. Hir reset it, waited on the handshake, and put it on the log: the time, the fault, BEARING.

When Fescue looked back at the Nest, Aster had not moved. The guest lay with eyes open and both ears forward, asking the Core something, and then asking it again.
