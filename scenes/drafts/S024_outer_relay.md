# SCENE 024 — Outer Relay
**POV:** Kin (Pell-3) · **Form:** Short story

---

Firstly, the form is filled in. Pell filled it in on the walk up from the ring, in hir head, and then again at the terminal in the mess annex, and it is correct in every field, including next of kin, where hir has put *Cluster (Rue-3, Bracken-5, Linnet-2)*, which is also correct and also the reason hir is going.

Secondly, Agnes Ruhl is eating.

She has a paper twist of sunflower seeds open by her keyboard and a work jacket across her lap, and she is doing something to its zip with a pair of needle-nose pliers, and she has not looked up. The office smells of seed husk and warm plastic and, under that, of Agnes, who has come from somewhere with stairs. There is a line of sweat along her hairline. Her weather, as ever, is nothing. A human: flat as deck plate, everything on the outside, where Pell has to read it with hir nose like a dog at a bin.

"It's lunch," Agnes says.

"I know it's lunch. Your door was open."

"My door's always open. That's so people can see it's lunch." She cracks a seed with her front teeth, works the kernel out with her tongue and spits the two halves of shell into her cupped left hand. "Go on. You've got a face on."

Pell does not have a face on. Pell has a face. Hir sets the form on the desk, square to the edge, and pushes it across with one finger.

Agnes reads it without putting the pliers down. Pell watches her eyes get to the posting line, *Tallow Outer Relay, watch and maintenance, six months*, and then to the box under it, where the form asks, in the voice forms have, *Distance from nearest Kin (km): <12 / 12–800 / >800*, and to the last of the three, which Pell has ticked hard enough to dent the paper.

"This is E," Agnes says.

"It's the posting form."

"It's revision E. We're on F. F's got the extra box for dietary." She turns it over, looks at the blank back of it as if something might be written there, turns it back. "Doesn't matter. I'd only have to copy it across."

"So copy it across."

"No," says Agnes, "because I'm not signing it."

On the side of her console there is the strip that shows the ring crew's weather, one line a head, and Pell's line does something just then that Pell does not need to look at to know about. Two decks down, close enough to be next door, Rue feels it go.

*[Pell? ~ soft, reaching]*

*[Busy]*

*[You spiked ~ worried, fond, too much of both]*

Fond. It comes up the frame at hir like somebody opening an oven door, and it is kind, and it is all about Saturday, and there it is again without being sent for — *and I've known Dev since he was the one who let go of the torque wrench on* — and the sentence goes off the edge and Pell hauls it back, and the line on Agnes's console climbs a second time. Now Bracken as well. Linnet, sleepily, from wherever Linnet has got to.

*[I'm in a meeting]*

*[Sorry ~ not very]*

"Firstly," Pell says aloud.

"Oh God. It's a firstly."

"Firstly, Tallow has had no watch since the thirtieth day of this year. Your own report says so. The relay put in for a Kin for the dish realignment in the five-hundred-and-ninth year and got a human, and the human was sick for most of it, which I can quote you, *'nausea, persistent, not responsive to patches,'* and the dish is still out by—"

"Pell."

"—by a margin. Secondly, I'm qualified. Thirdly, there's no Compact rule on it, so you can't hide behind one."

"I'm not hiding behind anything. I'm hiding behind the refit." Agnes tips the handful of shells into the bin under the desk, wipes her palm on the jacket and takes up the pliers. "Starts on the ninth. You know it starts on the ninth, you've had the schedule since the spring. I've got the whole outer ring coming apart in sections and I need people on the snubs who don't let a spoke walk, and that's you. You're the best snub hand I've got. Rue's all right. Bracken's heavy. He lets the bite go early."

"Bracken's fine."

"Bracken dropped a spoke on the last refit."

"Bracken lowered a spoke rapidly," Pell says. "It's in the log as a controlled descent."

"It's in the log as that because Bracken wrote the log." She squints at the zip. It's the pull that's gone, the little tab, and she is trying to close the ring that holds it with the tips of the pliers, and every time she lets go it springs a bit wider. "So no. Come back after. Come back in the spring and I'll sign you out to Tallow for a year if you like, I couldn't care less."

"I don't want it in the spring."

"No, you want it now, because of Saturday." She doesn't look up. "I heard."

Everybody heard. There were a hundred and thirty people in the ring mess and the feed was open to the families on the inner stations, and Pell had been so quick all evening, so pleased with hirself for being quick, that when Dev's sister said *does anybody else want to*— That's as far as it goes. Past that is ⟨don't make me read it⟩ and hir does not.

Hir line goes up anyway. Hir can see the corner of it, a clean climb, like a load cell with somebody standing on the hook. Linnet, properly awake now, sends something wordless and warm that tastes of the little almond pastries the galley does on rest days, and that is worse, because there were almond pastries on Saturday, on the long table, under the banner with the spelling mistake.

*[Stop it]*

*[Stop what ~ honestly lost]*

"That's not why," Pell says.

"It's why." Agnes cracks another seed. "It's all over your line, love, I don't need to be Kin. You've been up and down like the service lift since Sunday."

"It's professional development."

"It's a sulk with a posting number. I don't mind. I'm not saying don't be embarrassed. I'm sure it was dire. Hana in stores said the bride's mother went out for a smoke halfway through, and there's no smoking on this station, so God knows where she went." She spits. "But I don't care, Pell. Sorry. I've got a refit."

"Where did she go?"

"What?"

"The bride's mother. Where did she go? Did Hana say?"

"I've no idea. The loo. Does it matter?"

It matters. It would matter enormously. Pell would give a great deal to know whether a woman in a green dress left the ring mess at nine minutes past twenty-one hundred because of what Pell was saying at nine minutes past twenty-one hundred or because her feet hurt, and there is no form for that, and there is nobody Pell can ask, and hir already knows the exact minute, which is no use at all.

Agnes has gone back to the zip.

There is a sticky note on the edge of her console, yellow, curling at the corners, that says *BERNARD — NO* in her square capitals. It has been there since at least the five-hundred-and-sixth year. Pell has never asked. Pell looks at it now for something to look at, and while hir is looking at it hir works it out, the thing under the refit, and it is so obvious hir is annoyed not to have got it on the stairs.

"You've posted one Kin past eight hundred," Pell says. "In thirty years. One."

"Have I."

"Vetch-2." It arrives whole, and Pell sets it down on the desk the way hir set down the form. "Posted to the Carrow deep-transit tender on the two-hundred-and-first day of the four-hundred-and-ninety-third year, revision B, eight months, your signature, countersigned by Operations. Hir came back on the ninetieth day of the four-hundred-and-ninety-fourth. At the ring meeting that week you said, and I'm quoting, *'Vetch'll want feeding up, so if anybody's got room on their rota, put hir on it, and don't ask hir about the trip.'* Hir came back thin, Agnes. You know hir came back thin. That's why you won't sign. It isn't the refit."

Agnes puts the pliers down. It is the first time she has put them down.

"Who?" she says.

"Vetch-2."

"Vetch," she says, trying it out. "Was hir ring crew?"

"Hull. Bay Six. Hir had a notch out of the left ear."

"Nineteen years ago."

"Nineteen years ago."

"Pell, I did every posting on this station before they split the office. Humans and Kin. Every form." She is not being clever. Pell can smell that she is not being clever; there is no fresh sweat, her hands are loose, she looks, if anything, a bit sorry for hir. "I couldn't tell you who I posted last Tuesday. Was hir all right? After?"

Pell opens hir mouth and what comes is: *transferred to Halcyon-1, the three-hundredth day of the four-hundred-and-ninety-fourth year, at own request*. Which is a fact. Which is all there is, because Pell never asked Vetch anything either.

"Hir transferred," Pell says.

"Well, there you go." Agnes picks the pliers back up. "Probably fine, then."

And the line on the console, which had been coming down, goes straight up through the top of its own scale and stays there, and Pell can hear hirself breathing, and down on deck nine the Cluster is not asking any more, they're just sending, all three of them, warmth on warmth on warmth, blankets thrown on a fire, and every one of them says *Saturday* and every one of them says *we saw* and Pell is — hir is a Kin of ninety standing in a postings office being wrong twice in a week in front of the one human on the ring who talks to hir as if hir were a rigger and not a pet, and *so raise your glasses, if you can find them, to* —

*[Stop. Stop. I mean it]*

*[Pell ~ frightened now]*

It is not the refit. Hir was right about that much. Hir only had the wrong fact.

"The three-hundred-and-twelfth day of the five-hundred-and-first year," Pell says. "Third shift. We were on the snub at spoke nineteen, because Toller had called in sick, and you'd brought a flask. You said, *'The good thing about you, Pell, is you don't count. I can say anything to you. It's like talking to the ring.'* And then you said, *'I'd have gone with him if he'd asked. He didn't ask. That's the bit I can't get past. Thirty-one years and he didn't even ask.'* And then you said, *'Don't you dare put that in your head,'* and I said I wouldn't." Hir breathes. "So I don't think it's the refit. I think if I go, you've got nobody on this ring to talk to."

Agnes doesn't move.

That is all Pell has: that she doesn't move. No weather. Nothing on any console anywhere. The pliers are open in her hand with the zip ring caught in the jaws, and she doesn't close them. Hir can smell her now, though, a hot sharp change coming off her skin all at once, and hir knows that one from the ring. It's what humans smell like when a line parts.

Pell could say it again. Hir could say it again in ten years, word for word, the flask and all. They both know that. It's sitting in the room between them like a third person.

Agnes puts the pliers on the desk, and the jacket over the back of her chair, and takes the form, revision E, and puts it on the pile to her left, which is the pile for things that aren't going anywhere.

"Pell-3," she says.

Not Pell. Not love. Thirty years and hir has never once heard her say the number.

"The answer's no. You're on the snub at spoke nineteen from the ninth. Shut the door on your way out."

Pell shuts the door.

In the corridor two of the galley staff are coming the other way with a trolley, and one of them says something and the other one laughs, a big helpless bark, and Pell walks past them very fast with hir ears flat and does not turn round to find out what it was about.

The ring is already coming apart when hir gets down to it. The riggers have strung the first of the refit catenaries between spokes seventeen and twenty-one, orange line on grey, and somebody's music is going on a speaker hung off a bollard, too loud, the same song as last week. Rue is at nineteen with a coil over hir shoulder. Hir looks at Pell, and hir mouth opens, and Pell holds up a hand, and Rue shuts it again.

Pell clips hir lanyard to the rail, tugs it, clips the second one. Takes the tail of the line off Rue without a word and walks it to the snub post and lays three turns on, tight, the way Agnes likes them, and leans back against the bite till hir feels the preload start to come on through the post and into hir feet.

"Taking," hir calls up the spoke, and somebody up there says, "Taking," and the spoke creaks, and Pell eases off a hand's width of slack, and another, and—
