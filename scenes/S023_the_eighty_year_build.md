# SCENE 023 — Long-Baseline Survey Platform, Uncrewed
**POV:** Kin (Juniper, later Sable-6) · the rock the humans called *[Kettle]*, outer Belt · AF 158–236 · **Form:** Short story
**Canon status:** DRAFT. Dramatises D-108 (how the arks were built), D-109 (the fait accompli), D-110 (the Weavers' opposition), D-90 (how a name passes). Offers no new canon.
**Convention:** Kin POV — *hir*. Section headers in Kin date form; the Kin do not abbreviate a date (DOC-00 §5). The ship is never named in the text.

## The hundred-and-fifty-eighth year

The first thing was spars.

Four hundred and eleven of them in the first decade, cut from the rock's own nickel-iron and trued to a tolerance the fabricator could hold and Juniper's Core could check faster than the fabricator could report it. They came off the line looking like nothing. A spar is eleven metres of nothing. You could stand in the stockyard with four hundred of them racked around you and see a stockyard.

Ivo used to stand in there anyway. He liked the smell.

He was twenty-six when he came out, which Juniper did not understand for about a year, having assumed he was older and slow. He asked more questions than any human hir had met: whether hir could hear a dog whistle, whether the ears were for the RADAR or the cooling and whether that was two answers or one, what it was like to have a tail, whether it was rude to ask what it was like to have a tail. He learned the pause in about four months, which is fast. He ran the galley fan before the Kin shift without ever mentioning that he did it.

The work order on his slate said *long-baseline survey platform, uncrewed.*

He read it out to them once, on his first week, the way a new supervisor does — here is what we are doing, here is the schedule — and Wren, who was young then and has never entirely lived it down, said *[Yes / Understood / Spars]* and went back to the line.

Ivo went up to yard manager in the hundred-and-seventy-seventh year and cried at the send-off, which the Kin found extraordinary and have retold ever since.

## The hundred-and-seventies

Noor did not like them.

She did not dislike them either; she had simply come from three tethers and a Mars contract and she ran a rock the way you run a rock, which is on the numbers, and the numbers on [Kettle] were absurd. Nobody had rotated out in twenty years. The fault rate on the spar line was a tenth of anywhere she had worked. The crew chief could give her material-state on any component she named, from memory, instantly, including four hundred she had not asked about, and had never once filed a report.

She wrote that up as a compliance risk and was correct.

The second decade was ribs. The spars went into frames now, curved, thirty metres across the chord, and they went out of the yard on barges to two other rocks, because the work was spread and always had been. From the stockyard you could see about nine per cent of it at any time. Noor never saw more than that and neither did anyone else, because the thing that would have shown it to you was going outside, and humans do not go outside.

She came round in the hundred-and-eighty-eighth year, in the way people do, which is over about four years and without announcing it. By the end she was asking Juniper to sit in on human meetings for reasons she never gave and everyone understood.

She died on the rock in the hundred-and-ninety-ninth year — a cardiac thing, sixty-one, in her own quarters, alone, which is a way of dying that does not exist for the Kin and which Juniper has read again more times than is good for hir.

The Cluster brought her in.

That is not a decision anybody made. They went and got her and they carried her, the whole way, all seven of them and nobody riding the rail, and they put her down in the medical bay warm, because that is what you do, and it did not occur to any of them until afterwards that for a human it does not matter whether the body is warm.

Nineteen humans stood in the corridor and watched them do it and did not know what they were watching.

Afterwards Juniper heard one of them trying to explain it to another, and getting it wrong, and getting it beautifully wrong, and said nothing.

## The two-hundredth year

Dan was the one they were fondest of and he is the reason the ship exists.

He was good at the job and he liked them enormously and he brought his children out at solstice for eleven years running, which is a nine-day transit each way for four days on a rock with no view, and the children learned to say *hir* and one of them still says it. He knew all their names. He knew which of them had carried whom. He asked Bramble-2 about the count of hir name and got the answer and remembered it.

He never asked what they were building. Not once, in twenty years.

It was not incuriosity about them — he was the least incurious human about *them* that anybody there ever met. It was that the work order said *long-baseline survey platform, uncrewed*, and Dan had been handed that file by Noor's successor who had been handed it by Noor who had been handed it by Ivo, and it had been true in the sense that documents are true for forty-two years, and you do not audit a thing that has never given you trouble.

The third decade was the throat.

Reactor throat, mated at the main rock in the two-hundred-and-eighth year, and from that point anybody standing outside in the dark could have read the whole shape of it off the RADAR return in about nine seconds.

Nobody stood outside in the dark.

## The two-hundred-and-ninth year, which is not about a human at all

Ember-3 came out from Sol on a supply run and stayed eleven days, and for three of them argued that the ship should not be built.

Hir argument was not about humans and not about money and not about any of the things the humans would have expected if the humans had been able to hear it. It was this: twelve hundred Kin is twelve hundred lineages of Readings, and every one of them will die out there, and not one of them will ever be taken at Sol. Long Messages are *received* and received is not carried. **Hir called it an amputation, and hir used that symbol, and nobody in the frame has forgotten which symbol hir used.**

Juniper argued the other side. So did Wren, badly. So, in the end, did most of the rock.

It ran in the frame for three days at full rate, which is to say it was the largest argument any of them had ever been inside, and it was conducted at twelve kilometres and 100 megabits in a language that has no written form, in front of nineteen humans who were being extremely courteous to a visiting inspector.

Ember-3 lost, and said so, and helped rack spars for the last six days, and went home.

The site log for that fortnight records: *WEAVER REP — SITE VISIT — NO FINDINGS.*

## The two-hundred-and-twenty-first year

Margit was not clever and would have said so herself.

She was an auditor, she had been sent out because the contract was up for renewal and somebody at the operator wanted a clean file, and she had eighty years of records on a slate and no particular idea what she was looking for. She was fifty-four. She had never worked with Kin before and was nervous about it in a way she handled by being very slightly too formal, which the Cluster found endearing and did not tell her.

She found the discontinuity on her fourth day.

"Crew chief, hundred-and-fifty-eight, Juniper," she said. "Crew chief, current, Sable-6. I've got no transfer record in between. Who's been running this line?"

The pause. Hir held the answer for a moment because hir could see, already, roughly where this was going to go, and wanted to be sure of being kind about it.

"I have."

"Since when?"

"The hundred-and-fifty-eighth year."

Margit looked at hir slate and then at hir and then at hir slate.

"Then who's Juniper?"

"I was. I set it down in the hundred-and-ninety-fourth year, when Sable-5 died and I took hir, and Juniper came free and went to a child in the two-hundred-and-eleventh. Hir is on the barge line at the second rock. Hir is eleven."

"You changed your *name*."

"I took one." Hir considered how much of this a human needs. "The count is the number of us there have been. Hir was the fifth. I am the sixth. When I go it will be somebody else's and they will be the seventh, and they will know all of us, because I will have given them us."

Margit sat down, which humans do.

"Who else," she said after a while, "was here at the start?"

"All of us."

---

She spent two more days on it and she did it properly, which nobody had done in eighty years. She pulled the contracting entity and found it had changed hands four times and had, all four times, the same registered address. She pulled the tonnage. She pulled eighty years of fabrication against the schedule for a long-baseline survey platform and got a number that was wrong by a factor she read three times.

Then she came and found Sable-6 on the stockyard gantry and asked the question.

"What is it?"

"It is a ship."

"Where does it go?"

"Wolf 359. Seven point nine light-years. It will take about two hundred and sixty years." Hir watched her take that at the speed humans take things, and waited, and then said the rest because hir had been asked. "There will be twelve hundred of us aboard. It is not finished. We think fifteen more years."

Margit said, "Does anyone *know*?"

And Sable-6 said:

"Everyone who has asked."

---

She wanted to see it, and there was no way to do that from inside, so they suited her.

It took an hour to get her ready and forty seconds for the four of them, and then they went out onto the hull with a human on a tether between them and walked her up the spine in the dark.

The Kin do not light things. There was nothing to see by. Sable-6 had to keep telling her where she was — *above you, four metres; that dark is not empty, that is the number two truss; put your hand here* — and Margit went along it with one glove on eighty years of work, in the black, at the end of a line, saying nothing at all for eleven minutes.

Then she said: "How long has this been out here where anyone could look at it?"

"Since the two-hundred-and-eighth year."

"Thirteen years."

"Yes."

"And nobody came out."

"No," said Sable-6, who had never once thought of it as remarkable until that moment, and who has thought about it a great deal since.

---

## What she did

She filed it.

She wrote it accurately and at length and she named the thing, and she attached the tonnage and the contracting entity and the registered address, and she flagged it up two levels and copied the Compact, which she was not required to do and did anyway.

She told Sable-6 she had done it, standing in the corridor outside the medical bay where Noor had been put down warm twenty-two years before, and she was braced for something.

"Thank you for telling me," said Sable-6.

"That's it?"

"What would you like me to do?"

"I don't know. Be worried."

Sable-6 thought about it, because hir had been asked directly and because the answer was not kind and Margit had earned better than a soft one.

"You will be very slow about it," hir said.

---

The Compact opened a docket in the two-hundred-and-twenty-second year.

It is a good docket. It is thorough and it is correct in every particular and it contains Margit's report in full, and a contract review, and eleven submissions from three operators about the availability of qualified vacuum labour in the outer Belt, and a question of jurisdiction that has never been settled because it has never had to be.

The file is still open.

The ship left in the two-hundred-and-thirty-sixth year.

---

Sable-6 is a hundred and eighteen and can still feel all of it: the smell Ivo liked in the stockyard, Noor's face the day she stopped disliking them, Dan's children shouting *hir* down a corridor in the wrong register, Margit with one glove on the number two truss in the dark, not saying anything, for eleven minutes.

Hir will have all four of them exactly for as long as hir has anything.

Hir will not be able to give any of them to anyone.

## Canon consistency

- **D-108, the whole scene.** Labour not money; the outer Belt; the same Cluster from first spar to boarding; the Trust's descendants behind a contracting entity that keeps changing hands to the same address; **and no law broken, because there is no law against building a ship.** The eighty-year build is one continuous act of attention, and the humans arrive in four twenty-year slices.
- **Nobody asked.** The scene's engine and D-108's *legal, contested, and finished before the argument was.* The Kin never conceal it; Sable-6 answers immediately and completely the first time anybody puts the question. The work order says *long-baseline survey platform, **uncrewed***, and the file was true in the way documents are true for forty-two years. **D-19/D-101 inverted:** the species that writes things down trusted the writing over the people.
- **Humans do not go outside.** Thirteen years of a mated spine visible to anyone with a suit, and the first human to look at it is the one who asked. That is the tech and body asymmetry (DOC-01E) doing the plot's work.
- **D-109, the fait accompli.** The docket opens in AF 222 and the ship leaves in 236. *You will be very slow about it* is the Bandwidth Gap as politics.
- **D-110, dramatised.** Ember-3's three days, at 100 Mbps, in front of nineteen courteous humans, logged as **WEAVER REP — SITE VISIT — NO FINDINGS.** The only argument that ever mattered about the arks, unhearable, unwritten, unlogged.
- **D-90, how a name passes.** Juniper → Sable-6: set down only to take a dead one's; the set-down name comes free and goes to a child; the count is the number there have ever been. **The sole discontinuity in eighty years of human records is a name change, and it is the least significant thing that happened.**
- **Body recovery** (D-17, DOC-10 §4). The Cluster carries Noor in warm because it does not occur to them to do otherwise, and only afterwards notices it made no difference. Nineteen humans watch and do not know what they are watching.
- **D-79.** Hir loved all four and the scene never says so, except in the last three lines.
- **D-57, D-104.** Hir is 118 — inside the window — so hir can still *feel* all of it. The close is DOC-12 §3: humans are the one thing the Kin cannot carry.
- ***Long Reach* is never named and its end is never mentioned.** The reader supplies AF 471 unaided, or does not.
