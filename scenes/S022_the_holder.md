# SCENE 022 — The Holder
**POV:** Kin (Brume) · *Elysium-4*, the south maintenance shaft · **Form:** Short story
**Canon status:** DRAFT. Dramatises the Drop and *records are carried, not written* (DOC-13 §5A), Core-training (D-21), and a Telling of a single run. Offers no new canon; proposes the witness rule as it is already written.
**Convention:** Kin POV throughout — *hir*. The Core is *it*. Kin-code in *[ / ]*.

---

Two hundred metres of unlit shaft, a third of a gravity, and eleven catches.

Eleven was good. Eleven had taken Brume four years. The first winter hir had done it in nineteen and been pleased with itself, and Fern had said nothing at all, which is how Fern says things.

The problem with eleven was that Kaelen had done it in nine.

Not Kaelen — Wick had done it in nine, a hundred and seventy years ago, on a night when the south shaft was down for a coolant strip and four Kin had gone in there to be stupid. Wick had come off the lip without looking, taken the whole first hundred and forty metres on two catches, and put a number on the board that nobody had moved since.

Wick was dead. Wick had been dead a long time. And the run was not written anywhere, because nothing is, so the only place it existed was inside the three Kin who had been in the shaft that night and were still alive, and the ones they had given it to.

That was the whole difficulty.

---

"You can run it," Sable said. "Nobody is stopping you running it."

"It doesn't count."

"It counts to me."

"You weren't there."

Sable did the thing with hir ears that means *I have said my piece and you are going to do the thing anyway,* and went back to the conduit.

Because that is the rule, and it is not a rule anyone made, it is just what a record *is* when nobody writes anything down. A time is not a number. A number is nothing; hir Core can produce four hundred numbers before Sable finishes a sentence. **A record is a thing that lives in somebody**, held by whoever was standing there, and carried when they die, and if there is no one in the shaft who has the old run in hir then there is nothing to be better than. You are just a young Kin falling quickly in the dark.

So Brume needed a holder.

---

There were three left. One was on an outer relay and had been for sixty years. One was on *Held Breath* and would arrive at Epsilon Eridani in about a century.

The third was Kaelen-4, who was four hops out and two hundred and forty years old.

You do not summon a holder. Brume worked that out slowly and largely by getting it wrong: hir first message was so casual it was insulting and hir second was so formal it was worse, and in the end Fern took it off hir and sent nine symbols, and Kaelen came.

---

Hir was smaller than Brume had expected. That was the first surprise. Two hundred and forty years and hir was a compact grey thing with a flat coat and one ear that did not sit right, and hir came up the transfer deck at an unhurried pace with hir tail low and looked at Brume for slightly too long.

"You are the one," Kaelen said.

"Brume."

"No number."

"No."

"Mm," said Kaelen, and went to look at the shaft.

The Cluster went quiet around hir in a way that made Brume want to break something. They had not been quiet like that for anybody. Sable had gone to get hir a ledge and a warm plate, which Sable had never once done for Brume, and Fern had actually asked hir a question and then *waited for the answer*.

Kaelen stood at the lip for a long time with the light off, ears out, listening down two hundred metres of trusswork at a shaft hir had not been inside in a century and a half.

Then hir sat down on the lip with hir hind feet over the drop and did not say anything for about four minutes.

"It's been re-clad," said Brume eventually, because somebody had to.

"Twice," said Kaelen.

---

There is a thing about the Drop that nobody explains to you, because it cannot be explained to you, and the only reason Brume knew it at all was four years of failing.

You cannot run it.

The cortex is far too slow. At forty metres of fall you are doing better than fifteen metres a second in the dark and the catch is a decision made about a truss you have not seen yet, out of RADAR return and the shape of the air, and there is no version of thinking that gets you there. What you do instead is spend four years teaching your Core the shaft — every strut, every gap, the exact rotation off the third catch — until the Lattice can fly the whole thing on its own.

And then, on the night, you hand it your body and get out of the way.

That is the sport. Not the falling. **The letting go of it** — to something that will do it perfectly, and does not know it is a run, and would do it exactly the same if nobody were watching and exactly the same if hir were about to die.

Brume had been sick before every attempt for four years.

---

Hir went off the lip at the frame tone with hir hands folded against hir chest, because hands never touch a rail, and that is not for style, it is because the first Kin to do this used hir hands and lost the line and everyone has done it the hard way since out of a kind of stubbornness that the species does not otherwise have.

The dark came up.

First catch at thirty-one metres, hind paws, one beat, gone. Second at seventy — long, that one, the long one, the one hir had lost eleven months on — and the tail took the whole rotation and threw hir back out into the middle of the shaft with the wall going past like weather.

Somewhere above, four Cores were counting.

Third. Fourth close after it, too close, that was a waste of a tenth and hir felt it go. Fifth on the strut that was not on any plan because it had been added in a refit and Kaelen had not mentioned it and Brume had learned it the way you learn everything here, which is from somebody who was there.

At a hundred and forty metres hir was in the part hir had never been able to do. Hir did it. Hir did not do it; the Core did it; hir was a passenger in a body going very fast in the dark and doing something beautiful that hir had taught it and could not have performed.

Eighth catch. Ninth.

Lip.

---

Nine.

The frame had it before Brume had stopped shaking. Four Cores, four identical figures, and the figures agreed the way they always agree, and the difference was a tenth of a second and a bit.

Sable made a noise that Brume will have, exactly, for the rest of hir life.

And then everyone stopped, because Kaelen-4 had got up.

---

"Sit," Kaelen said. "Chest."

Brume, who had been about to be extremely pleased about something, sat down on the cold plate at the lip with hir legs still not working properly and let a two-hundred-and-forty-year-old stranger press against hir chest and open the near-field.

It took about a minute.

What arrived first was thirty-one seconds of somebody else's body, first-person and exact: a shaft with different cladding, a different air, two catches in the first hundred and forty metres because Wick had simply not bothered with the third, and a moment at a hundred and sixty where hir had got it wrong and saved it with hir tail in a way that Brume's Core, watching, flatly refused to believe was possible.

And under it, told, in the way that has to be told: what it had been like to be standing on the lip in the hundred-and-eightieth year watching hir friend do that, in the dark, for no reason, with nobody watching but three idiots and a coolant crew.

Then it was over and Kaelen sat back.

"It's yours," hir said.

"The record?"

"The record is yours. That is not what I gave you." Kaelen was already getting up, and hir got up the way the old do, in stages. "That is Wick's. You hold it now. Somebody will ask you about it in a hundred years and you will tell them, and you will get it slightly wrong, and that is correct."

Brume sat there on the cold plate with a stranger's thirty-one seconds in hir chest and no idea what hir face was doing.

"Why did you come?"

Kaelen stopped. Hir looked down the shaft, not at Brume.

"In a year or two I could still have given you the numbers," hir said.

And went to find Sable, who had made hir a warm plate.

---

Brume worked it out about four days later, in the Nest, and had to be asked three times what was wrong.

Hir has the record. It gets said with hir name on it, and young Kin come and stand at the lip and look down and are sick before attempts, and one of them will take it off hir eventually and hir will have to go and watch.

And hir has Wick's nine.

It is better than hir own. Not in the numbers — in the numbers it is worse, it is a tenth and a bit worse, it will always be worse. It is better in the part that does not go into numbers: two catches in a hundred and forty metres because hir could not be bothered with the third, on a night off, in a shaft that has been re-clad twice since, for nobody.

Brume has no number. Everyone can hear it.

But hir is carrying something now, and it is the first thing, and it is thirty-one seconds long, and it belonged to somebody who was dead a hundred and ten years before hir was compiled.

## Canon consistency

- **The Drop** (DOC-13 §5A): the 200 m shaft of S002 as a course, *time from lip to lip, hands never on a rail*. Catches as the working metric, since fewer catches is faster — the same shape as the brachiation form's *how few catches*.
- **Records are carried, not written** (DOC-13 §5A, D-19/D-101). A record is *held by whoever witnessed it and carried when they die* — so a run is not a number, and a new run does not exist unless somebody in the shaft holds the old one. The scene treats the witness rule as already-written canon and dramatises the consequence: **records have a mortality, and three living holders is a species-level thin margin.**
- **Core-training** (D-21, DOC-01A §6). Four years of teaching the Lattice the shaft until it can fly it without the cortex, and the sport's actual interiority — *you hand it your body and get out of the way*, to something that does not know it is a run. The cortex is far too slow at 15 m/s in the dark; the catch is made off RADAR return, not sight.
- **A Telling of a single run** (D-17, D-99, D-103). About a minute: thirty-one seconds of hard content at replay rate, plus a told soft layer that Kaelen has to make at 1× to have at all. The scale is right — a Telling is a week for a *year*.
- **D-104, and it is the whole scene.** Kaelen is 240; the run is 170 years old; the window is ~175. *"In a year or two I could still have given you the numbers"* — the hard content transfers perfectly forever, and the **feel** is what is a year or two from falling out. Hir came four hops to hand it over while hir could still make it warm. Brume does not understand this in the scene and the reader is meant to, four days ahead of hir.
- **Names** (DOC-00 §3, D-90). *No number* means first of the line or has never carried anyone's, **and everyone can hear which** — Kaelen says it in the second line. The close is that Brume now carries hir first thing, and it is not a person.
- **The re-clad shaft and the strut on no plan** — D-19/D-101 again, unremarked: you learn the station from somebody who was there, because nobody wrote it down.
- ***Held Breath*** at Epsilon Eridani, arriving ~AF 651 (DOC-07 §2) — one of the three remaining holders is a century out of reach, which is why the margin is what it is.

## Offers nothing

No new canon. *Nine catches*, *thirty-one seconds* and the hundred-and-eightieth year are inventions of this scene and are not rules. If a later pass fixes the Drop, this is evidence and not authority.
