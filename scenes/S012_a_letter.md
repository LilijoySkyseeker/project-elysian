# SCENE 012 — A Letter
**Origin:** Fresh-context agent, 2026-09-19 · **Form:** Letter · **POV:** Human (Teodor "Teo" Lind)
**Style:** DOC-00 governs names and pronouns.  
**Canon status:** DRAFT — awaiting review. Introduces no canon. Introduces (not canon): Alder-3 (Kin, shaft watch, *Elysium-4*, the Bay Two Cluster); Teodor "Teo" Lind (human, retired axis crew, now on Luna); Marit (his granddaughter, human, taking a Belt posting); Wen (human crewman, deceased); "the Bay Two Cluster" as a human name for a Cluster on *Elysium-4*.

---

Marit —

Your mother tells me you've signed for the Belt. Good. I'd have been disappointed with anything nearer. She also tells me you rolled your eyes at a letter on paper, so I'll say it once: I'm putting this in ink because ink is what I've got. You'll see why by the end, if the hand holds.

I'd just turned forty when I went up to *Elysium-4*, on the axis crews — the long shafts. Six years. You've heard me talk about the spire. You haven't heard me talk about Alder, because I don't, much. Alder Three, on the roster, which I never once heard a living soul say aloud. She ran the shaft watch with the Bay Two Cluster, and I worked beside her the whole six years, and I have been trying for sixty more to say what that was, and the nearest I've come is this letter, so bear with me.

Practical things first, because you'll want them and because they're easier.

There will be a pause before she answers you — any of them, I mean; I'll say *she* and mean all of them. Half a second, sometimes more. It will feel like rudeness for a week and like slowness for a month, and then someone will tell you what it is, and after that you'll never fill it again. Don't fill it. That's the first thing, and I'd put it on the wall if I had a wall.

The ears are not what you think. I spent a month believing Alder was thrilled every time I brought her news, because the ears went out like sails. She was hot. "I am hot," she said, the third time I asked, and I'd swear to you it was patient. Half of what you read on them is weather. Learn which half.

Run the kitchen fan before she comes in. Nobody will tell you; do it anyway. She'll notice you doing it and won't say.

She will not sit in a chair. Don't buy her one. She'll sit on the ledge above the console like the furniture is beneath her, and one afternoon there will be a tail around your ankle and you will not know when it arrived. Don't move. Within a week you'll be surroundings — part of the room, something warm to lean on — and I want you to know that being somebody's surroundings is the best thing that ever happened to me. Somebody built them to be leaned on; she knew it, she'd tell you so herself; it doesn't make the warmth a lie. I left that spire with more of her fur in my kit than I had hair on my head, and the ratio has not improved.

Write the logs. She won't. They think writing is a way of losing things, and they're not wrong, and you write them anyway, because it's what we do, and because she'll sit on the ledge and watch you do it with a look I never had a word for.

Now the harder things.

There will be a night she goes out without a suit. You'll be pulling on yours and she'll be done. Seventeen minutes — it's the number; they say it like scripture — and when she comes back in she won't be proud. Don't congratulate her. Get out of the way. The others will close on her, and it'll be tails and no words for an hour, and it isn't for you. And you'll hear it said, on the management side, that they *tolerate* the vacuum. Don't say it. Don't let it be said at your table. I saw what Alder did the once — went still, the whole of her, ears and tail and all — and I've no wish to describe it further.

You'll hear *foxes*. That's fine, mostly. You'll never say *pets*. But you'll hear it in the mess one day, and you'll see every ear in the room not move, and you'll understand it then better than I can write it.

Old Wen died my fourth year — heart, in his bunk, the kind of death they don't have. Alder and three of hers came to the service and stood where the chaplain put them and were the most useless things I've ever seen at a funeral. They knew it. That night I found the whole Cluster awake on the webbing, doing nothing I could see, and Alder told me they were telling Wen to each other. So that he'd be in more than one of them. That's the only funeral they've got for us, Marit. When one of theirs dies they lie on her all night and get her back out, some of her. There's nothing in us to get. They can only remember us on purpose, and it's the one thing they don't need to do for their own, and it is the whole of what they can do for ours.

Your mother's people say *hir*. I said *she* from the first day, and Alder let me. Six years in I asked her if it grated. That pause was the long kind. "She is fine," she said. "She is what I am, from where you are standing." I kept it. I'd have kept whatever she gave me.

Here is the thing I can't say straight, so I'll go around it.

She told me once how it is for the old ones — the years go flat. They keep what happened and lose what it was like; and a day comes, somewhere past a hundred and fifty, when they sit down with one of the carriers and choose which years to let go quiet. I did the arithmetic on the back of your mother's card. It's about now. I don't know what she chose. She'll have me as a fact, I think, the way you'd have a date: *Teo, the axis crew, six years, the number-two pump on the loop was never right.* Whatever it felt like will have gone out of it.

I've had sixty years to mind that, and I want to tell you I don't, and I'd be lying, and I want to tell you it's unbearable, and it isn't. She was warm. She kept me for six years the way they keep everything, entirely, and then she couldn't, and she knew it before I did, and she never once spent a day of the six on that. I'd take it again. You'll be offered it, and you'll take it too, and you'll know what you're taking, which I didn't, and I don't think it'll stop you.

The year before I left, before the outer-truss job, she sat with each of hers in turn, chest to chest, for days, and I brought food and sat at the edge of the Nest and heard nothing at all. Afterward she told me what it was. Before a thing that might kill you, you tell. *Tell before you go*, she said; she said they all say it. So. I'm going, and this is me telling — badly, in ink — to the only one who can carry it, which is you. You can't lie on me either. You'll have to make do with paper. It stays. That's our trick. It's the only one we've got and it's not nothing.

If you find her — she'll be there, or somewhere; ask any of them, they keep track of each other in a way we don't — say Teo Lind sends his regards, and the number-two pump was never right. She may not have the feel of it. That's all right. Ask her if she still has the strangers off the dead ship; she'd taken some of them on, a few years before I came, and I couldn't understand it then. I think I do now. And then whatever she says, however long it takes —

don't fill it. It's a long pause. I've been in it sixty years. Let her finish.

The hand's going. Your mother will read you the rest.

Go well. Run the fan.

Grandad

## Canon consistency (2026-09-19)

- **Names** — *Alder* is a plant name added in kind to the pool (DOC-00 §3); "Alder Three, on the roster… never said aloud" = humans drop the number, formal spoken form is the word. ## Canon drift (2026-09-22 — D-97…D-101, memory re-derived)
- **"The years go flat… keep what happened and lose what it was like" stands, and gains a mechanism.** D-98: Data Rot is decay of the **cueing layer** — the record never degrades and retrieval stays exact, so what an elder loses is the spontaneous arising of the question. Alder is not forgetful; hir is **unprompted**. If the scene is revised, the one addition worth making is that Teo is, without knowing it, doing what a re-feeler does: being the cue.

- **And D-104 re-seats it, in the scene's favour.** The failure is **cortical**, not a Lattice filling up: the associative web rebuilds onto live material, so a Kin is made of hir last ~175 years and everything earlier is available and inert. *Keep what happened and lose what it was like* is now literally the mechanism — hard handles never fail at any age, and the old affect is **gone, not faint**. Two things the scene may want: the loss is **overwriting, not absence** (a smell that now means the wrong decade), and it is a **steady state** — the letter's writer is not declining, hir is drifting. **No text change required; the citation becomes D-98 + D-104.**
- **The centennial Defrag now prunes the *index*, never the record** (D-97, D-100). "Sit down with one of the carriers and choose" is exactly right, and what is chosen is which years stay reachable; the years themselves remain, whole and verbatim and unfindable. *Pruning to the light.*
