# X01 — Chapter 2 (v2)
**POV:** Madder-4 · **Form:** Chapter

---

*[What's burning]* went out from four of them at once, and Madder was one of the four.

Haw was the fifth, and was sending something else over the top of it.

*[I was falling fast ~ still falling, thrilled with it]*

*[Your leg's on my back]*

*[Whose leg, which one, I've got six—]*

*[Why is it heavy]*

*[It's hot on the left. It's hot on the right. Madder, it's hot on—]*

*[Sloe, that's my arm you're lying on]*

*[I'm not lying on anything, something's lying on me ~ muffled, indignant, and under that frightened]*

Madder got an eye open, and saw it.

Flame. Not a red mark on a deck plan, not a drill: the thing itself, standing up out of the ferns an arm's length away, yellow and tearing, with nothing between it and them but air. All five of them went rigid in the same instant. Somebody whined. Madder's ears had fanned wide against the heat before hir knew hir had them.

*[FIRE ~ five of them, on top of each other]*

*[Where's suppression, where's the—]*

*[Seal it, seal the—]*

*[There's no hatch. There's no wall. There's nothing to seal]*

They had gone down at the end of second watch in the web by the coolant bay, and they had come up in a heap. Nothing else was right. A heap in the web drifted. This one had gone flat like a sack put down, every body pressed hard into the one below, and Madder was near the bottom with Sloe's weight across hir ribs, and the ground under all of them was not a deck.

Hir put the checklist up. *Heads. Hands. Air. Exits.* Hir got as far as hands. Right against the heap, touching it, stood a box on wheels, and Yarrow's lower left hand was locked on its tailboard, closed there in sleep. Then four other threads came in over the checklist and hir lost the place.

*[There's a box. There's a box on wheels, Yarrow's holding a box on wheels]*

*[Something ran]*

That one was Teasel's, and it came clean, with nothing under it at all.

Madder swivelled both ears downhill. There was running down there, small and fast and more than one of it, and a smell came up the slope after it, sharp and sour, like a pump seal going. It went away until the fire covered it.

*[Where? What? How many? Is it coming back? Can I—]*

*[Two. Three? Small ~ counting, not sure of the count]*

*[Let it go,]* Madder sent, and Haw let it go, mostly.

Hir reached for the frame and it gave hir five before hir had finished asking. Sloe was hot and loud; Haw was louder; Yarrow was level as a strut; Teasel was held small and tight. And hirself. Five, all there.

Then, in the same instant, with nobody calling it, all five of them reached past each other for the ship: for fourteen hundred others and the Hum that had been under every hour of Madder's hundred and forty years. All five found the same thing together. There was nothing in the sky.

The fright went out through the frame and came back off four other bodies, doubled and doubled, before Madder could have said whose it had been first. Five tails went tight at once, round a leg, round a wrist, round each other, and pulled the heap in closer. Somebody made a noise through the nose, high and thin, like a line singing under load. It might have been hir.

*[Where's the bay]*

*[Where's anyone]*

*[If the Nest had five of us come off the table in one second—]*

*[Don't]*

*[I'm only saying what it looks like from there ~ and wishing hir wasn't]*

*[That's what it looks like when somebody—]* That was Teasel, clean, and it stopped halfway. Teasel's tail came round Madder's wrist and stayed.

*[Unconfirmed,]* Madder sent.

Nobody accepted it. Nobody argued with it either. The fright went round and round between them and would not settle; each time it thinned in one body it came back off another. Sloe was whining, very low, on every breath out. Madder counted hir own breaths and lost the count and started again. Hir asked the Core for the time and got ninety seconds, and asked again and got four minutes, and nothing had changed but the light on the underside of the leaves, gone from yellow to orange.

---

When Sloe rolled off hir, Madder's chest did not come up after the weight had gone, and hir had to lift it. Hir got hir four lower hands under hir and pushed. Hir tail lay out behind hir like somebody else's cargo, and one of hir hind hands had found Teasel's foot and stayed on it.

Hir ears came slowly forward. Hir asked the Core.

*One point zero two standard,* the Core said.

Hir asked it for the air.

*Breathable. Oxygen twenty-one percent. Water vapour high. Volatile organics: several hundred, unclassified.*

Hir asked it for the time.

*Six minutes forty seconds since theta release.*

Four other pairs of ears had come forward round hir. The others had asked theirs too, so the channel filled up with readings, all of them arriving at once and all of them intact.

*[One g. One g! We're on a surface,]* Haw sent.

*[Air's richer than ours,]* from Sloe. *[Twenty-one percent, and we pay for nineteen. Taste it. You can taste it]*

*[It smells,]* Teasel sent. *[It smells of everything]*

*[It's a planet, it's a planet, which planet, it's too heavy for a moon, is it a moon, is it one of ours?]* That was Haw again, stacking them.

*[Lifeboat,]* Sloe sent. *[Somebody put us in a lifeboat asleep and—]*

*[No boat,]* Yarrow sent.

*[A sim, then. It's a sim. It's a training sim and Ilex is laughing at us from—]* Haw.

*[Sims don't smell,]* Teasel sent.

*[Or we're dead ~ Haw, delighted by the idea for a second, then not]*

*[If this is dead I want a word with somebody,]* Sloe sent.

*[Heads,]* Madder sent, flat, into the middle of it. *[Hands]*

It went under the noise. Hir sent it again, and it went under again, like a rota posted on a hatch nobody used. And hir could feel the fire. It stood in a ring round the wagon and the heap, in the ferns on both sides of the old track they had woken on, pushing heat into hir face. There was no hatch to shut on it. It was out in the air, eating, and nothing was coming. Every ear in the heap was fanned out flat to the sides, and Haw let out a huff that shook.

Yarrow still had hir lower hand on the tailboard.

It was a wagon. They all knew the word once they saw it properly: a flat bed on four wheels, two shafts out the front, straps and a buckle. Something small and red was caught in the buckle, and a burnt sack hung off the back. Down its side, big and a little uneven, somebody had painted letters, and the heat had blistered the paint into bubbles.

Sloe read them first.

*[APPLE BLOOM]*

Then, while everyone was still getting it: *[That's our alphabet. That's our alphabet, we can read that]*

Somebody chirred, a small sound, surprised out of them.

*[Apple Bloom!]* Haw sent. *[Apple Bloom. It's a crate stencil. It's a produce brand. It's cider, I bet it's cider—]*

*[It's done by hand,]* Sloe sent. *[Look at the strokes. Somebody did this with a brush. The B's the wrong way round ~ fond of whoever it was, and surprised to be]*

*[So there are people,]* from Sloe, straight after. *[Somebody paints. Somebody sells paint]*

*[So it's Earth!]* Haw sent.

*[Earth's one g,]* Teasel sent.

*[So's half the colony list,]* Madder sent.

*[So is it humans, are there humans, where are they, did the humans put us—]* Haw, of course.

*[Unconfirmed]*

*[You always say that,]* Haw sent.

*[It's always true]*

Madder pulled hir tail round and got it under hir, and hir stood up on hir four lower hands with hir arms free, as hir had ten thousand times on a hull with a hand on the rail. There was no rail. Hir swayed and did not go over, and felt four people watching hir through the frame and deciding not to say so.

"Rota," hir said aloud.

It came out rough, with dirt in hir mouth, and the ferns gave nothing back. Hir said the rest slowly, every word put down on its own.

"Haw, point, inside ten metres of me. Sloe, left flank. Yarrow, rear. Teasel, with me. Watches change on the hour by the Core. We go downhill, on the burnt ground." Hir stopped, then added, "Now."

For a moment the channel was empty, which never happened with five of them. Speech, out loud, among Kin, with no human anywhere: somebody had got up on a chair.

*[~ a laugh, from Haw, stopped halfway out]*

*[Logged ~ very clean for Sloe]*

Haw went to point, came back to ask where point was, and went again. Teasel did not move until Madder put a hand on hir shoulder, and then did. Sloe crouched at the front of the wagon, one hip against Yarrow's leg, and worked the red thing out of the buckle: a strip of something soft, tied in a loop.

*[One wagon,]* Sloe sent. *[Two sacks, burnt through, empty. One mirror, no glass in it. One ribbon, red. Call it nothing, call it a day's water if we find a buyer. That's stores]* Hir put the ribbon over hir wrist and pulled it tight. *[~ laughing, not much]*

The mirror lay on its back in the wagon bed, a rim of dark metal round nothing, still too hot to touch. Yarrow got it out anyway, ears fanned wide, the burnt sack wrapped round both upper hands, and stood it on its edge against the nearest trunk. Then hir took hir hands off the metal rim and put one of them flat on the bark, and left it there.

*[Yarrow]*

*[Holds itself up,]* Yarrow sent. *[No guys. Nothing on it]*

Madder ran hir RADAR up the trunk. It went up and split and split again, with no line or strut anywhere, and it stood.

*[Leave the wagon,]* hir sent.

Yarrow did not leave the wagon. Hir lower left hand had opened a finger at a time, and it closed again on the tailboard, on purpose. Then hir went round to the front and took up the shafts.

*[It rolls,]* Yarrow sent.

Madder let hir have it.

Downhill to the left there was a strip where the fire had already been. Madder's eyes gave hir a black lane through the ferns; the near-field gave hir the same lane, cooler than everything round it. It ran away from the fire, which was moving uphill and right with the air. Hir had marked it as the exit before hir had finished standing up.

They moved off, and the channel went at once.

*[Is it always this heavy? Do you get used to it? Does anyone live like this?]* from Haw, already three metres past point.

*[Smoke, sap, something rotting, something sweet, and about twelve things I can't name,]* Sloe sent. *[Two of those I'd pay for. The rest I'd pay to have taken away]*

*[Did we do this,]* Teasel sent, clean, looking back up at the burning ring.

*[Came through,]* Yarrow sent, from between the shafts.

Nobody took that any further.

*[Where did the running ones go? Are they down there? Are we following them?]*

*[Point, Haw,]* Madder sent. *[Ten metres]*

They went down the strip.

---

The ash came up round them in a cloud with every step. They went as a clump, close enough to knock shoulders, Teasel's flank against Madder's, Sloe's tail across the back of Madder's leg, Haw's point shrinking to three metres and then two. Inside ten paces Madder came down onto six, with hir upper hands taking weight they had never been asked for. Behind hir the wagon's front left wheel squeaked on every turn, like a hinge.

Teasel sneezed.

Hir whole body jerked with it, and hir sat down in the ash. Five hearts went up and came down again through the frame.

*[What was that, what was THAT, do it again—]*

*[No,]* Teasel sent.

*[Are you hurt?]*

*[I'm fine,]* Teasel sent, and got up, and brushed hir front. Hir ears were flat. Madder could feel hir under the frame, working at something, keeping it down.

The clearing at the bottom of the strip was small, pale stems round the edge and thin grass in the middle. The fire had come this far and gone out; the grass was only singed. In the middle, on its side, lay the animal.

None of them had seen one anywhere but in a record. This one was the size of a hand, grey and brown and burnt black along the back, with a long tail flattened like a paddle. Its eyes were shut. Its feet were curled under it, with something like fingers.

Haw got there first and stopped so hard that Sloe walked into hir, and stayed there, chest to Haw's back. Somebody made a thin sound, a whine through the teeth.

Madder's ears came forward. Hir asked for small mammals. *Forty-one records,* the Core said. *No exact match.*

*[Is it one of them? The ones that ran?]*

*[Too small,]* Sloe sent.

*[Is it asleep?]*

Nobody answered that. Yarrow came up past the wagon and knelt, and turned the animal over with one finger. The underside of it was not burnt. It was pale and soft and the fur moved when Yarrow touched it, and nothing else did.

*[It's got a tail,]* Teasel sent.

*[It was here first,]* Sloe sent.

*[Did we kill it,]* Haw sent. There was nothing under it. Haw had never sent a clean line in hir life.

*[It was already—]* from Sloe, and it stopped.

*[Unconfirmed,]* Madder sent, and all five of them felt it be the wrong word. Yarrow sent nothing at all.

Haw put out one hand and laid it on the soft pale fur beside Yarrow's, and hir rider went out for a second, not stripped, just gone, as if there wasn't anything left to ride on it.

*[Moving,]* Madder sent.

Haw left the hand where it was a moment longer than that. Then hir took it back and went to point.

---

On the far side of the clearing the ground did not slope. It dropped, straight down between the trunks, with no track on it and nothing burnt, a different hill altogether. They came down it badly, Haw and Sloe on the shafts and Yarrow behind with all four lower hands on the tailboard, holding the wagon back while it tried to run them over. At the bottom was a hollow, cooler, full of a sound Madder had never heard: water running over stones, on and on, wider than any pipe. It came from up the slope to the right and went away to the left, and on its near bank there was a flat-topped stone the size of a table.

Haw was on the stone before anyone could send anything.

*[It's cold! It's wet on top. Everything here is wet. Sloe, come and—]*

Something on the stone moved.

It had been part of the stone. Madder's RADAR had it as a lump on the edge, and then it wasn't; it was a thing with legs that went off the stone in one long shove, past Haw's face, and hit the water with a loud slap.

The terror came out of Haw first and then out of everyone, and every one of them felt all five, so there was no floor under it anywhere. Haw came off the stone backwards and landed on Sloe, and both their tails whipped round each other and locked. Teasel screamed, a thin note that climbed out of hearing and went on. Madder found hirself flat against the bank with every limb dug in and hir tail wrapped twice round hir own hind legs, and could not have said when. Only Yarrow had not moved. Yarrow had hir hand on the wagon and hir tail coiled round a wheel, and was pressed so close against it that the wheel creaked.

The water went on running. Out in it, further off, something small went *slap* again.

Nobody sent anything for a long time. Five hearts went too fast and did not come down. Madder asked the Core for the time, and it told hir. It had been nine seconds. Hir asked it what the thing had been.

*Amphibian,* the Core said.

That helped nobody.

*[It was very small,]* Haw sent at last. It was meant for a joke and didn't make it.

Sloe got up from under Haw and went to Teasel, who was folded tight on the bank with hir tail wrapped round hirself and hir ears flat to hir skull, and put hir head down by Teasel's head and said something on the whistle. It was too high and too near for the frame to have. When Sloe lifted hir head, Teasel's ears had come up a little, and that was all Madder got.

*[Downstream,]* hir sent. *[Stay off the stones]*

---

They went down the stream all night.

It did not get lighter. Yarrow had the shafts again, and the five of them walked in a knot round the wagon, somebody's hand always on somebody's back. The fire was behind them and above them. Every time Madder looked back there was a smear of orange on the underside of the leaves, a little further round the hill to the right, and the smoke came down after them and lay along the water. Hir asked the Core for the time on the hour and changed the watch, and Sloe went to rear and Yarrow came up to the left with the wagon behind hir.

The talking came back in the first hour and went on a long while.

*[How long does night go, here? Hours? Days? Does it come back every time?]* from Haw.

*[Unconfirmed,]* Madder sent.

*[Do the lights come on? Is there a switch? Who runs it?]* Haw again.

*[Nine degrees down since the clearing,]* Teasel sent, and then, a second later, correcting it: *[Ten]*

The cold came up off the water and got into the pads of their hands. Above them the whole roof of leaves moved at once, and kept moving, with a long rushing sound that came and went.

*[What's moving the air?]* Teasel sent.

*[No fans,]* Yarrow sent.

*[No coats, no light, no food, and one ribbon,]* Sloe sent. *[If somebody offered to buy us I'd want to know what they'd done wrong]*

Something called on the far bank, a long note that fell away at the end. Every ear swivelled to it.

*[Is that one of them? Is it talking? Is it talking to us? Should we answer?]*

*[No,]* from Madder and Yarrow at once.

*[Forty years of rotas,]* Madder sent, *[and I never once put a night on one]*

Somewhere in the second hour hir put a lower hand down on something that bit. Hir lifted the hand and found three hooked burrs in the pad of it, brown, the size of peas. Hir asked, picking. *Seed heads,* the Core said. *Hooked, for carriage on fur.* They came off the pad and stuck to hir fingers, and off those and stuck to the others.

*[It's shipping itself on you,]* Sloe sent. *[Freight, no manifest, and it isn't paying]*

In the end hir walked on with them in hir hand and stopped trying.

In the third hour the trees came apart over the water for a stretch, and there was the moon.

It stood in the gap, white and nearly round, marked with grey, and laid a road of light down the stream. Somebody drew a sharp breath. All five stopped. Five pairs of ears came slowly forward together, and Madder asked, and the Core gave hir what it had: *Natural satellite. Reflected light.* Hir had a thousand moons in records, and hir had never seen one.

*[That's a moon,]* Haw sent. *[That's a moon, that's a real one, how far is it, is it always there, is it ours? Whose is it?]*

*[Something's lighting it,]* Sloe sent. *[So there's a sun. Round the far side of all this, there's a sun]*

*[It's bigger than the records,]* Teasel sent.

*[~ Yarrow, nothing but a long look]*

They stood in the cold and looked at it until the trees closed over it again.

In the fourth hour Haw sent, *[Is it second watch at home?]*

The frame went quiet. It had not occurred to Madder to wonder. Hir asked for the last watch, and it came up word for word: the web by the coolant bay, the lights down, hir own voice on the channel. *[Web in ten. Haw, off the rail.]* And Haw's answer under it, *[~ grumbling, going]*. It was all there, with the coolant trunk breathing along the wall.

Hir did not send any of it.

*[Unconfirmed,]* hir sent.

*[They'll be at the web,]* Sloe sent. *[Our end of it. Somebody'll be sleeping in our end]*

*[Somebody'll want our rota,]* Haw sent.

*[Nobody wants our rota,]* from Teasel, dry, and for a moment it was very like hir.

*[Dunlin would take the rigging,]* Sloe sent.

*[Dunlin can't rig,]* Yarrow sent.

*[Dunlin can rig!]* from Haw.

*[If the bay—]* Haw again.

*[If the ship—]* That was Sloe.

It came round to the reactor, as it had to, and went round it, and nobody said it. Madder felt the whole weight of it going through the frame between them, finding nowhere, and going on.

The halts got longer after that. At each one Yarrow set the shafts down and went and put hir hand flat on the nearest trunk, and stood. Then hir took the shafts up again. Nobody asked hir what hir was checking.

In the sixth hour Teasel sent, *[Is anyone else hungry?]*

It came clean, as everything from Teasel had since the wagon. Under it, through the frame, Teasel's hum held flat and tight as a cable at full load.

*[Stores are a ribbon,]* Sloe sent. *[I'm not giving you the ribbon]*

*[I'm fine. I only wondered]*

*[It all smells like food,]* Haw sent. *[And none of it is]*

The stream widened and got louder. The talk thinned to single threads, and then stopped.

The eighth hour had no talk in it. There was the stream, and the wagon, and the burrs in Madder's hand. There was Haw coming back from point every few minutes to put a hand on Sloe's shoulder and going away again, and Sloe's shoulder leaning into it each time. Teasel walked with hir tail hooked through Yarrow's. Behind them the orange had gone further round the hill.

---

The trees stopped.

First they were thin, then there was one, and after it open ground went down to the water in a long green slope. Grey light was coming up ahead of them, low and wide, on the side away from the fire.

Haw made a small chirr and did not seem to hear it.

A small bridge of planks went over the stream. On the far side the hill had a door in it, a real door, and windows, and grass on the roof as if the hill had only let a house in partway. Little wooden boxes stood on poles and walls and up in the trees round it, dozens of them, each with a round hole in the front. Off to one side stood a shed on legs, with a plank running up to it like a ramp.

Madder counted exits: the bridge, the path going away from the door, the stream, which hir did not want. Hir sent nothing. Five of them stood at the edge of the trees in the ash they had carried down on their fur, pressed shoulder to flank, and Yarrow had hir hands on the shafts, and Sloe had the ribbon on hir wrist.

At the top of the ramp, a small hatch opened.

A bird came out. It was brown, with a red comb. Madder's ears came forward, and hir asked. *Poultry,* the Core said. After the bird came another, and another, until there was a line of them coming down the ramp in the grey light, one behind the other, talking to themselves.

Then, one after another, every one of them stopped dead.
