# X01 — Chapter 2 (v2)
**POV:** Madder-4 · **Form:** Chapter

---

*[What's burning]* went out from four of them at once, and Madder was one of the four.

Haw was the fifth, and was sending something else over the top of it.

*[I was falling fast ~ still falling, thrilled with it]*

*[Your leg's on my back]*

*[Whose leg, which one, I've got six—]*

*[Why is it heavy]*

*[It's hot on the left. It's hot on the right. Madder, it's hot on—]*

*[Sloe, that's my arm you're lying on]*

*[I'm not lying on anything, something's lying on me ~ muffled, indignant, and under that frightened]*

They had gone down at the end of second watch in the web by the coolant bay, and they had come up in a heap, which was right, since a Cluster wakes the way it sleeps. Nothing else was right. A heap in the web drifted and turned over in its sleep, and whoever ended up on top got peeled off in the morning with a lot of complaint. This heap had settled. It had gone flat like a sack put down, with every body pressed hard into the one below it, and Madder was somewhere near the bottom with Haw's leg across Sloe's back and Sloe's weight across hir own ribs, and the ground under all of them was not a deck.

Hir put the checklist up. *Heads. Hands. Air. Exits.* Hir got as far as hands, since Yarrow's lower left was clamped on something hard beside hir face, when four other threads came in over the checklist and hir lost the place.

*[There's a box. There's a box on wheels, Yarrow's holding a box on wheels]*

*[Something ran]*

That one was Teasel's, and it came clean, with nothing under it at all.

Madder swivelled both ears downhill. There was running down there, small and fast and more than one of it, crashing through something that crackled, and a smell came up the slope after it, sharp and sour, like a pump seal going. It went away from them, on and on, until the fire covered it.

*[Where? What? How many? Is it coming back? Can I—]*

*[Two. Three? Small ~ counting, not sure of the count]*

*[Let it go,]* Madder sent, and Haw let it go, mostly.

Hir reached for the frame and it gave hir five before hir had finished asking. Sloe was hot and loud; Haw was louder; Yarrow was level as a strut; Teasel was held small and tight, as if hir had wrapped hirself round something to keep it off the channel. And hirself. Five, all there.

Then hir reached past them for the ship, for fourteen hundred others and the Hum that had been under every hour of hir hundred and forty years, and there was nothing in the sky.

All five of them reached at the same moment, so all five of them found it together. The fright went out through the frame and came back off four other bodies, doubled and doubled, before Madder could have said whose it had been first. Somebody made a noise through the nose, high and thin, like a line singing under load. It might have been hir.

*[Where's the bay]*

*[Where's anyone]*

*[If the Nest had five of us come off the table in one second—]*

*[Don't]*

*[I'm only saying what it looks like from there ~ and wishing hir wasn't]*

*[That's what it looks like when somebody—]* That was Teasel, clean, and it stopped halfway, which was worse than if hir had finished.

*[Unconfirmed,]* Madder sent.

Nobody accepted it. Nobody argued with it either. They lay in the heap with the fright going round and round between them, while the light on the underside of the leaves went yellow and then yellower.

---

Getting out of the heap took longer than it should have. When Sloe rolled off hir, Madder found hir own chest did not come up after the weight had gone; it stayed down, and hir had to lift it. Hir got hir four lower hands under hir and pushed. The ground was soft and gritty and full of thin threads that tore. Hir tail lay out behind hir like somebody else's cargo.

Hir asked the Core.

*One point zero two standard,* the Core said.

Hir asked it for the air.

*Breathable. Oxygen twenty-one percent. Water vapour high. Volatile organics: several hundred, unclassified.*

Hir asked it for the time.

*Six minutes forty seconds since theta release.*

The others had asked theirs too, so the channel filled up with readings, all of them arriving at once and all of them intact.

*[One g. One g! We're on a surface]*

*[Air's richer than ours. Taste it. You can taste it]*

*[It smells,]* Teasel sent. *[It smells of everything]*

*[It's a planet, it's a planet, which planet, it's too heavy for a moon, is it a moon, is it one of ours?]*

*[Lifeboat,]* Sloe sent. *[Somebody put us in a lifeboat asleep and—]*

*[No boat,]* Yarrow sent.

*[A sim, then. It's a sim. It's a training sim and Ilex is laughing at us from—]*

*[Sims don't smell]*

*[Or we're dead ~ delighted by the idea for a second, then not]*

*[If this is dead I want a word with somebody]*

*[Heads,]* Madder sent, flat, into the middle of it. *[Hands]*

It went under the noise. Hir sent it again, and it went under again. Hir could feel hir own procedure going past the others like a rota posted on a hatch nobody used, and hir could feel hir own heart, which was not slowing down, and hir could feel the fire, which was on both sides of them now, low and busy in the ferns, working its way up the slope to the right.

Yarrow still had hir lower hand on the box.

It was a wagon. They all knew the word once they saw it properly, a flat bed with a board at each end on four wheels, and hitched at the front with straps and a buckle. Something small and red was caught in the buckle, and a burnt sack hung off the back of the bed with its seams showing. Down its side, big and a little uneven, somebody had painted letters.

Sloe read them first, since Sloe read everything first.

*[APPLE BLOOM]*

Then, while everyone was still getting it: *[That's our alphabet. That's our alphabet, we can read that]*

*[Apple Bloom!]* Haw sent. *[Apple Bloom. It's a crate stencil. It's a produce brand. It's cider, I bet it's cider—]*

*[It's done by hand,]* Sloe sent. *[Look at the strokes. Somebody did this with a brush. The B's the wrong way round ~ fond of whoever it was, and surprised to be]*

*[So there are people]*

*[So it's Earth]*

*[Earth's one g,]* Teasel sent.

*[So's half the colony list]*

*[So is it humans, are there humans, where are they, did the humans put us—]*

*[Unconfirmed]*

*[You always say that]*

*[It's always true]*

Madder pulled hir tail round and got it under hir, and hir stood up on hir four lower hands with hir arms free, which was a thing hir had done ten thousand times on the outside of a hull with a hand on the rail. There was no rail. Hir swayed and did not go over. Nobody said anything about it, but Madder felt the flicker of four people watching hir through the frame and deciding not to.

Then hir did something hir had not done in forty years with a crew.

"Rota," hir said aloud.

It came out rough. There was dirt in hir mouth, and the ferns took the word and gave nothing back. Hir said the rest of it anyway, slowly, in speech, every word put down on its own.

"Haw, point, inside ten metres of me. Sloe, left flank. Yarrow, rear. Teasel, with me. Watches change on the hour by the Core. We go downhill, on the burnt ground." Hir stopped, then added, "Now."

For a moment the channel was empty, which never happened with five of them. Every one of them knew what a voice meant, used out loud among Kin, in a place with no human in it: somebody had got up on a chair.

*[~ a laugh, from Haw, stopped halfway out]*

*[Aye,]* Sloe sent, very clean for Sloe.

Haw went to point, and then came back to ask where point was, and went again. Teasel did not move until Madder put a hand on hir shoulder, and then did. Sloe crouched at the front of the wagon and worked the red thing out of the buckle with two fingers, and turned it over. It was a strip of something soft, tied in a loop with two ends hanging.

*[One wagon,]* Sloe sent. *[Two sacks, burnt through, empty. One frame. One ribbon, red. That's stores]* Hir put the ribbon over hir wrist and pulled it tight. *[~ laughing, not much]*

The frame was in the wagon bed, lying on its back, dark metal round nothing. There were a few splinters of something glassy in the corners, and it was still too hot to touch. Yarrow got it out anyway, with the burnt sack wrapped round both upper hands, and stood it up on its edge against the nearest trunk. Then hir took hir hands off the frame and put one of them flat on the bark, and left it there.

*[Yarrow]*

*[Holds itself up,]* Yarrow sent. *[No guys. Nothing on it]*

Madder looked up the trunk as far as hir RADAR would go. It went up and up and split and split again, with no line or strut anywhere to keep it standing, and it stood.

*[Leave the wagon,]* hir sent.

Yarrow did not leave the wagon. Hir lower left hand had opened a finger at a time once hir had noticed it, and it closed again on the tailboard, on purpose.

*[It rolls,]* Yarrow sent.

Madder let hir have it.

Downhill to the left there was a strip where the fire had already been. Madder's eyes gave hir a black lane through the ferns; the near-field gave hir the same lane, cooler than everything round it and nothing standing in it. It ran away from the fire and down the slope to where the ground opened out. The fire was moving the other way, uphill and right, along with the air. Hir had marked it as the exit before hir had finished standing up.

They went down it.

---

The ash was fine and deep, and it came up round them in a cloud with every step. Madder had given up on walking upright inside ten paces. Hir came down onto six, with hir arms taking weight hir arms had never been asked for. Behind hir the wagon squeaked on every turn of its front left wheel, a small dry sound like a hinge.

Teasel sneezed.

None of them had ever done it. It went off in hir face with no warning, and hir whole body jerked with it, so hir sat down in the ash. Five hearts went up and came down again through the frame.

*[What was that, what was THAT, do it again—]*

*[No,]* Teasel sent.

*[Are you hurt?]*

*[I'm fine,]* Teasel sent, and got up, and brushed hir front. Hir ears were flat. Madder could feel hir under the frame, working at something, keeping it down.

The clearing at the bottom of the strip was small. Pale stems stood round the edge of it and thin grass grew in the middle. The fire had come this far and gone out, since there was nothing here for it; the grass was only singed, and a few curls of smoke were coming up out of it. In the middle, on its side, lay the animal.

Madder had never seen one. None of them had. There were none on the ship, there never had been, and none of them had seen one anywhere but in a record, flat and small and far away. This one was the size of a hand. It had fur, grey and brown and burnt black along the back, and a long tail flattened like a paddle. Its eyes were shut. Its feet were curled up under it, very small, with something like fingers.

Haw got there first and stopped so hard that Sloe walked into hir.

*[Is it one of them? The ones that ran?]*

*[Too small,]* Sloe sent.

*[Is it asleep?]*

Nobody answered that. Yarrow came up past the wagon and knelt, and turned the animal over with one finger. The underside of it was not burnt. It was pale and soft and the fur moved when Yarrow touched it, and nothing else did.

*[It's got a tail,]* Teasel sent.

*[It was here first ~ quiet, and meaning more than that]*

Nobody took that up. Haw put out one hand and laid it on the soft pale fur beside Yarrow's, and hir rider went out for a second, not stripped, just gone, as if there wasn't anything left to ride on it.

*[Moving,]* Madder sent.

Haw left the hand where it was a moment longer than that. Then hir took it back and went to point.

---

Below the clearing the ground fell away steeply, and they came down it badly, with Yarrow holding the wagon back from the rear while it tried to run over hir. At the bottom there was a hollow, cooler, full of a sound Madder had never heard: water running over stones, going on and on and not stopping to take a breath. It was wider than any pipe Madder had ever stood by. It came from somewhere up the slope to the right and went away down the hollow to the left, and on its near bank there was a flat-topped stone the size of a table.

Haw was on the stone before anyone could send anything.

*[It's cold! It's wet on top. Everything here is wet. Sloe, come and—]*

Something on the stone moved.

It had been part of the stone. Madder's RADAR had it as a bump, a lump on the edge, and then it wasn't; it was a thing with legs that went off the stone in one long shove, past Haw's face, and hit the water with a loud slap.

The terror went through the frame all at once. It came out of Haw first and then out of everyone, and every one of them felt all five, so there was no floor under it anywhere. Haw came off the stone backwards and landed on Sloe. Teasel screamed, a thin note that climbed out of hearing and went on. Madder found hirself flat against the bank with every limb dug in and hir tail wrapped twice round hir own hind legs, and hir could not have said when hir had done any of it. Only Yarrow had not moved. Yarrow had hir hand on the wagon and was pressed so close against it that the wheel creaked.

The water went on running. Out in it, something small went *plip* again, further off.

Nobody sent anything for a long time. Madder listened to hir own heart and to four others inside hir, all going too fast and not coming down. Hir asked the Core for the time, and it told hir. It had been nine seconds.

*[It was very small,]* Haw sent at last. It was meant for a joke and didn't make it.

Sloe got up from under Haw and went to Teasel, who was sitting on the bank with hir arms round hir knees, and put hir head down by Teasel's head and said something on the whistle. It was too high and too near for the frame to have, and Madder did not ask for it. When Sloe lifted hir head, Teasel's ears had come up off hir skull a little, and that was all Madder got.

*[Downstream,]* hir sent. *[Stay off the stones]*

---

They went down the stream all night.

It did not get lighter. The fire was behind them and above them. Every time Madder looked back up the hollow there was a smear of orange on the underside of the leaves, a little further round the hill each time, and the smoke came down the slope after them and lay along the water. The frame kept all five of them close. The wagon squeaked. Hir asked the Core for the time on the hour and changed the watch, and Sloe went to rear and Yarrow came up to the left with the wagon behind hir, and nothing else changed at all.

Somewhere in the second hour hir put a lower hand down on something that bit. Hir lifted the hand and found three hooked burrs in the pad of it, brown, the size of peas, and hir picked at them while hir walked. They came away from the pad and stuck to hir fingers. Hir picked them off the fingers and they stuck to the other fingers. In the end hir walked on with them in hir hand and stopped trying.

The talking came back slowly, a thread at a time.

*[How long does night go, here?]*

*[Unconfirmed]*

*[Do the lights come on? Is there a switch?]*

*[There's one on the ceiling, look, off to the right. It's moving]*

*[That's not a light, that's—]*

*[Don't,]* Madder sent, and they didn't.

Then Haw sent, *[Is it second watch at home?]*

The frame went quiet. Madder had not wondered it, and so had not looked. Hir looked now, and the Core put the last watch up for hir entire and exact: the web by the coolant bay, the lights down, hir own voice on the channel. *[Web in ten. Haw, off the rail.]* And Haw's answer under it, *[~ grumbling, going]*. It was all there, word for word, with the coolant trunk breathing along the wall.

Hir did not send any of it. The ship's clock was the one thing hir could not ask for.

*[Unconfirmed,]* hir sent.

*[They'll be at the web,]* Sloe sent. *[Our end of it. Somebody'll be sleeping in our end]*

*[Somebody'll want our rota]*

*[Nobody wants our rota]*

*[Dunlin would take the rigging]*

*[Dunlin can't rig]*

*[Dunlin can rig]*

*[If the bay—]*

*[If the ship—]*

It came round to the reactor, as it had to, and went round it, and nobody said it. Madder felt the whole weight of it going through the frame between them, looking for somewhere to put itself down, and finding nowhere, and going on.

In the fourth hour Teasel sent, *[Is anyone else hungry?]*

It came clean. Everything from Teasel had come clean since the wagon. Madder could feel how much work it was taking hir, and so could the other three, and none of them could find what was under it.

*[Stores are a ribbon,]* Sloe sent. *[I'm not giving you the ribbon]*

*[I'm fine. I only wondered]*

*[It all smells like food,]* Haw sent. *[And none of it is]*

The stream widened and got louder. Yarrow put the wagon down on its wheels on a stretch of gravel and walked to the edge of the water and put hir hand flat on the next trunk, and stood. Then hir went back to the wagon. It happened three more times before morning, and nobody asked hir what hir was checking, or remarked on it.

The fifth hour had no talk in it at all. There was the stream, and the wagon, and the burrs in Madder's hand. There was Haw coming back from point every few minutes to put a hand on Sloe's shoulder and going away again, and Sloe's shoulder leaning into it each time without Sloe seeming to know. Behind them the orange had gone further round the hill.

---

The trees stopped.

First they were further apart, then they were thin, then there was one, and after it was open ground going down to the water in a long green slope. Grey light was coming up from somewhere to the left, low and wide.

A small bridge of planks went over the stream. On the far side of it the hill had a door in it, a real door, and windows, and grass growing on the roof as if the hill had only let a house in partway. There were little wooden boxes on poles and on the walls and up in the trees round it, dozens of them, each with a round hole in the front. Off to one side stood a shed on legs, with a plank running up to it like a ramp.

Madder counted exits. There was the bridge. There was the path going away from the door. There was the stream, which hir did not want. Hir sent nothing, and nobody sent anything back. Five of them stood at the edge of the trees in the ash they had carried all the way down on their fur, and Yarrow had hir hand on the wagon, and Sloe had the ribbon on hir wrist.

At the top of the ramp, a small hatch opened.

A bird came out. It was brown, with a red comb, and after it came another, and another, until there was a line of them coming down the ramp in the grey light, one behind the other, talking to themselves.

Then, one after another, every one of them stopped dead.
