## 1. The want
Rarity wants to finish the best work of her life and get it on **Thursday's eight o'clock train to Canterlot**: a gown for a client (the Countess Silverlace) who still hasn't paid for the last one, sewn with **thirty sapphires Spike polished**, which Rarity hasn't paid him for either. She is thrilled and frightened and lets neither show. **Under that:** her sister has been strange for two weeks, and Rarity has been too busy to ask properly, and knows it.

## 2. The shape
- **Tuesday, dusk, in the rain: the window.** Rarity is at the worktable in the boutique window with the gown on the form. Up the street from the mill end, in the rain, **the Kin go by, carrying Haw**: Yarrow with Haw across hir upper arms, the others close round, touching, going fast and badly on six in the mud. One of Haw's forelimbs hangs wrong, and there is **blood, very dark**, on Yarrow and on the street. Keep it plain: what she sees, no more. **Sweetie Belle is at the window beside her** and sees it too. Rarity sends her upstairs, and Sweetie goes without arguing, which is the strange thing.
- **Wednesday: the last day of work,** and the town full of it. Pieces, as her day comes to her:
  - customers with news;
  - **Twilight** stopping by, grey, with the pencil she has taken to carrying in her mouth: the hospital did what it could for a body it doesn't understand, and in the night they **took the hand, and the arm to the elbow**;
  - the Kin said **six years** for it to grow back, and **no pony can take that in** (Twilight tries to say it and can't make it sound like a fact);
  - **Spike's sapphires** on the bodice, one by one, and the thought of what she owes him;
  - the Countess's letter, which says nothing about money;
  - **Sweetie** not eating, and at school all day with the other two. Under it all, Sweetie comes home and says **Apple Bloom told a lie last night** (her wagon came home burned on a cart, and she said they'd left it by the creek and somebody took it), and then stops dead.
- **Wednesday night: the telling.** Late, the gown nearly done, one sleeve still to set. Sweetie comes down in the dark and **tells her everything**, plainly, in her own order (she runs on, then stops dead): the dare, the castle, the mirror, dragging it home on Apple Bloom's wagon, the mirror waking, the fire, the shapes, running, and two weeks of not saying. ***It's our fault.*** Not wailed, and not a speech. Rarity has to decide what to do with that tonight, with the train at eight.
- **Rarity's decision (imperfect).** She decides that Sweetie should not have to stand in a room of grown ponies and strangers and say it. **Rarity will say it herself, tonight, quietly, to the Kin at the hospital**, and then tell Applejack and Scootaloo's people properly herself **first thing in the morning, before anypony else can**. She'll be back to set the sleeve by dawn. It's kind and it's controlling, and it's Rarity. She takes Sweetie with her, because leaving her alone is worse, and does the talking.
- **The hospital, night.** Plain, and seen by a pony who notices workmanship. There is a small ward with one lamp. **Haw** is in the bed, dosed with more than the doctor was sure of, the stump bound. **The four others** are round hir in one knot, touching hir and each other, still in the way she has heard about and not seen. **Twilight** is asleep in a chair. There's a night nurse, and **the miller** (he helped carry Haw in from the bridge, and he hasn't gone home). Rarity says it. **The Kin's answer is not what she prepared for**:
  - **Madder** asks for it exactly (the day, the hour, the place, which object) with none of the blame she braced for, and none of the forgiveness either;
  - **Sloe** asks whether the children were hurt;
  - **Haw**, from the bed, laughs, a real laugh, at something no pony can see;
  - **Yarrow** says nothing, and looks at Sweetie.
  - Rarity can't read any of it. She has said it for Sweetie, and **Sweetie stood beside her and said nothing at all.**
- **The small hours: the sleeve.** Back at the boutique, she sets the last sleeve by lamplight, and it is **not as good as the other**: a quarter-inch off at the cap, and she can see it. She has no time to unpick it. She boxes the gown anyway. That is the other imperfect thing.
- **Ending: reversal.** Thursday, a little before seven, with the box on the counter for the eight o'clock. What she decided comes out differently than she meant: **the miller and the night nurse have had the whole night and a breakfast queue**, and the town knows before she's told anypony. **Applejack comes to the boutique door first**, soaked, having heard it at the market from somepony else, with the wagon lie now public and Apple Bloom's face behind it. **The line that ends it is Applejack's** (e.g. that Rarity told *them* before she told *her*), or **Sweetie's** (that Rarity said it *for* her), whichever the draft earns. Stop on it. No reply, no train, no resolution.

## 3. What is lost
The perfect sleeve; the chance for Sweetie to say it herself; the chance to tell Applejack first; the town's ignorance of the Crusaders' part; Haw's arm, for six years. The worst week.

## 4. Voice card
**Rarity** (VOICES.md) in the narration: precise, stylish, funny on purpose, warm with those she loves. **She notices workmanship, finish and money**, even here: the weave of a hospital blanket, how a bandage is wound, the miller's hat, the Countess's letter paper. The theatrics are for customers; alone and frightened she is plain and quick. **Every pony POV has a person in it** (VOICES): mutters, her reactions, thoughts of her own that have nothing to do with the Kin (Opal, the Countess, her own first commission, the train timetable, a hem she once got wrong). **Not** fainting, **not** "darling" as a personality (once to a customer at most), **not** measuring a Kin or sewing them anything, **not** melodrama.
**Sweetie Belle:** smarter than people give her credit for, more scared than she shows; runs on, then stops dead; asks the question everyone was avoiding. **Not** squeaky, not dim, no singing.
**Applejack** (the ending): plain, tired, honest in what she says; the loan never named. **Not** folksy.
**Twilight:** stretched and kind, pencil in her mouth, can't make *six years* sound like a fact.

## 5. The Kin, from outside (she can't hear the radio)
In the street: fast and bad on six in the mud, all touching, Haw carried. In the ward: one knot round the bed, hands on Haw and each other; they go still together; the pause before they answer; careful plain speech; first names. Haw's blood is **very dark**. **Pronouns:** Rarity starts with *it* in the street; by the ward, speaking to them, she chooses *she*, as a courtesy, without remark. **The bow** is on Teasel's wrist in the ward. **Rarity does not see it**, or sees a red ribbon on a wrist and thinks nothing of it; it's not hers to know (and Sweetie is looking at the floor).

## 6. Lore budget
Two mechanics, from outside and never explained: **the body** (dark blood; a hospital that doesn't know the dose; a limb that takes *six years*) and **the frame** (the knot round the bed, going still together). Nothing else.

## 7. Continuity (CONTINUITY.md governs)
Haw fell from the corner tree (an old ash just below the mill bridge) at about five on day eleven, Tuesday the 23rd, in the first rain. Yarrow was nearest. **The wagon was carted up to Sweet Apple Acres at four that day**, and Apple Bloom lied about it that evening. Spike polished thirty sapphires for Rarity's Canterlot commission, and she hasn't paid him. Twilight is the liaison. **The Crusaders' secret breaks in this chapter** (Sweetie to Rarity, then Rarity to the Kin). Calendar: Wednesday the 24th and Thursday the 25th. Units: miles, inches, pounds.

## 8. Details that demonstrate nothing (at least three)
1. Opal asleep in the box meant for the gown, and having to be lifted out of it twice.
2. The Countess's letter is on paper so heavy it won't fold flat, with nothing about money in it.
3. The miller's hat is on the ward chair, with rain dripping off its brim onto the floorboards.
4. Rarity's own first commission (a christening gown for a neighbour, when she was younger than Sweetie is now) and the hem she got wrong on it, which nobody else ever noticed.
