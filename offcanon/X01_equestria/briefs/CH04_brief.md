## 1. The want
Sloe wants five people fed every day, which is about 10,000 kcal of dense protein, in a town that eats flowers, with no money. She wants the arithmetic to close. **Under that:** she is *enjoying* this. A market is a market, ponies are people, and she is good at people. She is also more tired than she has ever been.

## 2. The shape
- **Day two (the duck house, then the town hall).** Sloe starts from what Fluttershy brought at four on day one: a basket of eggs (about nineteen) and a sack of sunflower seed meant for the birds. She makes that about 7,000 kcal against the five's 10,000, which is the first number of the chapter and the best it gets. Madder sends her to town on the radio (*[Sloe. Food status, by dusk]*), because she is the one who talks. **The walk into town costs her** (void-built, 60 kg, the ground heavy). The town has heard the Crusaders' story, garbled and monstrous (shapes, fire, teeth, a lot of legs), and half believes it: doors close as she passes, a foal is pulled indoors, and nopony says anything to her face. **The Mayor** is a person with a job: she has a problem that isn't her fault and a budget that won't cover it. She gives them the **old seed barn on the river road**, empty since the co-op moved, a long way from anything, and a **town chit** for "reasonable costs", paid at quarter-day. She does not shake hands.
- **Day three (five in the morning, then the market).** Sloe learns where the eggs go: the **bakery takes the egg-cart's round at five**. She is at the bakery's back door at a quarter to. **Pinkie** is on shift, flour to the elbows, and blunt at that hour: Sloe is in the way of the flour. She tells her the egg farmer's name and which days he comes, and she notices, without saying so, that Sloe hasn't eaten. There is one moment where a welcome party is plainly on her mind (she starts to ask something, looks at the order board, and doesn't). The reason is hers, and the chapter doesn't explain it. **Then the market:** eggs, cheese, dried beans and last autumn's nuts are what she can use, and there aren't enough of them. Sloe hunts them stall by stall and door by door (every pony with hens has too many eggs in spring), and **trades work for bits**: a day counting stock for the general store. **She prices her own day badly**: it's a third of what the storekeeper expected to pay, and the storekeeper takes it. **Applejack's stall** has eggs, nuts from the farm's store and dried beans. She sells to her as a customer: a fair price and a hard one, weighed to the gram, and the chit taken with a face (she needs cash, and the town pays late). Sloe likes her enormously. She starts a tab.
- **After the middle (day four), it gets worse, and it's her own doing.** Her buying has moved the market. The small sellers who were glad of her on day three have caught on, and **eggs have gone up**. Applejack puts her price up too, **to her face**, and tells her why, and she's right. **Sloe should have seen it coming**: on the ship this was the first mistake she learned not to make, and she made it anyway because she was tired and it was going well. The numbers don't close. She *was* good at this.
- **The Cluster, on the radio, throughout:** Madder's orders, with times; Haw's chatter from the barn (the swallows in the rafters, the height of the roof); Yarrow almost nothing, and working on the barn door; **Teasel** sends clean, and *"I'm fine. Is anyone else hungry?"*. Through the frame Sloe feels all four, hunger in all of them, and **something low and steady in Teasel with no cause she can find**. Don't explain it (it's ch. 8's).
- **Ending: document end.** Applejack's tab, in her handwriting, as a page: dated lines, items, prices, what the chit covers and what it doesn't, and the running total at the bottom. Stop on the total. **One quiet thing in it:** on day three the tab says *"the strange one (Sloe)"* and *it*; by day four's line Applejack has written ***she***. Nobody remarks on it (VOICES: ponies trend from *it* to *she*).

## 3. What is lost
The day-two price, the day-three feeling that it could work, and Sloe's pride in her trade.

## 4. Voice card
**Sloe-5** (VOICES.md) in the narration: warm, quick, a little too many words. **She counts everything**, and the counting is how her mind holds the world, as Fluttershy's animals were hers: prices, eggs, kcal, steps to town, who's in charge versus who decides. Pony idiom picked up within a day and used slightly wrong. She notices who is hungry. Humour: easy, social, and at herself first. She likes ponies. It's tiring. **Not** the diplomat who smooths everything over; **not** the wide-eyed alien astonished by money (she was a quartermaster: money is the one thing here she understands).
**Applejack:** plain, practical, tired this week. Honest in what she says, not in what she doesn't: she counts the cash twice and puts it away fast, and Sloe notices without knowing why (the loan is never named). **Not** folksy wisdom, **not** "sugarcube" or "y'all" in every line, **not** giving her farm work or anything for free.
**Pinkie:** a working pony at five a.m.: quick, funny, blunt, flour everywhere. **Not** random, **not** a party, **not** cupcakes, **not** "Pinkie sense".
**The Mayor:** tidy, careful, overworked, frightened and managing it. Not a villain.

## 5. Channels (VOICES.md "How Kin talk among Kin" governs; DOC-00D is canon)
- **To ponies: aloud**, careful, first names from the first day. Sloe's speech is the warmest of the five, and it's still a little too exact.
- **To the Cluster: radio**, *[words ~ rider]*, from anywhere in town (they're within the 12 km frame). Several threads at once when it's busy. Teasel's lines clean. The whistle not at all, or once.
- **The Core** for sums, in bare italics: prices into kcal, the walk's distance, the time. Sloe asks it all day long, and nobody remarks on it.
- **Body:** ears fanned in the sun and when working the Core, forward when consulting it; tail; her hand on her own wrist (the bow) when she's nervous. Involuntary sounds. **Alone in town, she misses touch**, and the first thing she does back at the barn is get into the heap.
- **Ponies call her *it*** (VOICES.md). Pinkie might not.

## 7. Continuity (CONTINUITY.md governs)

## 9. Numbers (verified; the Core has them, the page uses few)
- A pony egg is about the size of a hen's egg, about 72 kcal (USDA large egg, 50 g). **10,000 kcal is about 139 eggs a day.**
- Nineteen eggs plus about a kilo of sunflower seed is about 7,200 kcal.
- About 2.5 kg of hard cheese, or about 1.5 kg of walnuts, is 10,000 kcal.
- Prices in bits are invented; keep them consistent and plausible.
