## 1. The want
Yarrow wants to work: a real job, with hir hands, where the load is known and the knot holds. **Under that:** the rain. Hir has spent fifty years on the outside of a hull, and water has never fallen on hir from the sky. Now it does, and hir can't stop going out in it.

## 2. The shape
- **The rain comes (the night before, and first light).** It starts in the night, on the barn roof. Yarrow goes out in it, and goes out again, and Madder sends hir back in on the radio, and hir goes out again. Hir doesn't *say* anything about it. It's in hir body: fur taking the water, the cold, ears folding, the smell (the forest across the river after rain), water finding its way down hir back and along the tail. **Find it through the body; never announce it.** The others feel it through the frame, a steady bright thing in the quietest of them, and Haw can't leave it alone on the radio.
- **The job (a half-job).** Twilight found it in the first week, by asking. **The weather team's ground crew** keeps a line of **tethered clouds** along the forest edge, low over the trees, to soak the burn and hold the fire in (the Everfree's weather runs itself, so they can only work the border). The ground crew drives **ash stakes** into the bank and makes the tethers fast; the pegasi bring the clouds down to them. It's earth-pony work, paid by the day in bits (some of which goes to Sloe's tab). Yarrow is **only allowed to haul and drive stakes**, not to make the tethers fast. That's half a job. Hir hands are the best on the crew and hir body is wrong for the ground: sixty kilos on six limbs in mud under full gravity, slow on the bank, tired by noon. The crew talk about whether this is even their job (some think the Everfree should be left to burn, some think the captain wants it on his record).
- **An hour working well with Dash.** Dash is on the line, bringing clouds down to the stakes. She and Yarrow fall into a rhythm without much talk: she brings the cloud, hir takes the tether, and the loads go where they should. It's the best hour Yarrow has had on this planet. Dash says maybe ten words to hir, all about the work. Dash calls hir *she*, easily. The rest of the crew mostly say *it*.
- **After the middle: the tether.** Wind comes up off the forest in the rain. **A stake Yarrow drove pulls out of the wet bank** (hir drove it the way hir would have on a hull, and the ground is not a hull; it's the one thing hir misjudged), and a cloud starts off loose toward the fields, trailing its line. The procedure is three pegasi and ten minutes. **Dash does her trick** (a fast, tight manoeuvre only she can do; show it, don't name it) and has the cloud back over the line in about ten seconds. **The captain**, an older stallion who directs from the ground with a map board and hasn't flown on the job since winter, dresses her down **in front of everyone**: procedure, she could have torn it open over the town, and she's in the book already. He takes her off the line for the day. Nobody mentions the stake. **Yarrow sees all of it and has no idea what hir's seeing**: the one who fixed it is punished, the one who caused it is not, the others look away, and Dash's wings are tight to her sides. On the ship, the rigger who saved the load would be bought a drink. Hir goes and redrives the stake, deeper, and nobody watches hir do it.
- **The magic thread: hir hands on a cloud.** At some point in the day a tethered cloud is low enough, at its stake, for Yarrow to touch. Hir hand goes into it, wet and cold and with nothing to hold, but the tether on it is **taut**, pulling hard against the stake: a load with no mass to make it. Later a pegasus stands on a low cloud as if it were a deck. Yarrow tries (hir is a rigger; hir climbs things) and **goes straight through into the wet grass**. The crew laugh, not cruelly. That evening, or at the noon break, **the five argue it out on the radio**, several threads at once: engineers. What holds it up, what the tether is pulling against, why a pegasus stands and hir falls through, whether "the ponies do it and not the cloud" means anything, whether their own numbers are wrong or the world is. **Nobody says "magic"** (they have no such idea). It ends unresolved, and it is allowed to frighten them a little: every procedure they have assumes the numbers hold. Somebody (Madder) is quiet longer than usual.
- **The wagon (the Wagon thread).** In the afternoon two pegasi come out of the burn, low over the trees, carrying something between them on ropes, and set it down by the captain: **the wagon**, charred, one wheel gone, on its side, with the red letters blistered. The captain says, without much interest, that it's *"the Apple kid's"* and somebody should take it up to the farm. **Yarrow knows it at once.** Hir hand closes on nothing. Madder feels the spike through the frame and sends *[Yarrow?]*; Yarrow sends back *[Nothing]*, clean (a clean send is a move). **Yarrow tells nobody, on the radio or aloud.** The wagon goes off on a cart toward the farm. (The Kin do not yet connect "the Apple kid" with the small running bodies of the first night; Yarrow might, and keeps it.)
- **Haw, all day.** Haw has been climbing everything in town all week (roofs, the clock tower, the mill roof once): wonderful. Today hir comes out to the edge to watch the clouds and the pegasi, in the rain, delighted. Hir wants to *touch a cloud*. Madder's rule (ch. 4) is *nobody near the edge while they work, and that includes the tree*.
- **Ending: cut mid-action.** Late afternoon. Haw is up **the tallest tree at the forest edge**, high, in the rain, reaching toward a low cloud on its tether, sending pure joy on the radio. Void-built bodies misjudge height and fall under gravity; wet bark. Yarrow, on the bank below with a stake in hir hands, hears **the sound**: wood giving under a load. Hir is a rigger, and hir knows exactly what that sound means before anyone else does. **Stop on the sound, or on the instant of knowing.** No fall shown, no catch, no scream, no name called.

## 3. What is lost
Dash's day, and her standing with the captain, lost to fixing Yarrow's mistake; the one good hour; Yarrow's certainty that hir knows how a load behaves. And, at the cut, whatever the sound means.

## 4. Voice card
**Yarrow-3** (VOICES.md) in the narration: hir notices **how things are held up**: tension, weight, joints, knots, the grain of an ash stake, the pull on a line. Hir thinks in loads (the Core gives newtons and kilos when asked). Plain, physical, precise. Humour is silent and physical. **Hir sends almost nothing**, and the page is full of what the others send *at* hir, which hir receives and mostly doesn't answer. Wrong about: that being useful is the same as being all right. **Not** the gentle giant; **not** the silent one with hidden wisdom; no speech about rain or the hull.
**Rainbow Dash:** professional on the line, quick, dry, good at this, and she knows it. After the dressing-down she says nothing, and her wings say it. **Not** "awesome", not a race or challenge, not a speech to Yarrow about it, not the sceptic turned fan.
**The captain:** not a cartoon tyrant. He's right about procedure, and he's also not a flier any more. Both are true.
**The ground crew:** earth ponies with a job; one hums the same few bars all day.

## 5. Channels (VOICES.md "How Kin talk among Kin" governs; DOC-00D is canon)
Radio among the five, all day, from wherever they are (the barn and town are within the 12 km frame). Several threads at once in the argument about the cloud. Yarrow's sends are one or two words, clean. Speech aloud only to ponies, and Yarrow barely speaks aloud at all (a word to Dash, perhaps). The Core in bare italics, for loads and distances. Kin bodies throughout: ears (cold and wet flatten them), tail, touch at the barn, involuntary sounds. **Ponies are *it* or *she* by now** (VOICES): Dash says *she*, the crew mostly *it*, the captain *it*.

## 6. Lore budget
Native channels are not budgeted. The two *mechanics*: **the cloud** (a load with no mass, a deck for a pegasus and nothing for a Kin; never explained) and **void-built bodies under gravity** (mud, the stake misjudged, and the height Haw misjudges). Texture: the frame, the Core.

## 7. Continuity (CONTINUITY.md governs)
Week two, about day eleven. The seed barn, the river road, the forest edge across the river from the barn road (the burn is up the slope behind the edge). The weather team has worked the edge since day one; the captain rarely flies. **The radish stallion's rain did not come in week one; this is the first rain.** The wagon lay on its side on the steep slope below the scorched clearing, with a burned wheel broken and APPLE BLOOM blistered uppermost (CONTINUITY 10). Twilight has been liaison for a week (and got Yarrow this job). Teasel is thinner. The bow is on Teasel's wrist. Haw was warned off the mill roof (ch. 5). Units: Kin metric, ponies miles and pounds.
