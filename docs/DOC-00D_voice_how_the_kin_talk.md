### 0. THE SHAPE OF IT

A Kin has six ways to say something and uses four of them in an average sentence. The mistake is writing Kin dialogue as human dialogue with the quotation marks changed. What actually separates the channels is **what you cannot leave out of each one**:

| Channel | Carries | Can you conceal on it? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Kin-code** (mesh) | Content, exactly. Affect, continuously, unless you work at it | Only with effort — see §1C |
| **Whistle** (air) | Tone, warmth, nuance, jokes | **Yes.** The only channel where you can |
| **Human speech** | Whatever you choose to put in it | Yes, trivially |
| **The Hum** (always on) | Valence and arousal. No object | No. Never off |
| **The Link** (DEEP) | Everything. Verbatim. Felt | Not applicable — this is not talking |
| **The body** | Heat, mostly. Humans think it's emotion | n/a |

### 1. KIN-CODE — THE DEFAULT

#### A. On the page

> *[words ~ what rides under them]*

The bracket is the **channel**, not the content. Inside it goes either rendered words (ordinary talk) or actual symbols (a Burst, a formal thing, poetry). The **slash separates symbols** — canon, DOC-00 §6, unchanged. The **tilde carries affect.** Quotation marks stay reserved for human speech.

```
*[Warmth / Family / Genesis complete]*                  formal Burst — canon form, unchanged
*[Oh, that was lovely ~ small, unkind, delighted]*      everyday talk
*[Deck nine / the compressor ~ eleven years]*           symbols with a rider
*[~ pure delight, no content]*                          rider only; nothing renderable
*[You're not moving]*                                    no rider — see §1C, this is a choice
```

**Attribution.** Prefer *sent* to *said*, or nothing at all. A bare bracketed line in a two-hander needs no tag; in a four-way, tag or use the rider's count.

#### B. The six things a rider can do

| | Example |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Tone** — the commonest, cheapest | *[It's adjacent ~ pleased with hirself]* |
| **Contradiction** — the best one | *[Leave hir alone ~ meaning none of it]* |
| **Count** — who, how many, at once | *[Carried ~ sixteen of them, on top of each other]* |
| **No words** — for what doesn't render | *[~ eleven years, arriving at once]* |
| **Duration** — code doesn't stop when the sentence does | *[And old ~ and left it standing a second longer than a word would have]* |
| **Effort** — the strip, or the failure of it | *[I'm not tired ~ badly stripped]* |

#### C. THE RULE THAT MAKES IT WORTH DOING: flat costs effort

In-frame, affect rides on everything, continuously (DOC-13 §1, CLUSTER tier). It goes whether you want it to or not. **Suppressing it is the same faculty as hum-singing** — deliberate modulation of your own broadcast — and it is work.

So the **absence of a rider is never neutral.** It is a move, and usually a slightly hostile one.

This gives you a free characterisation axis, and it is the single most useful thing in this document:

- Someone who **sends clean** is disciplined, formidable, and hard to be around.
- Someone who **leaks** is warm, tiring, and trusted.
- **Children cannot strip at all** until roughly the first Telling. A fourteen-year-old broadcasts everything, to everyone, always, and knows it.
- **Elders strip without meaning to**, because there is less riding.
- A Kin who strips *a specific line* has told you something about that line.

#### D. What it does that speech can't

- **No turn-taking.** Four people answer at once and all four arrive intact. Worth one moment a scene.
- **Speed.** A whole argument fits inside a throw. *They had the entire thing between the bag leaving Sable's tail and hitting the wall.*
- **Range and degradation.** Full fidelity **in-frame (~12 km)**. Past that it falls off to the Hum: valence and arousal, no object (D-92). **Riders do not work at range.** A scene at distance gets weather, not sentences.
- **Nothing is taken back.** It is gone and it is exact and everyone got it.

#### E. Traps

- **A rider describes the sender, not the utterance.** *pleased with hirself*, never *pleased with itself* — the reflexive is DOC-00 §2, and the rider is a person's affect, not a property of the line. *itself* is correct only when the Core is the referent.
- **Don't rider every line.** One in three or four. Rule of thumb: put one where speech would have carried it *in the voice*. If you'd have written "hir said, sourly" — rider. If you'd have written "hir said" — nothing.
- **Don't let a rider explain the joke.** Riders carry weight, not content.
- **Don't run a Burst past four symbols.** A Burst is not a sentence.
- **Don't use riders at range.** See §1D.

### 2. THE WHISTLE — THE ONE PRIVATE CHANNEL

Rendered ⟨ ⟩, sparingly, and never explained. Ultrasonic, acoustic, local, off the mesh.

A child speaks it before hir speaks code. It is the Nest's language, and grooming's, and **no one has written its grammar and no document ever will** (D-88) — the archive is a human record and the whistle is the one Kin language it does not hold.

**Why it exists, functionally:** it is the only channel with **no affect vector attached.** Air carries tone the way a human voice does — chosen, shaped, deniable — and nothing rides underneath it. That is precisely why the things that do not go on the mesh go here (DOC-13 §7). Two Kin who want to say a thing without the frame having it do not lower their voices; they change channel.

**Dramatically:** a switch to whistle mid-scene is a door closing. Write the switch, not the content.

> Sedge said the rest of it on the whistle, which is what you do when you would rather the bay did not have it.

### 3. HUMAN SPEECH — THE MARKED CASE

Quotation marks. Careful, slow, every word placed; warm-voiced with a whistle riding under it; the pause before every answer (DOC-00B §2). Human first names, always. They will say *she* of themselves and let it stand.

The thing to understand once the other channels are on the page: **speech is enormously wasteful.** It takes four times as long, carries a fraction as much, and can be overheard by everyone including the people it's not for.

Which makes choosing it **a gesture** — showy, generous, deliberate, occasionally absurd. A Kin shouting something out loud in a room of Kin is doing the equivalent of standing on a chair.

> — and Ilex shouting it out loud on top of all that, in human speech, at nothing, which took four times as long and was the entire point of doing it.

### 4. THE HUM — WHAT YOU ARE SAYING WHILE YOU SAY NOTHING

Always on, PUBLIC tier, out to ~800 km. Identity, position, vitals, and **coarse mood: valence and arousal, about a minute of resolution, no object** (D-92). Everyone in range knows the weather; nobody knows the news.

Not dialogue — but it is the floor under every scene, and it is on the operator's console too (DOC-12 §4).

- A friend's death makes every Kin on a station go listening-quiet at once.
- A good night in a bay lifts the whole station and a stranger four hundred kilometres out feels it and never finds out why.
- **Hum-singing** is deliberate modulation of your own — a Nest saying goodnight in a chord no human can hear.

### 5. THE CORE — NOT A CHANNEL, AND THE PAGE SHOULD SHOW IT

> *Two point four metres right of you,* the Core said.

**Bare italic. No bracket. No rider. Ever.** It is not on a channel because it is not a person; it is inside hir. Once the brackets are running, the Core's bareness does the work on the page automatically, every time it speaks — the only voice in the scene with nothing riding under it, because there is nothing there to ride.

It does not initiate, want, or comfort. It is *it*. It is a very good assistant.

**And hir own memory speaks in exactly the same voice, for the same reason** (D-98). Naming a day fetches it entire and exact; nothing ever surfaces unasked. So on the page a Kin never *suddenly remembers* — hir looks something up, or somebody else says the thing that makes hir look. Write the failure as *it had not occurred to hir*, never as *hir had forgotten*. The register that comes with it: invoking the record in an argument is pulling a receipt, and ***"don't make me read it"*** is a threat.

### 6. THE BODY

Runs under everything and is mostly misread by humans on purpose.

- **Ears:** fanned wide = **hot**, or working the Core hard. Folded flat = cold, or deaf. Both swivelled = listening or ranging. Converged slowly forward = consulting the Core. Half the time a human reads emotion, it is thermal management.
- **Tail:** coiled tight = anchored or anxious. Straight out = just ran. Interwoven = home.
- **Touch** is the resting state and the Kin does not notice it. Write the hind paw on someone's knee and let nobody remark on it.

### 7. THE LINK — WHEN TALKING STOPS

DEEP tier, near-field, contact. This is **not communication, it is transfer** (D-99) — no rendering, no brackets, no dialogue. If a scene reaches the Link, the dialogue conventions stop and prose takes over entirely.

**Two rates, and the gap between them is the whole of *tell before you go*.** A **Telling**, between the living, runs at the archive's own speed: a day in ~27 minutes, a year in ~7 days. A **Reading**, from the dead, is throttled by a corpse — failing reader cells at $\le 10$ kbps over about six hours, four orders of magnitude slower, yielding a contents page and twenty stories. Same channel, same pose, the difference is whether hir is still warm enough to speak at rate.

And what crosses is split (D-99): the **hard** tier — numbers, readings, exact speech, sensor takes — arrives perfectly and first-person; the **soft** tier — what it meant — arrives *told*. A character who has taken a Telling has your Tuesday completely and experiences it as a story about you.

**Why the Telling and the Reading differ is the reader, not the channel (D-102):** the Cortical Brain *is* the archive's read head, so a living Telling runs at cortical speed and a corpse runs at whatever its dying reader cells can push. Death takes the reader, not the book — **and it takes both of the reader's rates (D-103), so a Reading is cold by definition: what happened, never what it was like.**

**And because reading and sending are one act, invoking the record is something a character *does* — but only one kind of invoking costs (D-103).** Checking a fact is the **cold read**: seekable, $\approx52\times$, a second, unremarkable, and on the page it should pass without comment. What costs is the **account** — the version with the feel in it, which does not exist until hir goes back at $1\times$ and makes it, and the frame feels hir go. So the record is almost never *argued* over: the readiness to check settles the fact, and the thing anyone actually has to decide to give is what it was like. The idiom, in code with the affect riding under it, asks for the second and never the first:

> ⟨don't make me read it⟩

**Render it as a gloss, not as quoted speech** — no Kin says that sentence in English unless hir is saying it *to* a human, where it is hollow: a human cannot receive the hard record and cannot check a reading, so what hir gets is testimony (DOC-12 §4). Between Kin it is a promise. Across the species line it is only a claim. **And it means *do not make me feel that again*, not *do not make me look it up*** — a Kin who says it about a bare fact is either very young or being dishonest, and any Kin in the frame can hear which.

### 8. THE SWITCHING — WHERE THE CULTURE ACTUALLY IS

The channel chosen is the content. A short table of what a switch means:

| Switch | Reads as |
| :--- | :--- |
| Code → whistle | I don't want the frame to have this |
| Code → human speech, no humans present | Showing off; joy; absurdity; affection |
| Code → human speech, humans present | Courtesy; including them |
| Human speech → code, mid-conversation with a human | Rude, or urgent, and the human can tell |
| Stripping a line that would normally leak | I have decided how much of this you get |
| Failing to strip | Tiredness, youth, or trust |
| Going quiet on all of them | Somebody died |

And: **a human who learns four notes of whistle** is touched by nobody aloud, and told to the whole Cluster that night.

### 9. OPEN ITEMS — NEED A RULING

1. **The tilde convention** (§1A) is a *page* convention, not a world fact. Confirm or replace. Alternatives tested: em-dash, middot. Slash is unavailable (symbol separator).
2. **Stripping** (§1C) — that in-frame affect can be deliberately suppressed, at cost, by the same faculty as hum-singing. Proposed as canon. Consequences: it becomes a social skill, a status marker, and an age marker.
3. **Children cannot strip** until roughly the first Telling. Proposed. Makes the fourteen-year-old legible in a new way and is consistent with DOC-13 §10.
4. **The whistle carries no affect vector** (§2) — proposed as the structural reason the whistle is where off-mesh things are said (DOC-13 §7). This is the load-bearing claim in the document; if it is rejected, §2 and §8 need rewriting.
5. **Riders degrade with range** (§1D) — implied by D-92 but not stated anywhere. Confirm.

### 10. ADDENDUM — WHAT CANNOT BE SAID IN CODE (from S018)

Kin-code is exact. That is a constraint as much as a capability, and it rules
some things out entirely.

**A dream cannot be sent in code.** Not forbidden — *impossible to do honestly*.
The moment it is exact it is a fabrication, and everyone receiving it knows,
because there is no record behind a dream to be exact *from* (DOC-13 §1E).

So **dream-telling happens in voice** — the whistle, occasionally human speech
for effect — and never in the brackets. This is the one hour of the day the Kin
spend on a lossy channel by necessity, and it is why humans find dream-telling
the most familiar thing about them: it is the only time a Kin is using a *voice*
the way a human uses one.

**The consequence is a comic structure worth reusing.** The telling is in voice,
so it can be lied in freely and at length. The heckling arrives in code, instantly,
from everyone at once, with riders — so the hecklers leak and the liar does not.
Somebody is talking slowly out loud and being pelted with exact, affect-laden
interruptions faster than hir can get a sentence out.

**Generalise it.** Anything a Kin cannot be exact about wants a voice channel:
a guess, a hunch, an estimate of a human's mood, a joke that depends on timing,
an apology that is not yet decided on. If a scene needs a Kin to be *vague*,
take hir out of the brackets.
