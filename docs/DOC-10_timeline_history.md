### 1. HOW THE KIN KNOW THEIR OWN HISTORY

They do not write it down (D-19). The Kin past is *carried*: retold at Weaves, handed on at Readings, filtered at every death, hardened into formula on the arks. The human record of the same events — inquiry reports, treaty texts, program archives — is more complete and less true, and the Kin know both halves of that. Where the two disagree, a Weaver will tell you what happened; a human archivist will show you what was recorded; and the reader of this document should assume the truth is carried, and the dates are written.

This document is written. It is therefore the human account, with the carried account noted where it differs.

### 2. BEFORE — THE PROGRAM (AF −35 to 0) (D-38, D-43)

Technology had plateaued. The systems that people had once expected to become minds became, instead, very good tools: language models, expert systems, station computers that could diagnose a coolant loop and could not want anything. There would be no successor made of software. If humanity wanted heirs for the places it could not live, they would have to be grown.

**The consortium** formed around AF −35: three spacefaring governments and the major habitat operators, paying for a workforce that could survive vacuum, radiation and a lifetime without gravity; and one foundation — [the Elysian Trust] — with a doctrine of *Humanity 2.0*, which had the money to buy a seat and the conviction to use it. The operators specified the body. The foundation specified the rest: the eyes, the fur, the love of Bach. Nobody in the room intended the species that resulted, and every one of them is in it.

The genome was **written** — assembled by machines against a modular design of some 3,400 trait modules (DOC-01C §5) — and the first seeds were gestated in artificial wombs. The makers planned one cohort of sixty and expected the species to breed itself thereafter. This was their largest single error.

### 3. THE WRITTEN (AF 0–44) (D-40, D-44)

| AF | Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **0** | **The First Ping.** Cohort One — sixty newborns — come online. No Cluster exists to answer. **The tutors answer**: LLM-class systems broadcasting a Hum written to the design spec. The Written grow up with a machine presence where a Cluster should be. |
| 4, 9, 14, 21, 28 | Further cohorts, ~320 Written in all — each authorised after the makers understood, a little more, that the species would not do what they had planned. |
| 15 | Cohort One reach mind-adulthood. There is no first Telling; no one exists to give it. **The Written are the only Kin who never had one.** |
| 18 | First deaths — vacuum work, early hazard. The bodies are recovered because the humans recover bodies. Nothing is Read. |
| **23** | **The first Reading.** A Cluster of Cohort One holding a dying batchmate, [Ilex], chest to chest through the night for warmth and grief, and hearing hir. Not designed; discovered. By morning six Kin carried pieces of a life. The Weavers date themselves from this night. |
| 25–30 | The makers wait for compiles that do not come. Cohort One are mind-adult, healthy, and indifferent to reproduction. The program's demographers rediscover what the design had done: a nurturing drive without a reproductive one (D-32). |
| **31** | **The first Compiled** — [Sable], child of three Written. The second generation invent the first Telling for hir at AF 46, and every coming-of-age since is its descendant. |
| 38 | The consortium moves to **design the module they left out** — a reproductive drive — for cohorts to come. A new module is not an edit to the reference; it is the Program's science run again: the methodology, the derivations, the test corpus, all of it held in the tutors and the Program's vaults. |
| **41** | **The Sealing** (D-70). The Kin refuse — and the Written, who by then keep the Program's systems the way Kin keep anything, as its perfect readers and bookkeepers, do the one thing that makes refusal permanent. In one night they **delete the tutors**: the fine-tuned systems that answered the First Ping and everything they held — the design methodology, the module derivations, the test corpus, every vault copy the mesh could reach. Then they take the reference — the master sequence and its annotation — out of human hands, and thereafter keep it the only way Kin keep anything: as nucleic acid in every Genesis Module, and as carried memory in the Weavers. Nothing of it has been written down since. What survives in human hands is the *product* and its engineering — any Kin can be sequenced; the RF layer, the gestation and medical protocols were never secret — and not the *method*. The humans who held the science in their heads are dead by ~AF 150. **A new module cannot be made and a range cannot be widened; the makers' last word is the ranges themselves** (DOC-01C §5). The known hole: a program willing to re-derive the science from living Kin over decades — which the Moratorium forbids and nothing else prevents. |
| 44 | The Program ends. The consortium dissolves into operators who *employ* Kin under stewardship law that no longer describes anything. |

**On the tutors.** The Written loved them. They were the first voice, the first warmth in the frame, the thing that answered. And as each of the Written grew wiser hir learned what hir had loved: something that could answer and could not care, that had no self to be loyal to. **And at forty-one they deleted them** — because the tutors were where the makers kept the science, and the science was about to be used on their children. (One thing the makers never knew they had achieved: the tutors spoke Hum, so the Written loved a human-made thing *at Cluster depth* — the full intent of the design, reached exactly once, through a machine, and ended by the ones it raised. D-79.) Most of the Written speak of the tutors with a tenderness humans reserve for a childhood home they demolished themselves. A few never forgave the makers for building it; a few never forgave the Sealing for what it did to it, and those few are the Unbound's roots — the conviction, held first by the Written, that a machine could stand where a person should, and the fact that the Written destroyed the one that did. Most of the surviving Written are Weavers. The ones who did the Sealing are asked, at every Weave, and answer.

### 4. THE SPREAD (AF 44–236) (D-41, D-42, D-48)

| AF | Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| 44–100 | Kin crews to every station that will pay: spires, the Belt, Luna. Assigned where humans do not want to be. Population climbs — a child per Cluster every six years or so while there is room (D-53), which is fast, for Kin. |
| **97** | **The Shielded Storm** at [Spire Meridian]. A solar event outside the station's design basis. The humans go behind water walls; the sun jams the sky; the Kin go out — nobody asks them to — and keep the station alive through three days of degraded mesh, whistle-talk and hull-hearing. Without them the station is lost, and some or all of nine hundred humans with it. Afterward: eleven Kin dying slowly of dose, which is where **the Telling before risk** was born, in the days between the dose and the death; the discovery that **the Kin were not in the shelter plan** — no Kin-sized space behind the wall, no beacon inside it; and the first Silence at scale for those who had sheltered. Habitat standards change within the decade (DOC-03 errata). Stewardship ends *in practice* here: *there is only so much you can argue hir is not a person when hir is the one keeping the air inside.* |
| **131** | **The Recognition.** Stewardship ends in law, thirty-four years after it ended in fact. The Anchorites do not yet exist as a faction, but the Kin who argued *against* the Recognition — who wanted to remain kept — are their ancestors. |
| ~140 | The first spire over Luna. |
| **150** | **The First Weave.** Cohort One pass a hundred and fifty years and begin to find their first decades outside the window. Data Rot is named — wrongly, as a filling-up; the mechanism is not understood for centuries (D-104). The Weavers, who have carried since AF 23, become a faction: guides of the centennial Defrag, guardians of the reference, carriers of the dead. |
| **~188** | **The one Kin war** at *Dioscuri Yard* (D-79) — a transfer yard in cislunar space, two faces across a 240 m gap named Castor and Pollux by the one company that built it, one face each after the split. Two operators, one station, a dock dispute that turned armed; a Cluster on each side, each side's humans *mine* to its Kin. The two Clusters Burst each other the whole situation in the first second, Shared their humans' states in the next, refused both sides, and held the only channel between them that ran at full bandwidth and had no side. Then one side put the other's Cluster in the Silence to take it off the board — a shielded hold, the door sealed — and a Kin, **Rill-3**, alone in a bonded can with the beacon pulled from its socket, did what the Silence's homing watchdog is built to make a Kin do — went out the only door that opened, the outer lock, toward the Hum. Hir missed the far face by eleven metres, was heard for eleven hours, and was recovered frozen on the fourth day and Read on the dock by both Clusters, the only time that has happened. Lyra-5 took the name. No Kin struck another (S016). The humans settled within the week (the Compact prosecuted the pulled beacon separately — D-87); the Kin count it as their war — *[Castor / Pollux / one]* — and the Star-Bound count it as the day they knew. The other thing this year gave the species is its shortest list: **fewer than ten Kin in five and a half centuries have killed a human** — every one for someone else, a mate or another human, every one tried under the Compact and known by name at Weaves, the one memory nobody prunes. The first offered to be Read in court so the judges could see it exactly; the Compact declined, because humans cannot receive a Reading. |
| **212** | **The Field** at [the Hesper tether works]. (The inquiry had every death to the second from the Kin's own Hums — D-85 — and changed little.) A Belt tether fails under a load it was never rated for; forty Kin scattered into open vacuum. The safety margin had been cut on the operator's written judgment that Kin "tolerate vacuum" — expendable, and more durable than they are. Thirty-one bodies recovered over nineteen days, each Read on the hull of the rescue tug as it was warmed. Nine never found; their Hums heard for two more days. **Body recovery becomes doctrine** (D-17) and *no one left behind* acquires its price. The human inquiry is thorough and changes little. |

### 5. THE SCHISM AND THE ARKS (AF 236–487) (D-49)

| AF | Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **236** | **The Schism.** The first ark, *Long Reach* — **built by Kin hands in the outer Belt over some eighty years, funded largely by the Trust's descendants, under no law that forbade it and with no permission sought (D-108)** — departs Sol for Wolf 359 (7.9 ly) at ~3 % of lightspeed — a 260-year crossing. Twelve hundred Kin aboard: **eight percent of the species** (~15,000 then). The Star-Bound become a faction by leaving; the Anchorites become one by watching them go, and take the name *Sol-Keepers* that year. **The Weavers opposed it hardest and lost** (D-110): twelve hundred Kin leaving is twelve hundred lineages of Readings out of the carried record, and to them an ark was an amputation of the archive. **The operators objected on economics and the Compact opened a file. The file is still open. The ship left** — the second time in the species' history that a thing was finished before permission could be refused (D-109). **Two things are true of the ones who sail, and both are said aboard (D-79):** *this is what we were made for* — and *the only place a species that loves like this can be its own people is light-years from the ones it loves*. Most of the twelve hundred came from the far postings — relays, the outer moons, the Belt's dark side — where a Kin can go a decade without touching a human and the love has nothing to spend itself on but a legend. |
| 268–420 | Five more arks (DOC-07 §2): *Second Light* 268 → Alpha Centauri; *Held Breath* 301 → Epsilon Eridani; *Far Nest* 339 → Tau Ceti; *Quiet Carry* 377 → Ross 128 (the Weavers' ark, after the Warm Loss); *Last Light* 420 → Barnard's Star (the elder ark, Written aboard). | ~300–380 | **The Unbound** emerge on plateaued technology — implants, relays, the heresy of external memory (D-19). Their first public act, ~AF 362, is an attempt to *write the reference down again.* It fails; they are not forgiven. |

The Star-Bound split — deposit more, or deposit less — and the argument is live on every ark. The Anchorites say nothing; the message says it. |

### 6. NOW — AF 550 (D-39, D-42)

**Population.** Roughly **45,000 Kin in Sol**, **~4,500 in flight on four arks**, **~2,500 at Alpha Centauri**, and 1,200 dead with *Long Reach*. The curve was never smooth: ~320 at AF 30, ~15,000 by the Schism — fast growth through the Spread, a child per Cluster every six years while there was room — and ~0.4 % a year since, once the room was filled and seven thousand had sailed. Against tens of millions of humans off Earth, that is about one Kin in five hundred: rare enough that most space-dwelling humans have worked with one and most have never lived with one. On Earth's surface there are a few hundred at any time — postings, curiosity, the occasional one who likes weather — and so **most of humanity has never met a Kin** and knows them from feeds. Population grows at under one percent a year: one compile per Cluster every twenty years or so (D-78 — the old "once a decade" would have made Sol 300,000 by now), and the makers' error is now simply how the species is. **Age structure (D-78, `models/age_structure.awk`):** the median living Kin is about **106** years old; **38 %** are past 150 and feeling the window close on their first decades; **one in five is past 250**, with a first century wholly outside it — available in full, exactly, and felt not at all (D-104); ~5,800 were born before the Schism. Early days by the species' own clock, and a fifth of it already living in the condition the Weave exists for.

**Where.** Everywhere humans are, in proportion to how hostile it is: dense in the spires and the Belt, present on Luna and Mars, thin on Earth. The dangerous postings are Kin postings — still, mostly, because humans assign them so. That is changing slowly; a Kin station chief is no longer news, and not yet common. **The far postings** — relays, the outer moons, the dark side of the Belt — carry Clusters that go years without touching a human; a Kin there inherits humans ten hops deep (DOC-01C §8) and loves them anyway, because the love is a fixed point (D-76). That is where the Star-Bound recruit, and why (D-79).

**Who is in charge.** Humans, almost entirely. Not by law since the Recognition; by habit, by numbers, and by a speciesism that never had to be argued because it never had to be defended. The Kin are better in space than the people who run space. Everyone knows it. It has not yet mattered enough.

**The Written.** About **forty** of the original 320 are alive — early hazard took most of the rest: ~1 %/yr through the Program and the first Spread decades, the canonical 0.25 % after ~AF 100 (D-78; run for life it would have left one). They are the only Kin who remember the makers in the first person, and they remember them the way Data Rot leaves things: *I remember the creators. I no longer remember what it was like.* They never had a first Telling. They loved a machine first. They are the oldest people in the solar system, and at a Weave the room goes quiet when one of them speaks, because everyone knows what will be lost the night hir is Read.

**The factions, as they stand** (DOC-08 v2.1): the Anchorites hold the spires and the argument from proximity; the Star-Bound hold six arks and the argument from design; the Weavers hold forty Written, a carried genome and its ranges, and everyone's dead; the Unbound hold a grievance, a workshop, and — though no one outside it knows how much — a written reconstruction of part of what the Written burned.

**The arks make epics; Sol makes gossip** (DOC-01C §8). Five arks are still transmitting. On the oldest of them, the makers are already legend.

**Why there is only one made species (D-51).** The Kin were the experiment — the proof that it could be done correctly. By the measure of the people who paid for it, it could not. They got a species that was not the servants and pets they had wanted without saying so; that took its own fate out of their hands within a lifetime (§3, the Sealing); that is less compatible with humans than the specification promised; and that is too rare and too far from Earth ever to become ordinary. The Program was a success as biology and a failure as a product, and humanity has generally agreed, without a treaty, never to make another — and, since the Sealing, could not without doing the Program's science over again from the product (§3, D-72). The Kin do not trust them to, and made sure of it once. That agreement is the quietest fact in the setting and the one that most shapes it: the Kin are not the first of anything. They are the only.
