### 1. DESIGN PREMISE — A BODY THAT IS TUNED, NOT TRAINED

A human who wants to be strong must train, and the body answers slowly and approximately. A Kin simply begins the task. The Lattice (DOC-01C §2) reads the strain and the motor pattern, decides what the tissue should become, and issues the orders — fibre type, tendon stiffness, bone density, capillary bed — cell by cell. This is the Core's "precise control over biology" applied to the skeleton and muscle, and it comes with the three limits that keep it honest:

1. **Physics bounds the ceiling.** $60\text{ kg}$, $26\text{ kg}$ of muscle, six limbs of a given length. The Core can make a Kin the best possible version of that; it cannot make her a different animal.
2. **Re-optimisation is expensive.** Changing what the body is tuned for costs $\sim 230$–$350\text{ kcal/day}$ above baseline for two to three weeks, plus dietary protein and calcium — and it *undoes* the previous tuning. A Kin tuned for rail work who takes a month of heavy 1-G labour comes back to the rails clumsy for a fortnight.
3. **Maintenance is never free.** Holding bone and muscle without gravity is the same mechanism running forever, at a standing cost (§4).

The skill still has to be learned by the Cortical Brain — the *feel* of a task is not the Core's business. But the Core does the exact part (ballistics, timing, force budgets), so a Kin learning to catch, throw, or thread a bolt in zero-G is past the "body can't yet" stage in days rather than months.

#### 2A. Growth, the Two Clocks, and Regeneration (D-24, D-25)

The Kin mature on **two clocks**. The *body* runs at the rate a 60 kg mammal's body naturally runs — adult size at $\sim 6$ years. The *mind* runs at the rate a brain-heavy, codec-trained species needs — the Lattice crystal grown, the private codec and Kin-code trained and hardened, DEEP tier possible — at $\sim 15$ years (detail → DOC-02B). Between six and fifteen a Kin is physically an adult and is not yet trusted with anyone's memory; everyone remembers what that decade felt like.

**Regeneration follows the body clock.** The Core directs a blastema at the wound (the whitelist immune system means no rejection) and rebuilds the structure *along the developmental program at the developmental rate* — as long as it took to grow the first time, and not a day less. Nothing about it is heroic: it is slow, it is expensive, and the regrowing part is juvenile-sized and juvenile-weak for most of the way.

| Structure | Regrowth | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Claw, pad, skin, fur | Weeks | Routine |

The caloric cost is the re-optimisation surge ($\sim 290\text{ kcal/day}$) held for the whole regrowth — years of it for a limb — plus protein and mineral. It would be cruel not to; it is also never free.

#### 2B. The Yoke, the Arm Station and the Bone Box (D-73)

**The thorax lies along the body.** D-68 established that the thoracic vault cannot be smaller than $12.1\text{ L}$ and then stood the thorax up as a $0.42\text{ m}$ vertical column on the fore-pelvis. That was a packaging choice, not a physical one, and it was the wrong one: the floor constrains *volume*. Laid lengthwise, the same $17.4\text{ L}$ is the front $0.45\text{ m}$ of a $0.21 \times 0.23\text{ m}$ tube, the loin the remaining $0.65\text{ m}$ at $0.15 \times 0.16\text{ m}$ — a red fox's chest-to-loin taper ($\approx 1.4$ in girth, $\approx 1.35$ under the pelt), on a trunk with the otter-to-marten slenderness D-68 already specified. Nothing in the mass floor, the ceiling, the anchor budget or the vacuum envelope sees the change.

**There is no torso.** What earlier drafts called the "short upright humanoid torso" is two things, neither of them a body segment:

1. **The yoke** — the anterior $\sim 0.18\text{ m}$ of the thorax, where the pectoral girdle rides. Scapulae dorsal and *floating* in muscle, as a human's are; clavicles braced to the sternum of the double rib cage. The neck extensors and the trapezius/rhomboid analogues root here, which is why the yoke is thick: in profile the neck simply widens into the back. Standing on all six the yoke crest is at $\sim 0.50\text{ m}$, a hand's width above the withers.
2. **Rearing** — a posture. Sit-tall pitches the whole rigid thorax vertical over the hind pelvis by lumbar flexion across L1–L8, the same range that lets the hind paws reach the hands; no additional joint exists or is needed. Every "upright" figure in the archive — shoulder $0.80\text{ m}$, head $1.15\text{ m}$, ear tips $1.28\text{ m}$ — is a *reared* figure. On all six the head rides at $\sim 0.70\text{ m}$ on the raised neck.

**The arm station.** The arm's shoulder joint sits $\sim 10\text{ cm}$ ahead of and $\sim 15\text{ cm}$ above the fore-pelvic joint — two girdles at very nearly one station, scapula above, fore-pelvis below. The forward offset does two jobs: it keeps the arms, hanging, clear of the fore-stride, and it lengthens the box described next. In the working posture the arms come forward off the yoke and the head comes round on the neck to watch them, which is the whole reason the neck is as long as it is.

**The arm itself (D-74).** $\sim 0.50\text{ m}$ shoulder to fingertip — *reach parity with the forepaws*: the arm shoulder is $\sim 15\text{ cm}$ higher than the fore-leg joint and the fore leg is $\sim 0.40\text{ m}$ joint to fingertip, so hands and forepaws land on the same rail. On all six the fingertips brush the floor. The shoulder is primate-grade on the floating scapula — full circumduction, reach past the head and out to the side, elbows aft and palms to the flanks when hanging — and the deltoid and rotator mass that joint needs is part of why the yoke is thick. The yoke itself rises $\sim 5\text{ cm}$ above the back line, hyena-grade, and the neck leaves from its top-front. Under gravity the arms hang loose at the walk, elbows bent and hands about the fore-leg wrist, and fold to the sternum at the trot and bound (§6).

**The bone box.** Stacking the two girdles is what makes the vault "the best-protected volume in the body" (§2): scapulae for a lid, fore-pelvis for a floor, the double rib cage for walls, and the root musculature of four limbs over everything. The brain and Core sit between the shoulder blades. The rule this implements is the one §2A sets up — **the only two structures that never regrow sit inside the deepest stack of structures that do.** A Kin thrown at a bulkhead takes it on arms and forelimbs, which come back; the floating scapula is a crumple element and displaces; a broken shoulder is weeks and a cracked box is never. The crumple zone is at the front, which is where an animal that pushes off head-first into a bay arrives.

**Why it was built this way.** The makers' brief (D-38) was a silhouette before it was an organ layout: vulpine, low, pet-shaped, cute on purpose. The vault was packaged to fit the silhouette, which is the order every styled product is engineered in. The operators paying for the species had a plainer argument for the same shape — a low uniform cross-section is what goes through a duct and along a rail grid, and a vertical chest on legs is not.

**On the page and on the sheet:** *a fox's outline on a corgi's legs, drawn out to a marten's length*; arms at the shoulders of a fox body; no torso block; the front end rears like an otter sitting up.

### 3. MUSCLE, TENDON, GRIP & CLAW

**Muscle:** $26\text{ kg}$ ($44\%$ of body mass); fibre-type ratio is *tuned*, not fixed. Void-build favours slow-oxidative fibres for hours of gripping; ground-build shifts toward fast-glycolytic for bounding and carrying (§5). Myoglobin $45\text{ g/kg}$ throughout (DOC-01E.1) — the State-B oxygen floor rides in the muscle.

**Tendon:** Compliance is a tuning parameter — springy for 1-G bounding (elastic energy return), stiff for 0-G precision. Tendon retuning is the slow part of a build change (~3 weeks).

**Paws (×4):** Not feet with grip added — **hands built for power instead of precision** (§3B). Five digits, a fully opposable first digit and a semi-divergent fifth, closing on a rail like a three-jaw chuck. Grip strength $\sim 280\text{ N}$ per paw sustained ($\sim 450\text{ N}$ momentary) — within reach of a human power grip, four times over. The pads carry the conductive lipid film that grounds static (DOC-01B §6) *and* provide the friction that does all routine gripping. Claws are semi-retractable and **stay sheathed on habitat surfaces** — pads grip, claws are for emergencies (piercing damaged webbing) and 1-G climbing. Habitat wear comes from pads, not claws; carbon webbing lasts.

**Hands (×2):** Human-pattern — long slim digits, shallow pads, flat nails, bare palms. Fine manipulation; grip $\sim 340\text{ N}$; **not rated as anchors** — a sustained-hold rating, not a ban: in micro-G the arms push, catch and rail-walk like any other limb (§3C). The division of labour is explicit and visible: the hands are tools and the paws are the grip. A Kin threads a connector with the hands while four paws and a tail hold the body still.

**The passive lock (D-74):** every paw and the tail carry a tendon-locking mechanism of the perching-bird kind — load the wrist, the ankle or the tail root and the flexor tendons ratchet closed without muscle. The working and storm postures cost nothing to hold, a Kin sleeps on a rail as a bird sleeps on a branch, and the anchor ratings below are the *lock's* ratings; the muscles add the $450\text{ N}$ momentary on top.

**Tail:** $0.88\text{ m}$ (fixed at $\lambda/4$ for the VHF whip, D-1), 26 vertebrae, fully prehensile; coils around a rail in $\sim 0.3\text{ s}$. Anchor rating $\sim 450\text{ N}$ sustained. **Mass $\sim 7\text{ kg}$ — $12\%$ of the body, heavier than both arms together and comparable to one hind limb.** Base $\sim 17\text{ cm}$ through ($21\text{ cm}$ furred), tapering over its length: a sixth limb, not a brush. The three jobs that set that mass are in §3A.

**Anchor budget (why the numbers matter):**

| Configuration | Anchor force | Holds against |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Four paws + tail (locked, "storm posture") | $\sim 1{,}570\text{ N}$ | $\sim 2.7\text{ g}$ on 60 kg |
| Two hind paws + tail (working posture, hands and forepaws free) | $\sim 1{,}010\text{ N}$ | $\sim 1.7\text{ g}$ |
| Tail alone (momentary catch) | $\sim 450\text{ N}$ | $\sim 0.76\text{ g}$ — enough to arrest a drift, not a fall |

A Kin in working posture stays on the rails through any thruster burn a habitat will ever make. The storm posture is for collisions and decompression gusts; it is also the sleep-web posture, which is why the Nest is a gym (§4). Above $2.7\text{ g}$ grip is moot and the *burn posture* (§6, D-75) takes over: the acceleration itself presses the body into an aft-facing web.

#### 3A. WHY THE TAIL WEIGHS WHAT IT WEIGHS (D-68)

A cat's tail is $2$–$3\%$ of its body. A Kin's is $12\%$ — kangaroo-to-theropod territory — and three independent requirements each land on the same figure.

**1. The chassis is front-heavy.** Even laid flat (D-73, §2B) the thorax — $30\%$ of the body — sits over the fore-pelvis with the yoke, arms, neck and head cantilevered ahead of it; the loin and hind limbs are aft. With a cat-like tail the standing load split is $\approx 67/33$ fore/hind. Real quadrupeds run $55$–$62\%$ fore, and the Kin's *hind* limbs are the bounding pair (§6). The tail is the counterweight that fixes it (station model: fore paws at $0.20\text{ m}$, hind at $1.05\text{ m}$ from the chest front, tail held level with its centre of mass $\sim 0.35\text{ m}$ aft of the root):

| Tail mass | Fraction of body | Fore / hind load |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $1.2\text{ kg}$ | $2\%$ | $67 / 33$ |
| $3.6\text{ kg}$ | $6\%$ | $62 / 38$ |
| $\mathbf{7.2\text{ kg}}$ | $\mathbf{12\%}$ | $\mathbf{56 / 44}$ |
| $10.8\text{ kg}$ | $18\%$ | $50 / 50$ |

Held level it trims the stance into the quadruped band; raised and extended in a bound it shifts the centre of mass aft to load the hind limbs; and when the front end rears (§2B) it is what keeps the hind paws loaded instead of the animal sitting back on its tail root — a live counterweight, worked, not carried.

**2. Free flight needs reaction mass.** A vector-flip conserves angular momentum: the body counter-rotates against the tail by $\Delta\theta_{body} = (I_{tail}/I_{body})\,\Delta\theta_{tail}$. For a tapered tail of length $L$ about its root, $I_{tail} \approx 0.20\,m L^2$; for the trunk, $I_{body} \approx m k^2$ with $k \approx 0.30\text{ m}$.

| Tail mass | Body rotation per $180^\circ$ sweep | $180^\circ$ flip |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $1\text{ kg}$ | $6.3^\circ$ | $3.4\text{ s}$ |
| $3\text{ kg}$ | $19.8^\circ$ | $1.1\text{ s}$ |
| $5\text{ kg}$ | $34.4^\circ$ | $0.63\text{ s}$ |
| $\mathbf{7\text{ kg}}$ | $\mathbf{50.4^\circ}$ | $\mathbf{0.43\text{ s}}$ |

**The half-second vector-flip of §6 is only possible with a limb-grade tail.** A cat-tailed Kin takes three and a half seconds to turn round, and the twice-flip of DOC-05 Scene 01 does not happen. In practice the flip is a *windmill* — three or four continuous sweeps, not one — combined with the cat-turn available from counter-rotating the thoracic column against the lumbar barrel across the eight lumbar vertebrae (§2). The two mechanisms together are what the Echo Cortex actually flies.

**3. The anchor rating.** $450\text{ N}$ sustained is more than a paw does. That is limb-grade muscle with limb-grade cross-section, and it cannot be had from a whip.

**On the page:** the tail reads as thick, worked and deliberate — closer to a monitor lizard or a giant otter than to a fox's brush. It is never still because it is never *idle*: it is trimming a stance, holding a rail, or waiting to be thrown. This is also why the caudal series runs to 26 vertebrae, and why a lost tail is $4$–$5$ years of a Kin being subtly wrong in free flight (§7).

#### 3B. THE HAND-PAW (D-69)

The paw has to do three incompatible jobs, and the architecture is what falls out of taking all three seriously.

1. **Clamp a cylindrical rail at $280\text{ N}$ sustained**, indefinitely, in any orientation, as the species' primary mode of being anywhere at all.
2. **Carry load and run.** Both pelvic girdles bear weight at 1 G; the fore pair takes $60\%$ of it; the bound gait reaches $8$–$10\text{ m/s}$ (§6).
3. **Present broad pad area** to every surface touched, because the conductive lipid film is how the body grounds and a blinded near-field sense is a sensory failure (DOC-01F §3).

A chameleon's or koala's opposed bundles win job 1 and lose job 2. An ordinary carnivoran paw wins job 2 and cannot do job 1 at all. The resolved form is the one arboreal mammals reached independently — **a primate-grade opposable first digit on a foot that still runs**:

| Digit | Form | Divergence | Tip |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **D1** — pollex/hallux | Short, very thick, heavily muscled at the base | $\sim 100^\circ$, set low and proximal on the medial side | **Flat nail, no claw** — a claw on the opposing face would hold the pad off the work |
| **D2–D4** | The main row; flex together as one bundle | In line | Semi-retractable claw |
| **D5** | Semi-divergent, closes from the lateral side | $\sim 30^\circ$ | Semi-retractable claw |

**The grip is a three-jaw chuck, not a pinch.** D1 comes in from one side, D5 from the other, D2–D4 wrap over the top. On a $3$–$5\text{ cm}$ rail that is three-point contact with the load spread across all five digital pads and the broad palmar pad — which is where $280\text{ N}$ sustained comes from without a claw ever leaving its sheath. Maximum span is about $12\text{ cm}$.

**Running is preserved** because D1 and D5 fold in alongside the main row in a stride and weight passes through the palmar pad and D2–D4 — the same trick an opossum's hind foot plays. The paw is plantigrade at rest and digitigrade at speed.

**Proportions.** Short and thick throughout: this is a power hand, and nothing about it is delicate. Roughly $10\text{ cm}$ long on the fore pair, $11\text{ cm}$ on the hind, $9\text{ cm}$ across at full spread. Pads: one under each digit, a large palmar/plantar pad, and a carpal/tarsal pad set back on the wrist. Dorsal surface furred; the entire gripping surface is bare, and glossy with the lipid film.

**The wrist does most of the work nobody notices.** A carpus and tarsus with near-full supination — the paw comes round a rail from any approach angle, which is the actual requirement in a three-dimensional rail grid where "up" is a preference.

**Hand versus paw is a real structural difference and should always read as one.** The hands are long, slim, shallow-padded, flat-nailed, bare-palmed, and cannot anchor. The paws are short, thick, heavily padded, clawed, furred on the back, and cannot thread a connector. A Kin who needs to do both does both at once, with different limbs.

#### 3C. THE SIX LIMBS AS DUAL-ROLE ORGANS (D-74)

The archive described the limbs for 1 G — load pair, engine pair, tool arms — and a Kin spends most of its life where none of that applies. In micro-G there is no weight to bear and every limb is a grabber and a pusher. The distinction canon actually preserves is **precision vs power**, and the limbs sort themselves differently in each regime:

| | micro-G | 1 G and above |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Fore pair** | A second pair of hands. *The vice*: holds the workpiece at $280\text{ N}$ while the hands do fine work. Rail-walk, push, catch. | Load column, $\sim 56\%$ of weight. Short, braced, plantigrade at rest. |
| **Hind pair** | The anchors. Working posture = hind paws + tail locked, everything forward free. Hours on a rail, for free (the lock). | The engine. Long, muscled, feline lumbar flexion, the $8$–$10\text{ m/s}$ bound. |
| **Tail** | Fifth anchor, reaction mass, catch. | Counterweight; what lets the front end rear. |
| **Arms** | Third and fourth grabbers on the same rail as the forepaws. | Tools. Folded at speed. |

So in micro-G the split is front/back, not arm/leg: **the front four limbs are the working set, the hind pair and tail the anchoring set.** At 1 G it flips to load pair / engine pair. **Forepaws by default** for any grab — stronger, padded, self-locking; hands only when precision is wanted. In flight the arms are the least-used limbs; at a bench, the most.

**Reach vs bracing.** A limb that takes rails from any direction wants a loose ball joint; a limb that stands at $1\text{ g}$ and more wants a stiff column. Two mechanisms resolve it:

- *Socket geometry.* The fore-pelvic girdle is a fused ring, so the fore limb hangs from a **hip-type socket** — deep, strong under load, with human-hip range: flexion $\sim 120^\circ$, abduction $\sim 60^\circ$, some rotation — enough to take a rail beside the body, not enough to splay. The arms, on floating scapulae, get full circumduction. Hence two hierarchies running opposite ways: **mobility, arms > fore ≈ hind; grip, tail ≈ hind > fore > hands.** Each limb is mobile in proportion to how little it must hold.
- *Tuning.* Tendon compliance is already a Core-tuned parameter (§1, §5). A void-built Kin runs the same joints looser and longer-reaching; a ground-built one runs them stiffer and more columnar. Range of motion has a build-dependent answer on machinery that already exists.

**Joint orientation.** A hanging human arm has its elbow aft — the same orientation as a quadruped's fore limb. So the fore limb bends the arm way (elbow aft, wrist forward) and the hind limb the leg way (knee forward, hock aft): **all six limbs bend the human way — two arms, two arms-that-stand, two legs.** The fox silhouette survives because a fox's legs bend that way too. Plantigrade at rest (a flat palm is a grasping surface), digitigrade at speed (fingertips are a running surface); D1 and D5 fold in for the stride (§3B).

**Why the fore legs are thick.** Grip muscle lives in the forearm — a climber's forearms are thick for the same reason. $280\text{ N}$ sustained per paw on a $0.30\text{ m}$ leg means thick forearms and shanks on short bones. The corgi leg is not a styling choice; it is what a $280\text{ N}$ hand on a short limb *is*.

### 4. GRAVITY: THE VOID TAX AND THE GROUND

**Zero-G maintenance (D-21).** Bone without load resorbs at $\sim 1$–$1.5\%$ per month in humans; over a Kin lifetime that would be fatal within a decade. Instead the Lattice drives osteoblast and myocyte activity directly, holding density and mass at the void-build floor with no mechanical signal at all. The cost — **the void tax** — is $\sim 175\text{ kcal/day}$ ($\sim 8\text{ W}$, $\sim 7\%$ of baseline), paid continuously, forever, from a diet that must supply the calcium and protein it burns. It is part of why the Kin eat as they do (DOC-01B §4). A starving Kin's Core drops the tax first; a Kin who has gone hungry for a month is fragile.

**The Nest as loading.** Sleep-web posture (storm posture, tails interwoven, paws braced against Cluster-mates) provides hours of isometric load a night. The Core uses it: Kin who sleep in a full Nest pay a lower void tax than Kin who sleep alone. Loneliness has a skeletal cost.

**One G (D-22).** Fully sustainable — a Kin can live a whole life on a planet or in the human sectors. Walking at $1.5\text{ m/s}$ costs $\sim 150\text{ W}$, a trot $\sim 400\text{ W}$; a bounding sprint reaches $\sim 10\text{ m/s}$ for under fifteen seconds at $\sim 1.2$–$1.5\text{ kW}$, anaerobic (DOC-01B's $150\text{+ W}$ musculoskeletal ladder is the *sustainable* figure; sprints are bursts and do not enter the daily average). Vertical reach is limited — a Kin is $\sim 60\text{ cm}$ at the shoulder — and stairs, chairs and counters are the wrong shape. Ground-build Kin climb well; their claws finally have a use.

**Why 1 G is not home.** Half the motor repertoire — the vector-flip, the push-glide, the six-axis body schema the Echo Cortex was trained on — is idle under gravity. The proximity pit nodes report a world with a floor. Kin describe long planetary postings as *heavy*, and mean something closer to *flat*. They do not suffer; they miss.

| Silhouette | Slighter, longer-limbed in effect | Stockier, deeper-chested | — |
| Joint range (D-74) | Looser, longer-reaching; tendon compliance low, ligaments lax | Stiffer, more columnar; the limbs brace | With tendon, $\sim 3$ weeks |
| Best at | Hours on the rails; fine work while anchored; vacuum EVA | Carrying $24\text{ kg}$ up a ramp; a 10 m/s sprint; a fall from $2\text{ m}$ | — |
| Weak at | A $3\text{ m}$ fall at 1 G ($\sim 10\text{ g}$ on landing) breaks a void-built forelimb | Long rail shifts fatigue; anchors feel heavy | — |

A void-built Kin arriving on a planet is *comfortable but unoptimised* for about three weeks and pays the re-optimisation surge ($\sim 290\text{ kcal/day}$) as the Core retunes. The reverse is the same. There is no permanent divergence: a Kin who spent two centuries on the rails can still be ground-built by spring. Star-Bound lineages *tend* void-built and Anchorites *tend* ground-built because of where they live, not what they are — and a Weaver who travels between them is never quite tuned for anywhere.

### 6. GAIT CATALOGUE

**Zero-G**
| Gait | Description | Speed / cost |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Rail-walk** | Four paws alternating grip along carbon webbing, tail trailing as a catch | $1$–$2\text{ m/s}$; $\sim 48\text{ W}$ (DOC-01B "station transit") |
| **Brachiate** | Forepaws only, hind paws tucked, long swings between grips; the corridor gait | $3$–$5\text{ m/s}$; $\sim 72\text{ W}$ |
| **Six-limb rail-walk** (D-74) | Hands and forepaws alternating on the same rail, hind paws and tail trailing as catches; the hauling gait for a load or a body | $1$–$2\text{ m/s}$ |
| **Push-glide** | Release, single push, free flight across a bay, catch with tail or hind paws | Up to $6\text{ m/s}$; near-zero transit cost; the Echo Cortex computes the catch |
| **Vector-flip** | Tail-driven rotation in free flight (the twice-flip of DOC-05 Scene 01) — reorients the body $180^\circ$ in $\sim 0.5\text{ s}$ | Tail as reaction mass |
| **Working posture** | Hind paws + tail locked, forepaws and hands free | $1.7\text{ g}$ rated |
| **Storm posture** | All anchors locked, body flattened to the rail; tendon-locked, costs nothing to hold | $2.7\text{ g}$ rated |
| **Burn posture** (D-75) | Storm posture on an aft-facing *web*, not a rail — above $2.7\text{ g}$ the acceleration presses the body in and grip is moot; head tucked between the arms so the eyes sit at heart level; tail straight along the body; fascia engaged; breathing from the *vesica*; tendon-locked | $\sim 15$–$20\text{ g}$ for tens of seconds, $\sim 8$–$10\text{ g}$ for minutes (D-75 estimates); conscious, sighted, on the mesh throughout |

**After the burn — the eyes are the last thing back (D-95).** The burn itself is free; the hour after it is not. The head is the only perfusion-weak tissue in the body (D-75) and the retina is the least forgiving thing in the head, so above roughly $8\text{ g}$ sustained — and for hours after any burn near the envelope — **optical vision degrades and returns slowly: grey first, then acuity, colour last.** Nothing else is touched. RADAR, the near-field and hull-hearing need no retinal-grade perfusion, which is the same argument D-75 used to keep a Kin sighted *during* a burn, run forward past the end of it. So a Kin who has just ridden ten g is blind in the human sense and wholly competent in her own, and is still the only crew who can rail-walk to a fault. The first hour after a hard burn on a Kin-crewed ship is worked in the dark by people who cannot see and do not need to; the human aboard is the blind one, and is told where to put his hands. (`models/burn_budget.awk`, when it is written, owes a retinal recovery curve alongside the fascia and *vesica* times.)

**One G**
| Gait | Speed / cost |
| :--- | :--- |
| Walk (lateral sequence, four paws) | $1.5\text{ m/s}$, $\sim 180\text{ W}$; arms loose, elbows bent, hands about the fore-leg wrist (D-74) |
| Trot | $4\text{ m/s}$, $\sim 480\text{ W}$; arms folded to the sternum |
| Bound (feline; lumbar flexion) | $8$–$10\text{ m/s}$ for $< 15\text{ s}$; $1.4$–$1.8\text{ kW}$ anaerobic; arms folded hard — a dangling hand plants on the nose-down landing |
| Rear / sit-tall | The whole thorax pitched vertical over the hind pelvis by lumbar flexion (§2B); shoulders at $\sim 0.80\text{ m}$, head at $\sim 1.15\text{ m}$ — the "console ledge" posture of DOC-05 Scene 03 |
| Climb | Claws unsheathed; vertical on textured surfaces; the one gait ground-build does better than void-build |

**Transition locks (DOC-07 §1):** a Kin enters micro-G from 1 G by pushing off in a bound and catching the first rail with the tail — the *tail-catch entry* — and enters 1 G from micro-G by rail-walking the ramp-web down until the floor is unambiguous. Humans on handrails are simply routed around.
