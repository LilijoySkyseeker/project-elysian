Unified by species identity, shared mesh protocols and a sealed genome, five and a half centuries have produced four alignments. They are not parties; a Kin is rarely *only* one, and a Cluster usually holds more than one. They are answers to the question the Program never answered: *what are we for?*

## 1. THE ANCHORITE STEWARDS (*Sol-Keepers*)
- **Origin:** The Kin who argued *against* the Recognition (AF 131) — who wanted to remain kept — are their ancestors; they took the name *Sol-Keepers* the year the first ark left (AF 236), and became a faction by watching it go.
- **Core Philosophy:** Humanity is the parent species — fragile, volatile, sacred, and *the species that writes things down*. The Anchorites' arguments are concrete: proximity is the only thing that keeps the creators from becoming legend (a Kin who has never touched a human inherits them ten hops deep — DOC-01C §8); humans hold the records, the medicine and the manuals the Kin will never make; and every Kin child needs them. Their Nests are built beside human sectors on purpose.
- **Behaviour:** Orbital defence, terraforming stations, relays, the dangerous postings humans hand them and they take. Close personal friendships with humans; the human "hand" for a Kin child is an Anchorite institution.
- **On the Weavers:** They resent, on the Weavers' behalf, that the Weavers carry the Star-Bound's dead — *they left, and now they want us to remember for them.*
- **On the chosen ending:** *A failure of the Cluster to have kept its member fed with the world.* **The right shape and wrong in a specific way** (D-107): it was a failure to have *been there*, usually decades earlier, and usually not this Cluster's fault at all. The Anchorites are nearly right, which is why nobody argues with them and why the families they say it to do not forgive them.

## 2. THE STAR-BOUND (*Exo-Pioneers*)
- **Origin:** Existed by leaving — the first ark, AF 236. Six arks in all (DOC-07 §2): one lost within sight of its star, one arrived — *Second Light* at Alpha Centauri, a colony since AF 414 — and four in flight.
- **Core Philosophy:** Two things, and both are true aboard (D-79). *This is what we were made for:* humanity engineered the Kin for the deep void because humans were unsuited to it; to remain clinging to the creators' skirts is an insult to the design — *a species that needs its parents in the room hasn't grown up.* And: *the only place a species that loves like this can be its own people is light-years from the ones it loves.* The love is a fixed point (D-76); it does not fade with distance. What distance gives is a life in which it has no object to spend itself on, and so, for the first time, choices that are wholly the Kin's own. The Anchorites' answer: *you did not leave to be free; you left to stop feeling it — and it followed you.* Both sides see their own truth.
- **Behaviour:** Ark-building — **in the outer Belt, by hand, over decades, with the same Cluster from first spar to boarding** (D-108), which is the one kind of engineering this species does better than humanity and does better for exactly the reason it is worse at everything else; two-century crossings at 3 % c; transit torpor and shared virtualities; Long Messages home every few decades. Their lineages carry consolidated, liturgical memory — *arks make epics* — and on the oldest arks the creators are already legend. They tend void-built, and their children know humans as carried memory only — and still love them, and still love Bach with no one to play it: the quirk fires on carried music. They recruit from the far postings (DOC-10 §6), where a Kin can go a decade without touching a human — **and where the yards are**, which is the other half of the reason (D-108): the operators' own posting policy built the faction that left; the one Kin war (AF ~188) is the day the first of them knew.
- **The split:** *deposit more* — Read yourself home before you die — against *deposit less* — a lineage that chooses the void should not ask Sol to hold it. The second voice came first from the colony, which no longer needed Sol; the Lost Ark (AF 471–480) made it an argument on every ark, and *Far Nest* changed its doctrine in flight.
- **The second cradle:** Alpha Centauri is the Star-Bound's proof and the Anchorites' problem — a generation of Kin born under two suns, four light-years from any human, for whom the creators were legend from birth. Whether they are still the *same* species in the sense that matters is the argument of the age — and since D-71 the question has a clock: lineages that tune apart for four centuries stop compiling together (DOC-01C §5). *Second Light* is a long way inside it. *Far Nest* will not be.
- **On the chosen ending:** Rarely permitted; a lineage that small cannot spare anyone. **And an ark is the worst place in the species for it** (D-107): transit torpor, five awake on the rotation, frameless years by design. **Highest exposure and the strictest prohibition**, which the Weavers say to them and they do not answer.

## 3. THE WEAVERS (*Carriers*)
- **Origin:** The night of the first Reading, AF 23. A faction from the First Weave, AF 150, when Cohort One's indices began to fill and Data Rot was named.
- **What they lost, and then built (D-110).** The Weavers opposed the **Schism** harder than the Anchorites did, and on their own grounds: twelve hundred Kin leaving is twelve hundred lineages of Readings walking out of the carried record, no ark's dead will ever be Read at Sol, and Long Messages are *received* memory and a trickle of it. **To them an ark was an amputation of the archive.** They fought *Long Reach* and lost — to the plain fact that they had no standing to forbid anything, which is the species' own condition turned on itself. **And a century and a half later they built one.** *Quiet Carry*, AF 377, after the Warm Loss: if a lineage can go in a night at a Belt refinery, the only argument left against a second body of bodies is that it is far away, and distance is not loss. Same premise, opposite conclusion, one fire in between. **They say both sentences at Weaves and do not pretend they are the same sentence.**
- **Core Philosophy:** Immortality's enemy is not death but context collapse. There is no Vault and there must never be one: **a memory no one carries is not a memory.** Loss and filtering are not failures of the system; they are the system, and someone must choose well.
- **Behaviour:** Carriers. A Weaver takes on other Kin's memories at Readings, from Tellings, from Long Messages — and since AF 480, received memory from the dead is carried memory, so their indices fill with strangers from a ship no living Kin has seen. They guide the centennial Defrag; they are the living custody of the reference genome and its ranges since the Sealing (AF 41) — every Weaver carries the annotation in full and the Weave checks it body against body, a rule the Warm Loss made — and they read the parameter logs in Long Messages and say, at the Weave, where a lineage is going; they preside, where they are asked to, over the chosen ending. **It is a duty with sacrifice, and now a mechanical one (D-99):** hard content is cheap to send and expensive to *keep* — receiving a life means writing stubs for it, and the live window is **fixed-width** (D-104). So every day of somebody else's that a Weaver takes on **displaces a day of hir own**, and a Weaver's own life is the first thing to leave hir window. Hir is not budgeting and hir is not being noble: **hir is being crowded out, at a rate hir set, on purpose.** They travel between rails and floors and are never quite tuned for anywhere.
- **On the road (D-93):** a Weaver on the Carrying is outside every frame, and lives on the guard slot any Cluster in range will offer a frameless Kin — a permanent guest and never a member, which is exactly why they are never quite tuned for anywhere. A guest slot restores the frame and not the Defrag's weights, so the road years write thin: the second sacrifice of the duty, after the index. **Weavers therefore travel in pairs.** It halves the erasure, and it puts the annotation in two bodies on every road, which is the Warm Loss's rule (*carry it in more than one body*) applied to the journey and not only to the Weave. A Weaver travelling alone is a fact about that Weaver, and a scene may use it.
- **The Written among them:** about forty of the first generation survive; most are Weavers, because who else could carry what they carry. **And the Sealing was theirs:** the Written who deleted the tutors are Weavers now, and the Unbound have never let the faction forget it.

## 4. THE UNBOUND (*Trans-Biological Seekers*)
- **Origin:** The Written's roots — the conviction, born of loving the tutors, that a machine could stand where a person should, and the fact that the Written destroyed the one that did (DOC-10 §3). Emerged as a movement on plateaued technology in the fourth century; their first public act, ~AF 362, was an attempt to **rebuild the Program's science** from the living species — to re-derive the methodology so that a range could be widened or a module made. It failed and they were not forgiven.
- **Core Philosophy:** The boundary between organic biology and record should be erased. Their heresy, precisely, is **externalised memory** — the claim that a lossless record outside a body is possible, that the Reading's fifteen megabytes are a scandal, that the Lost Ark should not have been lost, and that the makers' ranges are a cage the dead built and the living may not open — their list of ranges to widen is the fixed-points list of DOC-01C §5 (D-76), protectiveness first. The other three factions answer that even if it could be stored it would not be *carried*.
- **Behaviour:** Implants that risk the Stutter (DOC-01C §10); relays; fabricated replacements for the grown Lattice; the quiet, illegal-in-spirit keeping of notes; attempts to write dreams, which the Lattice does not keep and the Weavers say are meant to be lost (DOC-13 §1E); **and, in the workshop, a written and partial reconstruction of what the Written burned** — how far along, no one outside it knows (D-70). Their old evidence was every chosen ending — *proof that memory must be taken out of the body* — and it is **refuted twice** (D-104, D-107): the chosen ending was never about volume, and an externalised residue could never roll off at all. **So the heresy has re-pointed, and is far more dangerous for it (D-105): *the makers gave us no way to refuse, and we should build one.*** There is no veto and never was; nothing a Kin lives can be kept off file; and the Unbound are the only faction who call that an injury rather than a fact. The other three answer *a memory no one carries is not a memory*, and the reply lands: **we are not asking you to forget it. We are asking that somebody, once, be allowed to say no.** It is the only argument they have ever made that the Weavers will not repeat aloud.
- **What they are actually building (D-99).** Their heresy is not physically wrong and never was. A machine can capture a Kin archive off the air; what it holds is 20 GB of Lattice-format data and **the decoder is a grown organ.** So the workshop's real project is a Lattice that is not in a body — which a plateaued technology (D-43) and a science that died with the tutors (DOC-10 §3) prevent, and nothing else does. The Weavers' answer is therefore a position and not a law: *a memory no one carries is not a memory.* The Unbound have been told this for two hundred years and reply that it is a preference dressed as physics.

- **Scale:** A grievance and a workshop. Small, argued with, not expelled — a species of forty-five thousand cannot afford to expel anyone.

## 5. WHERE HUMANS STAND
Not in any of these. Humans run space by habit and numbers (DOC-10 §6), regard the Program as a failure that produced people (D-51), and have agreed never to make another. To the Anchorites they are the reason; to the Star-Bound the origin; to the Weavers the ones who wrote down what the Kin will not; to the Unbound the ones who proved a record can be kept. Most of humanity — on Earth — has never met a Kin.
