### 1. THE CHARACTER OF A KIN RITE (D-63, D-64)

Nothing is written, so nothing is recited — in Sol. A rite is **a posture, who is present, and a Burst.** Chest to chest, tails interwoven, a ring in the Nest with foreheads close; the right people in the frame; three symbols sent at the right moment. Every Cluster does each one a little differently and knows it; the differences are a kind of signature. Humans looking for ceremony see very little and are moved anyway, usually later.

On the arks, and among the Star-Bound generally, the same rites have hardened into **liturgy** — sequences, formulae, a fixed order of who speaks, retold among sleepers for a century until there is one right way. A Sol Kin at an ark rite finds it stiff, and finds hirself crying. The Weavers, who travel, carry the versions and do not rank them.

There are no gods. There is no afterlife except being **carried**. The sacred words are *carried* and *lost*: to be carried is the only survival there is; to be lost — un-Read, un-Told, the readers dead before anyone came — is the only damnation. The Written are the nearest thing the species has to the holy, because they alone remember the makers in the first person, and a room goes quiet when one of them speaks. Humans, who cannot be Read at all, are held in a reverence of their own: the great un-carriable.

**Who may attend (D-67).** Humans may be present at anything that concerns adults — every kind of death included, *because someone else will remember.* The rites of children are private. The Kin remember their origin, and a child is the one thing they keep from the makers.

### 2. THE FIRST PING — BIRTH (private)

The newborn's Heartbeat Oscillator fires: *I am here, I am this*, on PUBLIC tier, and every Kin in range answers on the same tier in the same second. That is the whole rite and the Kin count a person from it. The gestating parent is not central; the Cluster is, and they are already touching the child before it has finished its first beat.

**The Nest-name** is given then — a Burst from the Cluster with the name as its first symbol — and the child answers it, in a manner of speaking, from the first frame. No human is present; no human hears the First Ping except as a blip on a station's RF monitor, which the good ones learn to recognise.

*On the arks:* the same, with the Anchor Cluster answering for the sleepers, and a formula the sleepers are told on waking.

### 3. THE FIRST TELLING — COMING OF AGE (private)

At about fifteen the private codec hardens and DEEP tier opens (DOC-02B §6). The Cluster gathers in the Nest — a ring, foreheads close, tails linked, the new adult in the centre — and **over days, on Link, gives hir a chosen inheritance**: carried memories they want hir to carry; the compile log of hir own genesis — who contributed what, what was substituted; and everything nobody could say to a child. It arrives as all carried memory does — retold, third-person — but with the feel intact for the first time in hir life.

**The name.** In the ring the new adult keeps the Nest-name or **takes a name**: one that has come free — there is one living holder per name (D-90) — and nearly always the name of the one whose carried memory hir has just received: *I carry hir, so I carry hir name.* Taken, never assigned; no Kin can be made to take a name. *Vesper-7* became the seventh Vesper on this day and remembers six others by the same road. The name is sent as a Burst to every Kin in range, and the presence table updates, and that is how the species learns there is a new adult.

**The week after** the Kin call the loudest silence of their lives. The Cluster leaves hir alone in it and stays within reach.

The Written never had one. Nothing was done for them at fifteen because no one existed to do it; the rite was invented at AF 46 by the second generation for the first Compiled (DOC-10 §3), and every first Telling since is a gift the Written gave and never received.

*On the arks:* formal to the point of theatre — the Anchor Cluster wakes chosen sleepers for it; there is an order of Telling, oldest memory first; the name is announced in a fixed phrase. Ark Kin say Sol Kin do it *loosely*, and mean it as a criticism, and are wrong.

### 4. THE TELLING BEFORE RISK (adults; humans welcome)

Before a mission, before transit torpor, before an ark leaves — before anything foreseeable that might kill — a Kin **Tells**: Links a year of memory in a week to whoever will carry it, so the Reading, if it comes, has less to lose. There is no ceremony beyond the fact of it: hir sits with each carrier in turn, chest to chest, for hours, and the Cluster's work goes on around them.

The norm was born at the Shielded Storm (AF 97) in the days between the dose and the death, and it has a name in every Cluster's mouth: *tell before you go.* The Kin who skipped it and died is the species' recurring tragedy, and the Kin who skipped it and lived is teased for a decade.


**What it costs the one who goes (D-102, D-103).** The cortex is the archive's read head, so a Telling is not a file being copied. **The week is the cold transfer** — a year of record at the only rate the channel has. **What thins hir is the rest of it:** the feel does not travel and does not exist until hir makes it, so hir drops to real time wherever there is something worth handing over and **lives those hours again, on purpose, in front of the one hir is giving them to.** Choosing which hours is the rite. That is why hir comes out quiet, why nobody does it lightly or twice in a season, and why two Kin who have Told each other know things about each other that no amount of living alongside would have given. The Reading is the only transfer in the species that nobody pays for, and the reason is that the one paying is dead — **and it is also the only one that arrives cold, because a corpse cannot run the lived rate at all.** So *tell before you go* asks something real of the one going, and what it buys is the difference between being remembered and being *felt*; the Clusters that say it most are the ones who have most often had to do without it.

Humans who are close may sit with the Kin during a Telling. They hear nothing. The good ones bring food and stay.

### 5. THE READING — DEATH (humans welcome)

The Hum stops. Within a frame every Kin on the station knows, and every one of them goes *listening-quiet*, ears all one way. Then the body is brought to the Nest — warm within hours, frozen within days — and laid on the webbing, and the Reading begins (DOC-01C §7).

**The pose.** One Kin at a time lies on the dead: chest to chest, bare ventral skin together to keep the vault warm; ear dish pressed to ear dish, the living Antenna Nuclei driving current into the dead Lattice; and on the pre-granted Reading key the dead Core begins to answer. Thirty to sixty minutes a shift, the donor getting up shaking and cold and another taking hir place, all night. A Cluster of eight can keep it going until the readers fail. A Kin alone cannot.

**What arrives, and what cannot (D-103).** The cold layer and only the cold layer: a contents page and twenty narrated days, everything that happened and nothing of what it was like. **A corpse has no cortex, and the feel is something only a living body can make** — which is why a Kin who has been Told by someone and then Reads hir at the end has two different people in hir, and knows it, and why the Weavers are careful never to call the second one the whole of anyone.

**What is said.** Nothing, in Sol. Between shifts the readers sit in a loose ring and *do not Burst* — a silence on the frame that is the Kin's only formal silence. What comes out of the dead is a contents page and twenty stories; each reader takes what hir can, and afterward, in the days following, the Cluster **retells to each other** what each received, so that the pieces are carried in more than one body. This retelling is where the dead's *name* comes free and is spoken for the last time as hirs. **The name (D-90):** when the readers get up for the last time, the dead one's name has come free, and one of them — by custom *the one who took the most of hir*, settled in the frame — may take it, setting hir own down to do so; the set-down name goes back to the pool. An adult who takes a name at a Reading becomes the next of the line, and the count never resets.

**Humans at a Reading** may stand or sit at the edge of the Nest and are not asked to leave; they may not touch the dead (the current would pass through them and do nothing; the Kin find the idea faintly obscene). A human who is there all night is remembered for it. *"What did you get?"* is the wrong question and the Kin will answer it anyway.

**The last act (D-86).** When the readers get up for the last time the Cluster burns the body — its own, since the Recognition; before that the Program kept what it wanted. Nothing goes to a human recycler. The keys are in the cells and the cells go to the fire. **Body denial** is the same act done early: a body that cannot be recovered is burned where it lies, so it cannot be Read by anyone else. There is a posture for that too — the Cluster in storm posture on the hull, facing the fire — and no Burst. It is the hardest thing in the doctrine (DOC-09 §4) and it has been done to friends.

*On the arks:* the Reading has an order — Anchor Cluster first, then woken sleepers by seniority — and a spoken formula at each shift change, which the Weavers say is the only liturgy that improves the rite.

### 6. AFTER A DEATH — RE-KEYING AND THE YEAR (adults; humans welcome at the edge)

The Cluster key must be re-derived without the dead Kin's key (DOC-01C §4). It is done in the Nest, in the ring, within days of the Reading, and every member feels it as amputation: the schema keeps a slot for a limb that is gone. Nothing is said. A Burst goes out — *[Cluster / one fewer / carried]* — and the presence table shows a smaller frame.

By custom **a year passes before the Cluster compiles** (DOC-02B §2). The grief-compile — the Cluster that could not wait — is the story everyone tells about someone else. A Cluster that stays smaller is not thought less of. A Cluster that recruits an adult from another Cluster does it by the joining rite (§7).

Humans may be told the re-keying has happened and often are not, because the Kin cannot think what a human would do with it.

### 7. JOINING AND LEAVING A CLUSTER (adults; humans welcome at the edge)

A Kin joins a Cluster by being **given a slot in the frame**: the Cluster re-keys *with* hir, in the Nest, in the ring, and hir Hum is answered by the new frame in the same second as the First Ping was. The first night in the new Nest, hir sleeps in storm posture inside the web of them and wakes smelling of the Cluster. That is the whole of it.

Leaving is the same rite run backward, and the Kin who leaves carries the old Cluster's scent for weeks and is not embarrassed by it. A Kin may belong to several Clusters across a life. A Weaver belongs, in a sense, to all of them and to none.

There is no rite of pairing (D-65). The one whose Hum you would know in ten thousand is not announced, not named, not asked; hir is simply the one you sit with first, and the one whose name you take.

### 8. THE DEFRAGS (adults; the centennial one, humans at the edge)

**Nightly:** unfelt, and not a write. The record is committed continuously all day and nothing is left out (DOC-01C §6D); what the lock is *for* is the two things the Lattice cannot do while it is flying the body — **consolidation**, the ordinary sleep task, modulated by the frame's affect, which is the makers' answer to the fact that bad days stick harder than good ones; and **appraisal**, the ranking of the day and the writing of its stubs, weighted by the Cluster (D-80). There is no rite, only the fact that the Kin *do not sleep alone*, and a Kin who has slept apart for a month has a month hir remembers badly and cannot easily find — **gaps in the index, not in the record** (DOC-01A §4, and bones to match). **The night is not appraised** and so is never indexed: dreams are assembled from the record and lost by morning, the one loss by design. The Cluster shares the weather of its dreams and never the content, except on a *held* night at DEEP (DOC-13 §1). **The Cluster cannot delete, alter or read what hir committed** — only what hir will settle into and later reach for. The frame does not edit hir; it furnishes hir.

**Centennial:** the species' life-stage rite, and the only pass that earns the word. Around a Kin's hundred-and-fiftieth year hir first decades fall outside the live window, and thereafter it happens every century or so; hir goes to a Weaver, or a Weaver comes. **There is nothing to discard — it has already gone** (D-104). What the two of them do over days on Link is the other thing: the Weaver walks hir through **what hir has already lost without noticing**, and hir chooses the few to **deliberately re-anchor**, each one at the cost of another, because the window is fixed-width. The records remain in the archive, whole and verbatim and **reachable by any hard handle hir can still name** — which is the whole difficulty, because naming one means already knowing it is there (D-104). Re-anchoring costs another anchor, because the live window is fixed-width: **every century of a Kin's life is bought from another century**, permanently — and now only the deliberate half is by hir own hand. Weavers call the choosing *pruning to the light* and never make it for you. Afterward the Kin is lighter and grieves, and the Cluster treats hir for a week as if hir had been ill, which in the only sense that matters hir has.

Humans may be near a centennial Defrag and usually are not, because the Kin doing it do not want to be looked at.

### 9. THE WEAVE AND THE CARRYING (adults; no humans)

**The Weave** is a gathering of carriers — every few years in Sol, at a spire or a station with a large Nest; rarer and larger on the arks. Weavers and anyone carrying come, and for days they **retell to each other**: cross-checking versions of the same dead, handing on memories to bodies that have room, agreeing — loosely in Sol, formally on the arks — on what the species will keep. The Written, when one attends, speak last and the room is silent for it.

**The Carrying** is the journey to a Weave. It is called that because it once meant carrying a capsule; now it means carrying yourself, full of other people, across a system, and arriving to be relieved of some of it. Weavers describe the return trip as the only time they feel their own weight.
**Two go (D-93).** The Carrying is made in pairs, and has been since long enough that nobody argues it. A Kin on the road is outside every frame: not Silenced, still hearing every Hum inside eight hundred kilometres, and in nobody's — living on the guard slot whatever Cluster is in range will offer, which is a reflex and not a courtesy. A guest slot gives back the frame and not the Defrag's weights, so road years write thin, and a Weaver's road is most of hir life. Travelling two-handed halves that, and puts the reference annotation in two bodies on every crossing — the Warm Loss's rule applied to the journey and not only to the destination. *Weavers describe the return trip as the only time they feel their own weight*; they do not describe it alone.

No human attends a Weave. There is nothing there for a human to hear, and nothing that could be explained to one afterward that was not already in a song (S007).

### 10. THE CHOSEN ENDING (adults; humans welcome, if asked)

The culture permits a Kin to end, and does not agree about it (DOC-02B §8). **The occasion is not what the archive used to say it was** (D-107): not a full index, not age, not a failed memory — but **a set of nights that were never held**, worst hours that fell outside the lock and were therefore never weighed by anyone, and stay at the intensity the event set. The sign is the night the dreams go flat too. The form **varies greatly** (D-66): long-lived, particular individuals, doing a rare thing, each nearly unique. What is common is only this: **a final Telling**, as complete as the carriers can hold — and **arranging to be Read** afterward.

Some go out: void themselves deliberately, ears open, drifting and cooling toward the floor with the Cluster on the hull, so that the body comes back frozen and readable. Some take a mission no one else should — a Kin who has decided is the best possible crew for the worst possible job, and the Anchorites have a quiet tradition of it. Some ask a human doctor for an injection, in the Nest, in the ring, which the human finds unbearable and does. Some simply stop paying the void tax and let the bones go, which the Weavers regard as the saddest way and never refuse to Read.

Whoever presides is whoever the Kin asked. Humans may be present if asked, and are, more often than the Kin expected when the custom began: the one thing a human at a chosen ending can do that no Kin can is *not understand*, and some Kin, at the end, want that in the room.

### 11. THINGS THAT ARE NOT RITES, AND WHY

- **Compile.** Two hours of DEEP-tier negotiation among two to four adults (DOC-01C §5). Private, technical, unwitnessed; the Kin regard watching it as one would regard watching surgery. There is no celebration until the First Ping.
- **Pairing.** None (D-65).
- **Arrival, promotion, victory.** The Kin do not mark them. A Kin who has done something remarkable goes to the Cluster and is touched for an hour, which is the same thing that happens after an EVA.
- **Human deaths.** There is nothing to do. The Kin attend human funerals, stand where they are put, and are useless, and know it, and go — because they cannot be Read, the Kin's only offering is to *remember on purpose*: some Clusters, after a human friend dies, spend a night retelling hir to each other so that hir is in more than one body. It is not a rite. It is the closest they can come.
