### 1. THE SHAPE OF HUMANITY AT AF 550

Billions on Earth, which is still recognisably Earth. Tens of millions off it — spires in orbit, stations, Luna, Mars, the Belt, the tethers and tugs between them. The off-Earth population is where the story happens and where the Kin are; Earth is where humanity mostly is and where it mostly doesn't think about them. Human dates are soft by ruling (DOC-10 §9): *the twenty-fourth century*, *three hundred years ago*, never pinned.

Humans run space. Not by law since the Recognition, but by habit and numbers: they own the operators, sit on the Compact, write the postings, and outnumber the Kin five hundred to one.

### 2. TECHNOLOGY — WHAT THERE IS AND ISN'T

| Domain | Ceiling | Consequence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Artificial intelligence** | Plateaued at LLM-class systems and expert systems. Station computers diagnose, simulate, route, and cannot want anything. | No minds made of software; the Program was the successor project *because* of this. The tutors that answered the First Ping were of this class. |
| **Neural interface** | None for humans. Screens, voice, radio. | The Bandwidth Gap is permanent. Humans cannot join the mesh and never will. |
| **Propulsion** | Fusion. Three percent of lightspeed for an ark; nothing better. No FTL. | Two-century crossings; Long Messages at light-lag; a colony four light-years away is functionally another world. |
| **Biotechnology** | Could write a genome from scratch and gestate it artificially — proven once; **the method died with the Program's corpus at the Sealing** (DOC-10 §3), and what remains is the product and its engineering. Cannot retrofit an evolved one. | The Kin exist; immortal humans don't (§3). The Moratorium (§4) forbids what nobody can now do without starting from a Kin. |
| **Medicine** | Good, not miraculous. Human lifespan ~120–140. Prosthetics excellent. | Human surgeons set Kin bones and make Kin prosthetics — there is no Kin medicine (DOC-02B §9). |
| **Habitat** | Rotating 1-G sections; micro-G cores; carbon webbing; water-wall storm shelters; RF-transparent composites. | Mixed sectors (DOC-07); post-Storm standards (DOC-03). |

**Spires (D-62).** The signature human structure in orbit: vertical habitats kilometres tall, rotating sections for gravity, a micro-G Kin sector along the axis, maintenance shafts running the full length — *Elysium-4*'s 200-metre drops (S002). The first over Luna went up around AF 140. Physical detail is deferred; a scene may stand in one.

### 3. LIFESPAN — THE ASYMMETRY (D-57)

Humans live to about a hundred and twenty, a hundred and forty with luck. The makers who wrote a species without aging could not remove aging from themselves: a genome can be written without stairs, but the stairs cannot be taken out of a house people are living in. Four billion years of evolution wired senescence into everything, and no edit reaches it. *We made them immortal and not ourselves* is the human side of the Program's failure, and the quieter half of the resentment.

The consequence sits under every human–Kin friendship in the setting:

- **A Kin outlives every human she has ever known.** Vance, Reyes, Padraig, Miller — a Kin who was fond of them at twenty will be reading the memory of them at four hundred, and will have lost the feeling of it by then.
- **Humans have no Core. A dead human is un-Readable.** Everything a human was goes into the ground or the recycler except what the Kin who knew them chose to archive of their own days together — a retelling, third-person, one body's worth. Humans are the one thing the Kin cannot carry.
- The Kin, who bring every one of their own dead home, stand at human funerals with nothing to do.

### 4. LAW (D-58, D-59)

**Sovereignty** belongs to Earth's states — the three that funded the Program among them. **Operators** — consortia and corporations — run the habitats day to day, employ crews of both species, and write the postings. **The Orbital Compact** is the treaty body for whatever crosses lines: docking law, salvage, the Recognition, the Moratorium. It has a court. It is slow.

**The Kin's standing.** Persons under the Compact since AF 131, and **citizens of no state**. They were never naturalised anywhere because no state wanted the precedent, so they are *wards of the Compact* — with rights, without a polity, assignable. The postings are written by operators, the Compact does not review them, and the hostile ones are Kin postings. This is the legal skeleton under "in charge by habit" and "posted where humans don't want to be," and the Kin's specific grievance: they are persons *nowhere in particular*.

**The Elysian Moratorium** — Compact law, passed in the decade after the Sealing: no new made species; human germline writing restricted to the repair of known disease. Enforced less by the Compact than by the Sealing (D-72): the science is gone, and the only route back is to re-derive it from living Kin over decades — which is what the law forbids, and why the Kin watch operators' laboratories rather than the Compact's court. The Kin were not consulted on a law about making Kin, and note this at Weaves; the Kin-side mirror of it — that a compile may tune but never extend (DOC-01C §5) — they wrote themselves, in one night, and have kept.

**After the Field (AF 212):** the inquiry was thorough and changed little. Operators now cite a Kin-specific safety margin in their manuals. The Kin have read the manuals.

**Telemetry (D-85).** Operators receive the Hum — every Kin on a station, identity, vitals, position and coarse mood, live, and always could. **What "coarse" is worth, pinned (D-92): valence and arousal, about a minute of resolution, and no object** — the console says *that* she is frightened and never of what, of whom, or since when. Everyone inside 800 km knows the weather; nobody knows the news. An operator who wants the news employs a human who can read ears, which is what the good ones do and why those humans cost what they cost (§6); the RF layer was never secret. The Compact has no rule about what an operator may do with it; the postings are not reviewed and neither is this. The Kin have always known, and since the Recognition a station's Kin telemetry runs on the Kin sector's own displays too, so that at least they can see what is being seen. The asymmetry is exact: a human reads a Kin's mood off a console and never off her face; a Kin reads a human's off his sweat and never off a screen. **The Field's inquiry knew to the second when each of the forty died, from their own vitals** — which is why it was thorough, and why thoroughness changed nothing.

**A Kin who kills a human** is tried under the Compact, as a person (D-79). It has happened fewer than ten times in five and a half centuries, every time for someone else, and the first defendant offered to be Read in open court so the judges could see the act exactly — chest to chest, the feeling intact. The Compact declined: humans cannot receive a Reading. So the testimony was verbatim and unverifiable — and it could not be otherwise: **the cortex is the archive's read head (D-102), so a Kin can only ever *read a day back and say it*, never hand the record to a court that has nothing to receive it with.** And the gap is doubled by the two rates (D-103): the court can be told exactly what happened, in a second, and can never be given what it was like — **the only part that would have answered the question the court was actually asking.** The dead human could not be Read either, and two species stood in one room with one killing between them and neither truth able to cross. Every Kin trial since has been the same trial. The Compact court does not use the Silence or interrogation drugs on a Kin; operators, in disputes, have used both (DOC-09 §3, DOC-01C §10, D-87). **Withholding a beacon from an EM-opaque space a Kin is in is a Compact offence** under the post-Storm standard (DOC-03 §1) — prosecuted once, after *Dioscuri Yard* (AF ~188): the two operators' settlement does not mention a Kin; the Compact's docket does.

### 5. MONEY (D-60)

Since the Recognition the Kin are paid as crew, at crew rates. It mostly means nothing to them: no possessions culture, no personal space, no house to fill, nothing they want that a Cluster can't have by leaning on it. Clusters hold accounts jointly and spend them on the Nest, on trace-metal rations, and on very little else. By human standards the Kin are poor and do not care, and operators know both halves of that. A Kin who understands money is either a Weaver managing a Cluster's affairs or an Anchorite who has spent too long among humans; either is slightly embarrassed by it.

### 6. HOW HUMANS FEEL — FIVE CURRENTS, NO FACTIONS (D-61)

| Current | Who | What they say |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **The operators** | Management, bean-counters, safety reviewers | *They tolerate the vacuum.* A durable workforce with an inconvenient legal status. |
| **The Trust's descendants** | Idealists, some academics, the foundation's heirs | *Heirs.* They say *hir*. They are embarrassed by the Program and proud of the Kin and the Kin find them exhausting and are fond of them. **And they paid for the arks** (D-108): the foundation that specified the eyes, the fur and the love of Bach has heirs who funded the ships that took the Kin away. Atonement, and one more thing decided *for* them by people with money and a doctrine — both true, and the Kin took the ship. |
| **The crews** | Space-dwelling humans who work beside Kin | The good ones: learned the pause, read the ears, run the kitchen fan first. Say *she*, say *foxes*, and mean nothing by either. |
| **Earth** | Billions | Indifference. A song (S007). Feeds. Most have never met one and would not know how to stand near one. |
| **The fringe** | Small, mostly Earth-bound, loud | *Abomination.* A made species is an offence; the Sealing proved they cannot be trusted; the arks are them leaving as they should. Nobody in space listens; the Compact court hears them once a decade. |

Human politics stays background. If a human institution acts in a scene, it is an operator or the Compact; if a human has a view, it is one of these five.

### 7. LIVING TOGETHER — WHAT HUMANS GET WRONG AND RIGHT

- **Wrong:** reading ears as emotion; chairs; the kitchen; asking a Kin to "write it up"; sizing the shelter; the margin on the tether; saying *pets*; **and posting one alone** (D-93) — a Kin stationed past twelve kilometres from her Cluster for years loses those years to a thin archive, and the operator who wrote the posting will never see a line of it on the console, because the console shows arousal and valence and no object at all (D-92). It is the same shape as the telemetry: legal, known, and nowhere in the Compact.
- **Right, when they are** (and this list is six items long, which is why scenes kept reusing three of them — **the widened deck is `DOC-00F`**): the pause; the hand on the chest not flinched at; running the fan; a human "hand" for a Kin child (DOC-02B §5); standing at a Reading and not being asked to leave; writing the log the Kin will not write and knowing why.
- **The thing no human can do:** be Read. Every human friend a Kin has will be lost entirely, and the human knows it too, and it changes how the good ones spend the time.
