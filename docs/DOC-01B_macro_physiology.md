
### 2. CERVICAL CONDUIT & NECK INTERFACE

Relocating the primary brain to the chest requires routing high-density neural, vascular, and digestive channels through a $25\text{ cm}–30\text{ cm}$ neck channel (**The Cervical Conduit**).

```
                       CERVICAL CONDUIT CROSS-SECTION
                       
                         [ Top: Dorsal Spine ]
                        /                     \
       [ Neural Superbus ]                  [ Coolant Jugulars ]
     (Heavy Myelinated Tracts)             (Brain-to-Ear Return)
               │                                      │
               ├─── [ Esophagus (Muscle-Shielded) ] ──┤
               │                                      │
       [ Coolant Carotids ]                 [ Trachea (Cartilage Rings) ]
     (Heart-to-Ear Supply)               (Reinforced Airway)
                        \                     /
                        [ Bottom: Ventral Ring ]
```

* **Neural Superbus:** A dense bundle of heavily myelinated axons ($>120\text{ m/s}$ conduction velocity) connecting cranial sensory pre-processors directly to the thoracic core. Total end-to-end signal latency across the neck is under $2.5\text{ ms}$, preserving instant visual and bio-RADAR reflexes.
* **Peristaltic Protection Sheath:** The esophagus is encircled by a fibrous muscular sheath that expands *away* from the central nerve trunk during swallowing, preventing solid food particles from mechanically compressing the Neural Superbus.
* **Vascular Ring:** Double-walled jugular and carotid arteries pass through dedicated bony foramina in the cervical vertebrae, ensuring uninterrupted cerebral blood flow and coolant circulation during $180^\circ$ head rotations.

### 3. DUAL-PUMP CARDIOVASCULAR ARCHITECTURE

To drive continuous perfusion across two brain centers, power high-flux thermal radiators, and prevent fluid pooling in microgravity, the circulatory system utilizes two distinct cardiac pumps:

```
 [ Primary Heart ] ──(High Pressure)──> [ Thoracic Dual-Brain ] ──(Waste Heat)──┐
        ▲                                                                       │
        │                                                                (Hot Blood)
        │                                                                       │
 [ Aux Venous Pump ] <──(Low Pressure)── [ Body/Viscera ] <── [ Ear Radiators ] ┘
```

1. **Primary Thoracic Heart:** Located ventral to the Neuro-Thoracic Vault. A thick-walled, four-chambered muscular pump operating at high stroke volume. It prioritizes continuous perfusion to the thoracic dual-brain, cranial turret, and *Rete Mirabile* heat-exchange loops.
2. **Auxiliary Venous Heart:** A secondary booster pump located at the junction of the four lower limbs. In zero-G, continuous peristaltic contractions prevent lower-limb fluid pooling and drive venous blood back toward the splanchnic filter and primary heart.
3. **Counter-Current Heat Loops (*Rete Mirabile*):** Arterial blood from the thoracic core passes through counter-current heat exchangers in the neck into the swiveling ear cartilage arrays, dumping thermal waste prior to venous return.
4. **Why two pumps:** haemoglobin at 210 g/L (~63 % haematocrit) roughly doubles blood viscosity against human; the high-pressure primary heart and the auxiliary venous pump exist in part to move it. Myoglobin at 45 g/kg is diving-mammal range (sperm whale 50–70 g/kg) and carries the State-B oxygen floor.
5. **Acceleration tolerance — the burn (D-75).** The layout in §1 was specified "to protect critical organs during high-g maneuvers," and this is the number. Human G-LOC is hydrostatics: a brain $\sim 0.30\text{ m}$ above the heart loses $\sim 23\text{ mmHg}$ per g and consciousness near $5\text{ g}$ sitting up. The Kin's brain sits *beside* the heart in the rigid, CSF-filled Endo-Thoracic Box — $\sim 0.08\text{ m}$ offset across the body, $\sim 0.12\text{ m}$ along it — and the box behaves as a skull (neutral buoyancy under sustained load, venous over-pressure contained). Tolerance scales as one over the column: across the body, brain-limited tolerance exceeds anything the structure survives; nose-forward $\sim 12\text{ g}$ unprotected and $\sim 15\text{ g}$ with the *fascia subcutanea compressa* engaged (15 kPa, $\sim 112\text{ mmHg}$ — a G-suit they were born in, now extended to the tail, DOC-01E §2) and the auxiliary venous heart running; tail-forward the box takes the over-pressure. **The head is the only perfusion-weak tissue** — eyes and ear dishes — and it is tucked level with the body so the eyes sit at heart level and stay lit; RF and RADAR sensing need no retinal-grade perfusion. The lungs are sealed and the *vesica oxygenii* breathes for them, so chest compression is not the ventilation limit it is for a human at $+G_x$. Whole-animal envelope, flat on a web with the head tucked: $\sim 15$–$20\text{ g}$ for tens of seconds, $\sim 8$–$10\text{ g}$ sustained for minutes — the fascia and the bladder each set a *time*, not a g (estimates pending a model). A human in the same ship: $6$–$9\text{ g}$ in a couch, briefly, and blind above $\sim 4\text{ g}$ sitting up. A long low body pressed flat to a surface is the reclined-couch geometry with every organ within $0.12\text{ m}$ of the heart: the Kin body is a G-couch with fur on it. Posture and doctrine: DOC-01A §6, DOC-09 §2.

### 4. GASTROINTESTINAL THROUGHPUT & TRACE-METAL CHELATION

To process high-density food rations rapidly without gastrointestinal lag or zero-G motility issues, the digestive system utilizes a two-stage chemical engine coupled with active trace-metal extraction:

1. **Pharyngeal Grinding Engine:** Secondary chitinous/calcareous pharyngeal plates in the throat crush pre-masticated food into a sub-millimeter slurry before it enters the esophagus, expanding chemical surface area by $400\%$.
2. **Two-Stage Stomach:**
   * *Stage 1 (Proventriculus / Acid Digestor):* Highly acidic ($\text{pH } 1.0–1.5$) environment breaks down dense proteins and synthetic paste gels within 45 minutes.
   * *Stage 2 (Gizzard / Enzymatic Vault):* Employs specialized bile salts and hyper-active lipases to rapidly strip fatty acids and simple carbohydrates.
3. **Metallophilic Trace-Metal Pathways:** Bio-radio array maintenance requires continuous absorption of **Copper ($\text{Cu}$)**, **Zinc ($\text{Zn}$)**, and **Sodium ($\text{Na}$)**.
   * Upper intestinal microvilli express specialized ion-transporter membrane channels that pull free metal ions into the **Metallophilic Hepatic Filter**.
   * Metallothionein carrier proteins transport these ions via vascular channels directly to the cartilage micro-arrays in the ears and the VHF tail antenna base.

### 8. THERMAL MANAGEMENT & ENVIRONMENTAL LIMITS

An active Aethela generates $115–140\text{ W}$ of heat during normal operations and up to $290\text{ W}$ at peak exertion. Thermal dissipation in cabin utilizes a multi-tiered array of specialized physical mechanisms (for vacuum, where the pelt dominates and the ears are a throttle, see DOC-01E.2 §3):

```
                   THERMAL DISSIPATION BUDGET (140W ACTIVE LOAD)

 │  EAR RADIATORS (Convection/Radiation)          ~46 W  (33%)                 │
 ├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
 │  RESPIRATORY mucosal HEAT EXCHANGER           ~35 W  (25%)                 │
 ├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
 │  VENTRAL THERMAL WINDOWS (Conductive/Radiative)~40 W  (28%)                 │

 │  PAW-PAD & TAIL GLABROUS SINKS                 ~19 W  (14%)                 │
 └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

1. **Ear Radiator Pinnae:**
   * **Dimensions:** $21–23\text{ cm}$ height $\times$ $13–15\text{ cm}$ base width ($0.16\text{ m}^2$ combined bilateral area, counting both faces of both pinnae with dish curvature).
   * **Thermal Capacity:** Dumps $\sim 40–46\text{ Watts}$ via high-flux sub-dermal venous plexuses and bare, high-emissivity ($\epsilon \approx 0.97$) inner dish skin. Specifically sized to cool the cranial turret and thoracic core dual-brain.
2. **Ventral Thermal Windows ("Splooting"):** Unfurred, highly vascularized skin along the chest, axillary armpit folds, and groin. In microgravity or on station bulkheads, individuals press their ventral side against cool metal/carbon surfaces to dump up to $80\text{ W/m}^2\text{K}$ via direct conduction.
3. **Internal Nasal/Pharyngeal Mucosa:** Internal evaporative cooling loop cycling air through resonant nasal turbinates, cooling carotid blood without spraying liquid droplets into zero-G cabin air.
4. **Dynamic Pelt Chimneys:** Muscle-driven *arrector pili* tracts open longitudinal furrows in the dorsal fur, allowing moving cabin air to strip heat directly from cutaneous veins.
5. **Terrestrial & Wet-Bulb Environmental Protocol:**
   * **Parity with Humans:** Like humans, Aethela baseline biology is not engineered for unassisted survival in hot, humid extremes ($>35^\circ\text{C}$ at $90\%+$ RH). High humidity halts mucosal evaporation and reduces radiative transfer.
   * **Mandatory Metabolic Throttle:** In tropical heat, the endocrine system enforces an automatic compute and physical throttle ($55–70\text{ W}$ baseline) to prevent core hyperthermia.
   * **Modular Auxiliary Gear:** When operating in unconditioned planetary hot zones alongside humans, Aethela wear low-profile chest harnesses equipped with active Peltier/liquid-cooling cold plates pressed against their ventral thermal windows.
