### 0. HOW TO USE THIS

§1–§10 are the reference: what the body actually is, with the canon citation for every figure. §11–§13 are the states a body can be in. §14–§16 are the image-generation kit — ready prompt blocks, the failure modes to guard against, and the scale sheet.

Nothing in the visual design is arbitrary. Every feature answers to something: the tail length is a radio wavelength, the ear size is a radio wavelength *and* a radiator area, the chest *volume* is a floor (its shape is styling — D-73), the tail mass is a balance requirement. When a rendering looks wrong, it is usually because one of those has been quietly traded away. **§15 is the failure list, ordered by what has actually gone wrong in practice.**

### 1. THE SILHOUETTE IN ONE PARAGRAPH

A sixty-kilo hexapod, dense and low: **a fox's outline on a corgi's legs, drawn out to a marten's length.** The body is one tube — a long deep chest running back into a slimmer flexible loin, the fur closing most of the difference — carried on four short braced legs, the front pair heavier than the back. There is no upper torso. The neck simply thickens as it meets the back into a short muscular *yoke*, and the two arms hang from that yoke almost on top of the front legs; the neck goes on above it, long and mobile, and the head is small and jaw-heavy with ears longer than the skull. Behind everything, a tail as thick as a forearm and nearly as long as the body. Roughly half the animal, end to end, is tail. It is the mass of a leopard on the frame of an otter, and it was made cute on purpose.

**The three reads that must survive any stylisation:** six limbs — arms at the shoulders of a fox body, no torso block · one tube, the chest a third heavier around than the loin · a tail that is obviously a limb.

### 3. BODY PLAN & GIRDLES

Three girdles, not two, and two of them stacked (`DOC-01A` §2, §2B):

```
   [ head ]
       \   neck 0.24–0.28 m, 180° yaw
        \
      ARMS ↓  pectoral girdle (scapulae, dorsal, floating)
   [YOKE][──── THORAX 0.45 m ────][──── LOIN 0.65 m ────][──── TAIL 0.88 m ────]
   ~0.18 m  double rib cage;         gastralia;              26 vertebrae
            brain + Core + heart +   8 lumbar vertebrae
            lungs + vesica
               │                            │
          FORE PAWS                    HIND PAWS
     fore-pelvic girdle, ventral,   hind-pelvic girdle
     ~10 cm aft of the arm joint
```

The thorax lies along the body like any quadruped's. Its front end is the **yoke** — the neck thickening into the back — and the arms hang from the yoke with their shoulder joints ~10 cm ahead of and ~15 cm above the fore-leg joints. Two girdles at nearly one station: scapulae above, fore-pelvis below, the double rib cage between them, the root muscle of four limbs over the lot — and the brain and Core inside, *between the shoulder blades*. Everything around that box regrows; nothing inside it does (`DOC-01A` §2B). That is the reason the front end is thick, and the reason it is not a torso.

**It is not a centaur.** There is no vertical body segment. The front of the animal rears as a *posture* — sit-tall, the console ledge, sitting up to look at something — by pitching the whole thorax vertical over the hips with the lumbar spine, like an otter sitting up. On all six it is a fox with arms.

The chest is the densest structure in the body and should read *deep and long*, not big: about a third heavier around than the loin, most of that hidden under the pelt.

### 4. HEAD & FACE

**No brain in it — but draw it as though there were.** The brain and the Bio-Core live in the chest (`DOC-01` §2); the cranial turret holds sensory nodes, RF pre-amps, jaw machinery, resonance chambers and gel shock matrices (`DOC-01B` §1), which fill the volume a brain would. So the cranium is an ordinary rounded one — a designer choice (v1.2) to keep the face readable to humans. Do not flatten the skull to "show" the missing brain.

- **Jaw-heavy.** High-torque jaws and pharyngeal grinding plates (`DOC-01B` §5) mean broad, deep masseter and temporalis — wide cheeks, a deep angle to the jaw. The dome is soft; the jaw is what gives the head its weight.
- **Short vulpine muzzle** — fox-to-marten, not borzoi.
- **Acoustic resonance sinus** in the turret — the reason their speech is warm and carries; it wants a slightly deep, hollow-sounding skull between muzzle and ears.
- **Brow whiskers** (`DOC-01F` §6): a second set of vibrissae above the eyes, as well as the muzzle set. Long, sparse.

Overall: a rounded, wide-cheeked head, with the eyes set forward and high and the ears dominating everything behind them.

### 5. EARS — THE SIGNATURE

21–23 cm tall, 13–15 cm at the base, 0.16 m² combined (`DOC-01B` §8, `DOC-01D` §1). **Longer than the entire skull.** Fennec-class in skull-relative terms; scaled up by radio, not by cuteness — 20 cm is the wavelength floor at 1.5 GHz, below which the phased array has no directivity.

- **Inner dish is bare skin**, not fur — and it is not ordinary skin. Copper/zinc metallothionein complexes make it conductive (σ ≈ 12.5 S/m), so it carries a faint warm metallic cast and a high sheen. Dense venous plexus beneath it: visibly vascular, pinker and hotter when perfused, pale and flat when shut down.
- **A sub-millimetre periodic structure** underlies the dish — dipole nodes at ~1.5 cm spacing (`DOC-01D` §1A). At close range this reads as the faintest regular stipple or quilting under the skin. A detail to imply, not to draw as a circuit board.
- **Outer surface** usually darker than the coat.
- **Cartilage is a low-loss elastomer** — the ears are *flexible*. They furl, cup, fold flat and swivel 180° independently.
- **Rimmed and cupped**, parabolic rather than flat — that curvature is where the 0.16 m² comes from.

They are never at rest. Positions carry meaning, though half the time the meaning is thermal (`DOC-00B` §1).

### 6. EYES

35 mm — half again a human eye, in a smaller head (`DOC-01F` §2). Large and forward-set; the eye-to-skull ratio sits between a human's and a cat's. Pupil opens to 14 mm.

- **No tapetum.** They do not eyeshine. A glowing-eyed Kin is wrong.
- **Human-compatible colour vision** — the eyes are for faces, paintings, screens and sunlight, not navigation.
- **Two nictitating membranes** (`DOC-01E` §3): an inner clear fluorocarbon moisture seal, and an **outer gold/melanin electrochromic filter** that darkens automatically in unattenuated sunlight. Closed, the eye becomes a flat gold mirror, opaque and featureless. *Humans watching a video feed call it silver; it is gold.* This is the single most striking transformation available to the face — use it for sun and vacuum.

Iris colour is not fixed by canon. Treat it as individual.

### 7. LIMBS — AND THE HAND-PAW

**The single most-missed feature in the design.** Canon gave no digit count until D-69, and "paw" is a word that makes both artists and image models reach for a dog's foot. It is not one.

**Arms (×2)** — human-pattern: long slim digits, flat nails, no claws. ~340 N grip and explicitly **not rated as anchors** (`DOC-01A` §3) — but full limbs in micro-G, pushing, catching and rail-walking beside the forepaws (D-74). **~0.50 m shoulder to fingertip**, so hands and forepaws reach the same rail and the fingertips brush the floor on all six. Primate-grade shoulder: full circumduction, **elbows pointing back, palms to the flanks** when hanging. On the page, under gravity: **loose at the walk** — elbows bent, hands about the fore-leg wrist — and **folded to the chest at any speed**. These are tool hands and they should look like tool hands.

**Fore limbs (×2)** — short, thick, columnar, braced: corgi-heavy, visibly heavier-boned than a fox's, and the place a fox prior will fight you hardest. They carry ~56 % of standing load: the *load* pair, set close under the chest, with the arms hanging just ahead of and above them. They hang from a hip-type socket — human-hip range, enough to take a rail beside the body, not enough to splay — and bend the arm way: **elbow back, wrist forward**, the whole palm flat on the floor at rest. The thickness is grip muscle: 280 N lives in the forearm. In micro-G this pair is *the vice* — it holds the thing the hands are working on.

**Hind limbs (×2)** — longer and more muscled (14 % of body mass vs 10 %), driven by eight lumbar vertebrae with feline flexion; knee forward, hock back, the leg way. The *engine* pair; the 8–10 m/s bound comes from here. In micro-G they are the *anchors* — the pair a Kin locks and forgets.

**All six limbs bend the human way** — two arms, two arms-that-stand, two legs — which is why a fox's legs are the right reference. **Every paw and the tail lock passively** when loaded (D-74): a Kin hanging asleep from a rail is holding on with no effort at all, and should be drawn relaxed, not clenched.

#### The paw itself (`DOC-01A` §3B, D-69)

**Five digits.** Not four, not toes:

| Digit | Form | Set at | Tip |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **D1** — thumb | Short, very thick, heavily muscled at the base | **~100° divergence**, low and far back on the inner side | **Flat nail — no claw** |
| **D2–D4** | The main row; flex together as one bundle | in line | sheathed claw |
| **D5** | Semi-divergent, closes from the outer side | ~30° | sheathed claw |

**It closes like a three-jaw chuck, not like a pinch.** D1 comes in from one side, D5 from the other, D2–D4 wrap over the top. That three-point wrap on a 3–5 cm rail is where 280 N sustained comes from without a claw ever leaving its sheath. Maximum span ~12 cm.

**Proportions: short and thick throughout.** This is a power hand and nothing about it is delicate. ~10 cm long on the fore pair, ~11 cm hind, ~9 cm across at full spread. Compare a raccoon's forepaw or an opossum's hind foot, not a dog's.

**Pads:** **bare skin and glossy** — the conductive lipid film gives a waxy sheen.

**Surfaces:** furred on the back of the paw, matching the coat; the entire gripping face bare. Show the fur/skin boundary along the edge of the paw.

**Claws barely show.** Semi-retractable and sheathed on habitat surfaces — blunt tips at most, often nothing. Claws out indoors reads as an emergency. And **D1 has no claw at all**, which is the tell that separates this from any real animal's foot.

**The wrist is doing invisible work.** Near-full supination, because in a three-dimensional rail grid the paw has to come round a rail from any approach angle.

**The contrast is the point.** Hands: long, slim, shallow, flat-nailed, bare-palmed. Paws: short, thick, deep-padded, clawed, furred-backed, thumbed. A Kin threads a connector with the hands while four paws and a tail hold the body still — and in any full-body image, both structures should be legible as different things.

### 8. THE TAIL

The feature most often under-drawn, and the one physics most insists on.

**0.88 m long and ~7 kg — 12 % of body mass**, heavier than both arms together and comparable to one hind limb (`DOC-01A` §3A). Base ~17 cm through bare, ~21 cm furred, tapering over its length to perhaps 3–4 cm at the tip. Fully prehensile along most of its length.

**Reference animals: monitor lizard, giant otter, a theropod's tail.** Not a fox's brush, not a cat's rope. Thick, muscular, visibly worked.

Three things force that mass, and all three are visible:
1. **Counterweight.** Even with the thorax laid flat the chassis is ~67/33 front-heavy; the tail brings it to ~56/44, and it is what lets the front end rear without the hind paws unloading. Held level at rest, raised and extended in a bound.
2. **Reaction mass.** The half-second vector-flip is a tail windmill — three or four continuous sweeps — combined with counter-rotation of thorax against barrel. It needs inertia.
3. **Fifth anchor**, 450 N sustained. It wraps a rail and takes more load than a paw does.

It also houses the VHF whip at λ/4, which is *why* it is 0.88 m and not some other length. Glabrous (hairless) patches on the underside serve as heat sinks.

**On the page it is never still, because it is never idle.**

### 9. COAT, SKIN & BARE SURFACES

**The pelt** (`DOC-01`, `DOC-01B` §6, `DOC-01E` §2): dense, plush, roughly 2 cm deep. Hair shafts carry eumelanin chelated with copper, iron and zinc — so the coat is *mildly conductive* and carries a faint metallic sheen, like graphite or oiled steel under a raking light.. A wax-ester sebum layer (which also seals the skin in vacuum) adds a slight, clean gloss.

Because the pelt is 2 cm deep, **the animal you see is 40–65 % bulkier in cross-section than the animal underneath** — and unevenly so. Add 4 cm to every width and depth in §2. The chest gains ~40 % in cross-section, the loin ~60 %, so **the pelt closes most of the chest-to-loin step**: a girth ratio of 1.4 in the skeleton is ~1.35 in the silhouette — a fox's taper, read through fur. Draw the taper at fur depth, not at flesh depth, or it reads as emaciated. A 60 kg Kin looks like a heavier animal than it is.

**Pale underfur** lies beneath the guard coat and is normally hidden. The albedo reflex lifts it in direct sunlight (`DOC-01B` §8, D-8) — the coat visibly *blooms* paler and rougher, dropping absorptivity from 0.70 to ~0.35.

**Dorsal pelt chimneys:** arrector pili tracts open longitudinal furrows along the back when hot, parting the fur into visible channels down the spine.

**Bare, un-furred skin** — worth knowing exactly where, because it is where colour and texture change:
- inner ear dishes (conductive, high-sheen, vascular)
- **ventral thermal windows** — chest midline, axillary folds, groin: unfurred, highly vascularised, the surfaces they press to cold bulkheads when splooting
- paw pads (glossy, conductive film)
- glabrous patches on the tail underside
- the face around muzzle and eyes, lightly

**Colour is deliberately open.** Canon fixes only the physics — dark, metal-laden, desaturated, sheened — and names exactly one individual's coat: Lyra-4's "dense chestnut" (`DOC-05`). Charcoal, rust, sable, chestnut, dark bronze and deep grey all fit; bright saturated hues and pure white do not. Pale cream or ivory underfur at throat and belly. Markings are unspecified — that is where an individual lives.

### 10. WHAT THEY WEAR

**Nothing, normally.** No clothing, no gear. This is not nudity to them and should not be drawn coyly.

Two exceptions (`DOC-01A` §7, `DOC-01B` §8):
- a **low-profile chest harness** with active Peltier/liquid cold plates, pressed against the ventral thermal windows, worn only in unconditioned planetary hot zones
- a **human-made prosthetic**, worn while a lost limb slowly regrows behind it — years, not months

Tools are carried in the hands: a diagnostic scanner, a probe. That is the whole inventory.

### 11. POSTURES

| Posture | What it looks like |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Sit-tall** | The signature. Hind paws tucked, forepaws folded, the whole thorax pitched vertical over the hips like an otter sitting up, arms free at the yoke, tail hanging with a curled tip. On a console ledge, a counter, the back of a sofa. Never in a chair. |
| **Walk** (1 G) | A cat's lateral-sequence walk on four paws; arms hanging loose, elbows bent, hands about the fore-leg wrist. The everyday 1-G read: a fox with arms. |
| **Trot / bound** (1 G) | Arms folded to the sternum, kangaroo-fashion — hard-tucked in the bound. Hands never near the ground at speed. |
| **Working posture** | On a rail: hind paws and tail locked (passively — relaxed, not clenched), hands and forepaws free; forepaws holding the workpiece, hands doing the fine work, head come round on the neck to watch. Rated 1.7 g. The default work stance. |
| **Storm posture** | All five anchors locked, body flattened to the rail. Rated 2.7 g. Also the sleep-web posture — tendon-locked, so the sleeper is limp. |
| **Burn posture** | Storm posture on an aft-facing *web*: body pressed flat by the acceleration, head tucked between the arms, tail straight along the body, fur compressed, eyes open. Conscious and talking at accelerations that have the humans in couches. |
| **Splooting** | Ventral windows pressed flat to a cold bulkhead, limbs splayed. Thermal, not lazy. |
| **Rail-walk / brachiate** | Four paws alternating along carbon webbing; or forepaws only, hind tucked, long swings. Hauling: hands and forepaws hand-over-hand on the same rail, six limbs working. Forepaws take the rail by default; hands stay free. |
| **Push-glide** | Free flight across a bay, body streamlined, tail trailing, caught with tail or hind paws. |
| **Vector-flip** | Mid-air, tail windmilling, body counter-rotating against it. Half a second. |
| **Bound** | 1 G, feline, lumbar flexion, tail extended and raised aft. 8–10 m/s. |
| **Crawl-home** | Vacuum, subdued, rails only, nothing in the hands. |

### 12. STATE CHANGES

These are the four transformations available to the design. They are dramatic and they are all canon.

**Vacuum / State A** (`DOC-01E` §1): both nictitating membranes closed — eyes flat gold, featureless. Ears folded hard flat to the skull to hold heat, flicking open every ninety seconds to dump it. Fascial compression tightens the body's outline; the fur compresses. Anchors locked. The whole animal reads *sealed*.

**Direct sunlight:** gold eye filter engaged. Coat blooms pale as the albedo reflex lifts the underfur. Ears usually turned edge-on to the sun — the orientation reflex protects the bare dishes.

**Hot:** ears fanned wide and held, dishes flushed and vascular. Dorsal pelt chimneys open along the spine. Splooting against cold surfaces. Nothing about it is emotional, and humans misread it constantly.

**Cold / torpor / State B:** ears folded flat, coat fully lofted, body curled tight. Peripheral perfusion cut by 95 % — the extremities go pale and cool. Unconscious, and cooling slowly toward 27 °C.

**Under burn** (D-75): the vacuum outline without the gold eyes — fascia tight, fur compressed, body flattened to a web, head tucked level with the body between the arms, ears flat. Eyes *open* and ordinary. The one state where the Kin is the alert one in the room.

### 13. HUMAN SCALE

Against a 1.72 m human standing:

| Top of head, on all six | a hanging hand |

| Top of head, reared | bottom of the ribs |

A 0.90 m counter is above the head of a Kin on all six and about chin height once hir rears — easily reached, which is why counters get used and chairs never do; for close work hir sits up onto it. Sitting tall on a console ledge, hir head is at a seated human's eye level. Carrying one is a two-person job.

### 15. WHAT GENERATORS GET WRONG

Ordered by observed frequency from actual runs, not by guess. Items 1 and 2 are confirmed failures from the first generation attempt; item 0 is the confirmed failure of the *spec* that followed it.

**0. The chest becomes a torso block — a meerkat, or a fat centaur.** *Observed, and it was the archive's fault.* Prompts built to the v1.2 geometry ("deep barrel chest, 42 cm tall, upright torso, inverted L") produced exactly that: a big vertical chest on top of the front legs, an animal that looked overweight and not tube-shaped. D-73 fixed the spec. **Fix:** never say *torso*, *barrel chest* or *upright*. Say "one tube-shaped body, a long deep chest running back into a slimmer loin", put the arms "at the shoulders, almost above the front legs", and name the reference — *arctic fox × marten on corgi legs*. If a model still stacks a torso, add `centaur, upright torso, meerkat, barrel chest` to the negative prompt.

**1. The tail comes out a fox brush.** *Observed.* The default for anything fox-adjacent is a big fluffy plume, and the word "thick" does not override it. **Fix:** never call it thick — name the animal. "Heavy and muscular like a monitor lizard's or a giant otter's tail, not a fox's brush." Give it a diameter in forearms. If it still blooms, add `fox brush tail, fluffy tail, plume tail` to the negative prompt.

**2. The paws come out dog paws, with no thumbs.** *Observed.* This is the worst one, because **the word "paw" is itself the bug** — models map it straight to a carnivoran foot and then discard every qualifier that follows. Saying "paw with opposable digits" reliably yields a paw. **Fix: never lead with "paw."** Lead with *hand* and qualify inward: "the four lower limbs end in HANDS, not animal paws — short thick five-fingered gripping hands with a large opposable thumb." Name a real referent the model knows — **raccoon forepaw, opossum hind foot**. Then run §14 C2 separately, because a detail this fine rarely survives a full-body composition at all; generate the hands as their own study and use them as reference.

**3. Limb count.** Four legs and no arms, or arms growing where the forelegs belong. State the count twice. A rough silhouette scribble as img2img input beats any wording.

**4. The taper goes wrong in one of two directions.** Either the body is a uniform sausage, or (see item 0) the chest balloons. The target is a fox's: chest a third heavier around than the loin, read through fur. Say "a long deep chest running back into a slimmer loin" — *long* is the word that keeps it from ballooning — and remember §9: draw the taper at *fur* depth, not flesh depth.

**The general lesson from 1 and 2:** where this design contradicts a strong prior, adjectives lose and **nouns win**. Do not modify the model's default noun — replace it, and name a real animal that already has the feature.

Secondary: "taur" alone pulls horse-centaur, so qualify before using it; ask for "unlabeled" or model sheets come back covered in gibberish lettering; ears come out too small unless compared to the skull.

**Negative prompt, where supported:**
`hooves, horse, horse body, centaur, upright torso, meerkat, barrel chest, human face, four limbs only, two limbs, clothing, armor, collar, saddle, thin whip tail, fox brush tail, fluffy plume tail, dog paws, cat paws, hooved feet, fingerless paws, white fur, eyeshine, glowing eyes, text, watermark, photorealistic`

### 16. WHAT IS DELIBERATELY OPEN

Canon fixes the engineering and leaves the individual. These are yours:

- **Coat colour and markings** — within the dark, desaturated, metal-sheened envelope of §9. Only Lyra-4's chestnut is on record.
- **Iris colour.**
- **Face proportions** — muzzle length, cheek width, brow, how much dome.
- **Ear shape within 21–23 × 13–15 cm** — rounded, pointed, notched.
- **Build within the band** — 50–70 kg, and the void-build / ground-build phenotypes (`DOC-01A` §5) differ visibly: void-built runs slighter and longer-limbed in effect, ground-built stockier and deeper-chested — but the deeper chest is *longer*, never taller; the taper stays a fox's.
- **Scars.** They regenerate, so old scars mean *recent*, or mean the Core had other priorities.
- **Age does not show.** Non-senescence means a Written four centuries old looks like anyone else. A room goes quiet when one speaks, and that is the only tell.
